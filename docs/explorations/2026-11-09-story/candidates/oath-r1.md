# In Front of Everybody

You earn each well by swearing a practical favour to the people who live on it, then spend the game coming back with stronger magic to pay, until the things you said can't all be done.

## The question

**When you've walked the whole circuit, which towns will still open the gate for you?**

The ending answers it literally. The last stretch of the game is a walk past every town you swore to, with each gate as your record left it.

## The opening

The school is on a tidal islet in the bay under the southern badlands, on an Arcane vein, one ferry from everywhere. On your last morning the cook has rats in the cellar, which teaches the queue, casts, friendly fire and the weapon from your etched focus. At the door, custom: every leaving journeyman practises on the cook. You swear, aloud, in front of the whole dining hall, to bring back a jar of pickled eggs from the market across the bay. A hazel stick is notched and split, and you each keep half. Twenty minutes later you hand over the eggs and both halves go in the stove. That is the entire system, taught. Any opening that ends with one stick cut and burned can replace this.

## The road

**How the word works.** Wells sit on town commons and belong to the people around them. They have always let journeymen attune, because a wizard who owes you is the only kind a village can afford. That is what the circuit is for, and the school tells you so on day one. To attune, you say a specific service aloud and a tally stick is split. Your half hangs on your belt, visible on the sprite. Nothing enforces it, and an attunement can never be taken back, which is why the town listens so closely.

- **Kept:** both halves burn at that town's hearth, and its side paths, shops and keeping shrines open.
- **Broken:** the town nails its half to the gate, and those things close. Each collision opens an equal path on the other side, so the total power on offer never changes. Only the roads and the faces do.
- **Timing:** a promise falls due when a story step is reached and stays due until settled, so dawdling can't break your word. The host decides, or a quick vote.
- **Small oaths:** edge villages offer two or three each, as side quests ("get my brother's mule back from the people who are, fairly, keeping it").

Every attunement gives a regen step and deepens the pool you choose. Order is fixed, because debts have to be scheduled, and the ferry keeps every old region one crossing away.

### 1. The Mesa (Earth), north-centre

- **Place:** a fortress built for a war nobody can date, now mostly cisterns with a town on top.
- **Oath:** *"When the cisterns fail, bring us water."* They haven't failed in two hundred years. It is practically a toast, and the crowd cheers.
- **Exam:** clear burrowers from the well shaft while the town's children heckle from the ledges.
- **Gain:** Earth's regen bend, and your first real walls.
- **Encounters:** switchback gauntlets, territorial burrowers, a defence of the cargo lift.
- **The beat:** the water-master drops a plumb line in the great cistern and reads the depth aloud, as every morning. You half listen.
- **On return:** the number is painted on the lift wall and is lower each visit. Nobody mentions it, until they do. Deeper Earth bridges the outlying mesas.

### 2. The Great Tree (Life), south-west

- **Place:** a parish around a very large tree that drops limbs the size of barns. The parish's main skill is knowing where not to stand.
- **Oath:** *"Never bring fire past the ford, and leave the ford-man be."* This one is real in your hands. In ford country a Fire spell counts only if something is still burning about ten seconds later and someone could see it. If you get it out before the goatherd turns round, you never did it.
- **Exam:** escort the fellers up the trunk road through pests that plainly want burning.
- **Gain:** healing that matters, and a reason to learn every non-Fire answer you have.
- **Encounters:** escorts, swarms, regenerating wildlife.
- **The beat:** the ford-man goes through your pockets for flint, apologising, a wet rag tied round one forearm.
- **On return:** if you've kept the ford, the upper trunk road opens by Cold or Earth. Limbs have fallen. Someone has built a house in one.

### 3. The Arch (Lightning), south-west badlands

- **Place:** the tallest thing in dry country gets struck most nights, so herders run lamps off rods bolted to it.
- **Oath:** *"Stand for our side when the boundary is heard."* The boundary is the summer pasture line against the tree parish you swore to last week. A herder asks if you've noticed. You are already holding the stick.
- **Exam:** cross the open ground to the well during a storm, with rods to charge and swarms that chain.
- **Gain:** the first big regen step, and a chain spell to use on your friends by accident.
- **Encounters:** steppe ambushes, your first enemy casters (claim-jumpers with the same runes as you, used badly).
- **The beat:** the Hearing is explained. Each side's sworn wizard stands on the line under the arch until one steps off.
- **On return:** lamps run further down the road each visit, and a coil-gate path opens to charged Lightning.

### 4. The Fen (Water), north-east

- **Place:** a town on stilts, rich in eels and reeds, wet in every sense.
- **Oath:** *"Come back and hold the dyke when the spring melt comes."*
- **Exam:** a flooded gauntlet to the well, soaking everything, which teaches conduction the hard way.
- **Gain:** Rain, freezes set up for later, and boats.
- **Encounters:** amphibious packs, a native boss in the sluice, puzzle rooms of gates fed by your spells.
- **The beat:** the whole town walks you along the dyke, and each family shows you its stretch. When it falls due, you will know whose it is.
- **On return:** see the midpoint. Afterwards, Cold makes winter roads out to the drowned farms.

### 5. The Furnaces (Fire), the volcanic south

- **Place:** lava led through stone channels to numbered furnaces. Number Four burst six years ago and killed nine.
- **Oath:** *"Bring us Harl Dunmow, who walked off his shift that night, to stand in front of us."* It sounds simple, and you've met nobody by that name.
- **Exam:** feed Number Five with your own Fire while lava-things come up the channels.
- **Gain:** full-strength Fire, after a whole game of being told where not to use it.
- **Encounters:** quick and aggressive packs, channel-gate puzzles, a defence of the shift change.
- **The beat:** Number Four's widow keeps nine chairs set at a long table in the street.
- **On return:** Number Four is rebuilt or left cold, according to the late-game turn. Earth stepping stones open the lava fields.

### 6. The Towed Tower (Arcane), the east

Your passage here is the fen's price; see the midpoint.

- **Place:** the deep field comes close to the surface here, as bad weather does in some valleys. The tower floats over the well and drifts a few yards a year. The local response is a tow day, with the whole district and every ox on ropes, hauling it off the orchard.
- **Oath:** *"Be on the rope on tow day."* After the Furnaces it comes as a relief.
- **Exam:** tow day itself. Defend the haulers from strange, out-of-place things that drop from the tower's underside.
- **Gain:** the beam, Attune, and the largest regen step in the game.
- **Encounters:** a defence, warped puzzle rooms, enemy casters who are simply good at this.
- **The beat:** the ropes take the strain and a mile of people grunt at once. It is the happiest scene in the game.
- **On return:** the tower is exactly where the ropes last left it.

### 7. The Ribs (Cold), the northern ice

- **Place:** whatever the skeleton was, it has been quarried for three hundred years and makes a fine windbreak. A town lives under the ribs. The children have a rhyme naming the wrong animal.
- **The headgate:** the river's source runs under the town, through sluice piers made of bone.
- **Oath:** *"Whatever's done with the river, you'll say it here, yourself, out loud."* The ice folk are tired of being decided for by people who send letters.
- **Exam:** a long ice-road gauntlet against large, patient things.
- **Gain:** freeze, shatter, ice sheets.
- **The beat:** you stand on the headgate and can see, by eye, both the Cut and the fen channel running south.
- **On return:** the post-game.

### Optional: the Crystal Island (north-west, after the midpoint)

The Decliners are wizards who finished the circuit and swore never to swear again. They live on a peak and answer the first three things you ask with "no". They teach Shield's wider arts, the will to refuse. Afterwards you may turn down small oaths at no cost. The seven main ones remain the price of the wells.

## The turns

**Midpoint: the Spires, and when Sigil opens.** The melt and the Hearing fall due in the same week, at opposite ends of the map. The skipper says so and looks at your shoes. Calling for coal at the black spires east of the volcano, you meet the cutters, who carve boundary stones and dyke stones that go on working after the cutter has gone home. A puzzle-room trial under one of them opens the full art: turrets, glyphs, Blink, auras. *"You've been shaping what you hold. Try the wall."*

The payoff is a three-part set piece. First you build along the dyke, family by family, knowing you are about to leave. Then comes the Hearing, the first duel against a caster with your full grammar. You stand until one of you steps off, and the stick is kept whether you win or lose, since nobody swore you would win. Then you sail back for the last night and find the dyke exactly as your layout left it. You patch what's left. Stretches that failed cost that family its house, visibly, and cost your record nothing. Both sticks burn. It feels brilliant. The dyke-reeve burns the half and says only, "You left."

The fen's boats are the one way down the warped river to the Arcane well, and the boatmen, in the warmest moment the town has had in years, ask one thing: *"Say the river stays where it is."* Of course you say it. The player with a good memory goes cold around here.

**Late-game turn: the ford-man.** A second kind of collision, where being in two places would not help. After the Arcane well you pass the ford. The wet rag has slipped, and the brand under it is a 4. Your Fire oath says bring him. Your Life oath says leave him be. He will go if you ask, and he won't argue either way.

- **Bring him:** an escort through a parish that tries to stop you without wanting to hurt you. The stick goes on the ford's gate, and the tree roads close. The Furnaces open Number Four's lower galleries. You watch him stand in front of nine chairs, from across the street.
- **Leave him:** the kiln crew and their hired casters come with torches, and you hold the ford without using Fire, because you swore that as well. The stick goes on the Furnaces' gate. The upper trunk opens instead.

## The ending

The cisterns reach zero. The skipper brings the mesa's half of the stick, which is how you learn what time it is. The Cut is a canal the mesa's grandparents half-dug when the rains last failed, and it takes water only if the headgate opens at peak melt. Opened, the mesa lives and the fen drains to mud under its stilts: the eels gone, the town's whole living gone. Shut, the fen is safe, and two thousand people walk down off a rock with what they can carry. The water-master, who has every reason to want otherwise, says once, with numbers, that a half-open gate silts the Cut in a season and ruins both.

You stand on the bone pier and say it, out loud. Both towns are there. Host's call or a vote.

The last battle is the same place either way. You get a prep window for every device you own, then three surges of breaking ice, the armoured thing that has wintered under the ribs, and the other side's sworn casters, among them Tobit, who promised both and apologises throughout. It is also tow day, which you miss in every ending. The district nails your stick up with a note saying *we managed*, and they let you in anyway, past it.

- **Cut opened:** the fen's mud uncovers a drowned older town, a new dungeon. The mesa's terraces go green.
- **Cut shut:** the emptied cistern halls open as a dungeon. The fen stays exactly as it was, which it regards as your duty.

Then the walk home, by road, every gate as you left it, with free play afterwards and any debts you still owe. Last image: you stop at the shut gate of the town you chose against. On the fen, the plank is drawn up. On the mesa, the lift stays at the top. A shutter opens and someone looks at you, for a while, and closes it. A little later, a bowl of soup is left on the last post, or a basket comes down with bread and no note. The gate stays shut. You eat it and walk on, with the unburned half still on your belt.

## The recurring faces

- **Goody Strake, ferry skipper.** Prices the fare by looking at your shoes. Wants a winter berth in the fen, which you may end up draining.
- **Tobit Lusk, fellow journeyman.** Cannot say no, and has forty sticks on the belt. Wants everyone to think well of them.
- **Harl Dunmow, the ford-man.** Checks pockets for flint and apologises. Wants never to smell hot iron again.
- **Oddny Sedge, dyke-reeve.** Counts everything aloud, twice. Wants the fen's children to stay in the fen.
- **Ysolt Ferrand, water-master.** Has read the plumb line every morning for thirty-one years. Wants the last reading not to be hers.
- **Marthe of the nine chairs.** Sets the table daily. Wants one man to stand in front of her and say what happened.
- **Bede, spire-cutter.** Speaks only while the chisel is moving. Wants someone to take the stones on.

## Why this is fun to play

You keep arriving at places you were weak in and solving, in thirty seconds of well-chosen runes, the problem you once promised to solve someday. The oaths reach into your spellcasting: holding a ford against torches with Fire forbidden, or scrambling to douse a friend's mistake before the goatherd turns round. Sigil arrives exactly when you need to be somewhere you can't be, so laying out a dyke and leaving it is preparation with a real stake. And when you come back, the half of the map you dealt with honestly is a different game from the half you didn't.

## The biggest weakness

The plot is about obligations and the combat is about explosions, and most monsters have no stake in any promise. Only the oath set pieces truly join story to hands, so the long stretches between them may play as ordinary region-clearing. Close behind: the seven main oaths are mandatory, so a sharp player will see the fen-and-mesa collision coming hours ahead and resent being made to swear it.
