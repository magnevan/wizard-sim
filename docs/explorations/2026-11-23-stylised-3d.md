# Stylised 3D instead of pixel art (2026-11-22 to 23)

**Status:** being explored, not decided. The user, after the spike's second round: "this is way way better, lets keep exploring this path". The pixel game on main is untouched. The work is on branch `spike-world3d`.

## How we got here

1. **Code-drawn art was rejected** (1–2/10). Five beauty rounds of procedural facades, stone, kerbs and re-tinted textures had a critic rising from 2 to 6 while the user saw 1–2. Two fixes followed: art only from painted sources ((memory: no-code-drawn-art)), and a calibrated critic ([the beauty rubric](../campaign/beauty-rubric.md), anchors in `docs/campaign/anchors/`).
2. **Codex repainting the grey-box** came closer to the concept, but it was locked into the grey-box's odd shapes. The user: "repainting the grey-box directly locks it into bad directions". Composing freely from the brief fixed the structure, but the camera was too shallow and the scale too low.
3. **Magicka's camera and scale** became a hard cap: as steep and close as Magicka (`docs/reference/magicka/`), which is itself 3D-rendered. At that camera, three styles were painted. Pixel art (critic 3) and hand-painted (3) both slipped back into perspective views and redesigned the wizard. A **stylised 3D render scored 6**: it held the camera and scale and kept the wizard's look.
4. **The user's case for 3D:** "it's significantly harder to actually create good maps with pixel art compared to stylized 3d. There's a lot of things we get for free like the ability to have more free angles, depth and angles of models are solved somewhat automatically". The characters are already live 3D models, and three.js is already in the client.

## The spike

A page of its own (`game/packages/client/world3d.html`, `npm run shot:world3d`). It draws:
- the courtyard's plan-made level as 3D terrain, from the level's heights and ground, with its 45° staircases cut on the diagonal so edges run straight on screen;
- Meshy models placed from the plan (`tools/place-plans/spike3d_place.py`);
- the wizard's own model walking;
- a steep, close camera, a warm sun, and ambient occlusion.

- **Round 1** used soft crops of a painted sheet, with Meshy's cheapest settings (smart topology, one colour texture). The user found the buildings "incredibly splotchy". The critic gave it 4.
- **Round 2** used the Meshy agent's pipeline, with its image step done free in Codex (`tools/building-art/README.md`, "3D kit pieces"). Each piece is isolated from the concept as a clean front-on picture, then made with Meshy 7.1 and full PBR maps, remeshed to about 30k triangles. The user: "way way better".

## Still open

- **Ground and edges:** 3D paving textures, cut-stone retaining walls with copings, and balustrades and steps as modelled pieces. The critic ranks this the biggest gap.
- **Dressing:** cypresses, lanterns, benches, urns.
- **The camera:** leads the view up-screen toward the buildings (round 3).
- **Not measured yet:** the frame rate on a laptop. The models are 9–17 MB each, so load size will matter.
- **If we commit:** the characters lose their pixel shader, the spell effects need revisiting, the HUD can stay pixel, and the renderer becomes the game's own rather than a separate page.

## The robust plan (2026-11-23)

Rounds 3 to 5 showed the limits of building the 3D view from the level's cells and fixed-size models. Curves and diagonals came out as staircases, retaining walls didn't fit every shape, and house modules didn't join. The fix is to build from the plan's exact shapes, with kit pieces that fit any length. The user approved both points this needed: **procedural geometry with painted surfaces** (simple shapes in code, the art from painted textures and Meshy models), and **buildings snapped to whole bays**.

- **A. Vector ground** (done, round 6). The plan tool records its shape ops (`Plan.ops`). `tools/place-plans/vector.py` replays them with shapely into exact regions, cuts flights into treads, and lists the vertical faces. Cell edits a spec makes by hand become rounded overrides. The 3D view triangulates the regions. The sim keeps its cells.
- **B. Edges as sweeps plus accents.** Every edge from A gets a type (retaining wall, parapet, kerb, grass edging). Each is built as a swept body along the exact line with tiling strip textures from Codex, then accents spaced evenly along it (buttresses, piers, balusters, lanterns as Meshy models), then corner and end pieces at its vertices, picked by angle.
- **C. A building kit that joins.** Bay modules with flat, identical sides, trimmed and scaled by a Blender script, plus corner modules. Roofs are built from each footprint with slate strips, with Meshy accents. Footprints are whole bays with corners at 90° or 135°. Hero buildings (the great hall) stay bespoke.
- **D. Prove kits once.** A kit test page runs each kit along standard shapes (straight, two curves, two corners, a short run) for review. Then performance: instancing, compressed textures, and a measured frame rate.

**Fix shapes upstream, not after (the user, 2026-11-23).** Smoothing the exported shapes hid glitches and made new wiggles. The plans were written for a cell grid, where overlapping shapes didn't show. In 3D, a plan's shapes have to meet on purpose: roads as clean bands at fixed radii, laid before what cuts into them, and nothing poking into a curve. Pavements stand a kerb above the road, never the other way round. `vector.py` only closes hairlines (0.3 u). Courtyard D rev 18 is the first plan written this way. The plaza's step rings and the flights are next.

The pipeline as it stands is written up for review in `tools/pipeline-3d/index.html` (published as The Plan-to-3D Pipeline).

**The user's answers (2026-11-23):** the adoption bar is at least 30 fps on the user's laptop (more is better); wall spells behave naturally on terrain, climbing stairs and stopping only at cliffs; some drops are open for knockback, so bodies need a falling state.

**First frame-rate measurement (2026-11-23,** `npm run fps:world3d`, the user's laptop, Radeon 780M, 1920 × 1080, the spike without enemies or spells): 85 fps mean with ambient occlusion off (p95 frame 8 ms), 41 fps with it on (the GTAO pass stalls about 300 ms every few frames). The bar is 30 fps with four wizards and spells.
