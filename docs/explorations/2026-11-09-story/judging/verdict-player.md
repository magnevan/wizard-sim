**Ranking: S, Q, R, P.**

## S: Your Go

**Best thing.** It's the only one where getting stronger is the plot. Bet flattens me with a spell I could have cast, it goes in my copybook, and I spend hours trying it on a duck. The hour-one hook is the coconut shy. The hour-twenty hook is the 50-to-1 tally and wanting one clean win. Then winning costs me the person I was chasing, a gut punch made from a scoreboard.

Co-op is the best here by a distance. The bell brawl is the session we'd talk about for a month, and slates give four friends a number to argue over. I'd tell a friend the first time someone does my walk at supper.

**What makes me quit.** Rivals who read as scripted. If Bet's fights are four patterns, or a loss feels rigged rather than instructive, the asterisks turn smug and fifty losses become an insult. Solo, the back half could go slack: the bell-thing is a thin threat and re-sits can become chores.

## Q: The Flitting Year

**Best thing.** A date everyone knows, and a map that really changes. Four thousand people on folding chairs applauding as the volcano goes out is the best single image in the four. Every return is a different land, the strongest case for going back. A line crossing the fight with different regen on each side is new for my hands.

**What makes me quit.** Arrive, pick one of two jobs, ride the front, six times over. By the mesas I'm managing a calendar. I'd also feel small: 212 die whatever I do, and my power moves a line a few hundred yards. I wanted to be the wizard, not logistics.

## R: What He Had on Him

**Best thing.** The strongest hook of the lot. A body, seven objects, and my spells make evidence talk. Frost showing where hands gripped is a brilliant verb, and the count of ten at the rail gave me chills. It's the one whose ending I'd most want to know.

**What makes me quit.** Prop-zapping at hour 20, with combat bolted on beside the real game. The story doesn't need my power: the climax says Cold depth 1 will do, which tells me my growth was beside the point. With four people, one reads the clue and three stand around or set fire to it, funny twice. Once I've spotted the boots, the last land and a half is paperwork, and a solved mystery gets no second run.

## P: Before All the People

**Best thing.** The wedding, where it's a discourtesy to leave a friend unburnt. Also the ford held against torches with Fire forbidden, an oath that reaches into my fingers.

**What makes me quit.** It's a long march of obligations. I'm forced to swear, the debts are scheduled, and foresight doesn't change who loses. Power gets me out of nothing. Hour twenty is guilt and bookkeeping, and my friends won't vote on land-use wording on a Friday.

## The one idea to steal

From **P**, for **S**: the defence that runs without you. You build a span, you sail, and when you return it stands or falls exactly as your layout left it, with named families' houses visibly gone where it failed. S currently releases your devices when you leave and gets its stakes from empty chairs. This gives the back half bite, makes Sigil growth matter to people I've met, and answers S's stated weakness without adding doom.
