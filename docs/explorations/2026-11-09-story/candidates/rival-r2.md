# Your Go

It's the summer two tiny schools sharing one harbour and one ferry send their journeymen round the wells together, and you spend forty glorious hours trying to beat four people who are cleverer than you, funnier than you, and all going to leave.

## The question

**Are they better than you?** You'll ask it on the very first afternoon, when a total stranger flattens you with a spell you could have cast yourself, and from then on it's chalked inside the front cover of your copybook, in her handwriting: *Bet 1, You 0*. That score never resets and it counts every try. The answer, I'm so sorry and so happy to tell you, is yes, by about fifty to one, and the one is the best day of your life and also the reason the summer ends.

**What drags you through forty hours when the world is completely fine:**
- **The slates.** Each cohort chalks its names, its finishing queue, and whatever that town actually cares about (goose health, stairs lost, friends struck), one line per party size. A re-sit is next year's job on the same ground, and the Dunnocks keep re-sitting behind your back. Bet will retake a line the day after you beat it, and Ines goes over your chalk in tidier handwriting, in front of everyone, in blue.
- **The copybook.** Any queue that touches you, anywhere, friend or foe, writes itself in.
- **The table.** Each region ends at one long supper, one line each, loser lays it, and somebody does an impression of the last time you set fire to yourself. There's a chair fewer as the summer goes on.
- **The letter.** Captain Sloe has been carrying one for Bet since the first week, and every port she holds it out, and every port Bet says "not yet" and comes to find you for another spar.

## The opening

Last-Day Fête on the quay at Pennick on the ochre peninsula in the northern bay, over an Arcane vein, with the Dunnock Point school close enough across the strait to throw things at. Every stall is an element with one rune each: freeze the duck pond, soak the mayor without soaking the queue, and Mr Sedge etches your focus so you can conjure your very first blade. Any stall can be skipped. At the coconut shy Bet casts one colossal five-rune spell, obliterates the shy, and stands there grinning for one entire minute while her runes come back, and you decide you're going to get her. There's only one ferry, so off you all go together.

## The road

The route round the sea is fixed, side paths are free on every return. Each attunement steps your regen and deepens the pool you pick, and each re-sit you win deepens one more, so you finish lopsided. Attuning means working a place until your casting picks up its accent, and each exam is a job set by the locals.

The spar rule: as many Dunnocks step up as there are of you, and if they run short, what they've built before you arrived makes up the difference, more turrets, more glyphs, never more health.

### 1. Thousandstep (north-centre mesas, Earth)
- **The place:** a fortress town where every single street is a staircase, wealth is measured in altitude.
- **The trouble:** scarp bulls can't climb stairs (the city's whole defence for six centuries), and this spring one calf worked it out and taught the others. There are bulls on the ninth landing eating a duke's topiary.
- **Asks and gains:** hold the landing with conjured rock walls, the first new verb you get. The slate counts stairs lost.
- **Encounters:** defence, herds, native boss, first spars.
- **The beat:** the Dunnocks have already passed the exam, by accident, looking for lunch, and their line on the slate is outrageously good.
- **On return:** the calf is enormous now and has friends, and Lightning opens the sealed quarry.

### 2. The great tree (south-west, Life)
- **The place:** the tree is so big it has its own weather and the village of Windfall lives on what drops and legislates who owns a bough while it's still in the air.
- **The trouble:** a bumper year, and boars are eating the festival.
- **Asks and gains:** escort the prize cart and its truly hateful goose through the woods alive, and the slate counts goose. Wards on an ally come from Hob, who walks beside you healing you through your own fire and apologising to you for the fire.
- **Encounters:** escort, swarms, a keeping shrine.
- **On return:** Hob's oven with a queue round the root, and a spar against months of strange Life spells.

### 3. The Stirrup and Cullet (south-west badlands into the volcanic south, Fire)
- **The place:** the rock arch where both cohorts camp, and then the mountain itself, which spits molten glass that cools in the air and comes down at dawn as bubbles over a black land split with fire, and the children of Cullet run out with nets.
- **The trouble:** Cullet has bet everything on one commission, the largest pane of glass ever made, and it must go down the lava road on a cart.
- **Asks and gains:** the funniest escort in the game, because your own rocks and shoves will break it as fast as anything else. So you learn the parry from Bet, batting fire away from a window. The slate counts cracks.
- **Encounters:** the first joint defence at the arch, Dunnocks in friendly fire in both directions, then ambushes. The black spires hold constraint shrines.
- **The beat:** the joint supper under the arch, where Hob, announces he's been asked to stay at Windfall, apologising, and everyone screams with joy. First empty chair. Next morning Dov plants a turret and walks away from it, and you have never wanted anything so much.
- **On return:** Cullet's windows are lit from below by the lava, and one tiny pane has your names in it, slightly cracked.

### The Crossing, where the Sigil art opens
Three days across the central sea with everyone stuck on one deck, which is the game's low-pressure practice ground. You pester Dov about the turret until he takes your etched focus, Mr Sedge's own work, and drops it over the side. You will be furious. Three days of empty hands and flailing at dummies, and on the third morning the blade you're reaching for arrives somewhere other than your hand, stands there by the mast, and stays. You could only shape what you hold; now you shape the world around you. Your first turret takes out the last chapter of Captain Sloe's novel. The ferry is your fast travel from here.

### 4. Longlegs (north-east fen, Water, with storms)
Told below. Regen, pool, the shared line.

### 5. The Ribs (north, Cold)
- **The place:** you walk the ice road through the ribcage of something so vast the storms break on it, and you will not want to be out there at night. The monks of the Copying House live in the skull, transcribing every well's slate, and their oldest book records it plainly: it came up out of the north, lay down here, took nine winters to die, and the first monks sat with it in shifts.
- **The trouble:** armoured things have started coming to lie down inside the ribs by the hundred, across the road.
- **Asks and gains:** freeze a lead of open sea and run the sledges over it, past a boss the monks call the Prioress. The road brings timber to Longlegs.
- **Encounters:** gauntlet, caravan, boss, traversal over water you froze.
- **The beat:** in the copied slates are two hundred years of paired cohorts, and there's Mr Sedge's name beside Mrs Ruddock's, the Dunnock teacher. They just never left, which is why there are still two schools on one harbour. Then the gossip: Ines says she's here to count the ribs, but it's the bolt she's been working on, and she has it, and Bet tells her to leave it alone, and you hear a Dunnock shout for the first time. In the morning her chair is empty and nobody does an impression.
- **On return:** she has a new rib theory each visit and never looks at you. Her spars open on the answer to whatever element you've cast most.

### 6. Twice (north-west island, Lightning)
- **The place:** the blue crystal peak rings like a struck glass with every bolt, and each house in the lamp-makers' town proudly paints its hits on the door.
- **The trouble:** one great double strike each year fills every lamp on the island for winter, and someone must be at the top to catch the second bolt.
- **Asks and gains:** climb through swarms that chain and charge the coil line with your own spells. The slate counts friends struck, and Bet's line is a zero. Blink, for the ledges.
- **Encounters:** gauntlet, chain swarms, coils.
- **On return:** your names are painted on a door, with a tally of your own.

### 7. The Rucks (east, Arcane)
You go alone now, and I'll keep this short.
- **The place:** rain falling upward over purple hills that are in a different order each morning, and the tower floating above it all, with its great east window missing for forty years and things coming in through the hole at night. Mrs Aumbry has held that room alone with a curtain and a terrible temper.
- **Asks and gains:** the Cullet pane arrives on Sloe's ferry and you fit it, under attack. The largest regen step. If a tenth element comes in, Air blows through that hole.
- **The exam:** Arcane draws out what's already in a caster, so the well room fills with Bet and Hob and Ines and Dov, drawn out of your copybook and casting everything they ever hit you with. They're exactly as good as you remember. For the first time in forty hours, none of them surprises you.
- **The beat:** it's the only slate with no line above yours. You chalk it. Then it's supper, and the loser lays the table, so you lay it, and you sit down.

## The turns

**The midpoint: Longlegs.** A stilt town in a storm marsh, its two halves joined by the Long Walk, a three-hundred-year-old timber bridge with forty houses on it and a great bell in the middle, which Upwater and Downwater have each decided is theirs for Lantern Night. Nan and Gaffer Cobbold hire one cohort each to move it, three tons of bronze shoved up and down a street by spells, the most joyous fight of the summer, everyone conducting into everyone, until the marsh gas under the planks catches. Every stray spell damages the Walk, and the town can name who did the last of it. Forty homes burn, the bell goes through the deck into the fen, and a bolt through wet planking burns Bet's casting hand, yours or Ines's, and she won't let anyone look.

The exam is that night: put it out, with Water, in three lulls between the gas pockets, a new device learned in each. Both cohorts chalk one shared line, the number of houses saved, and it's the only slate that can never be retaken. No supper.

**After it,** each half trusts only its own cohort, so each builds out from its own bank, and Dov, who is frightened of water, stays to run the Dunnock end. Chair. Lantern Night is chalked on the first post (Gaffer: Wick Eve), and that's your clock. Out in the fen at night you can hear the bell, tolling, somewhere new every visit, because something the size of a chapel has climbed inside and is wearing it. Each return is a defence on the newest span against what follows that sound, each span needs one new art (glyphs, orbitals, auras) and teaches it by needing it, and what you built stays standing. Departed Dunnocks answer one summons per region, complaining. Bet comes back casting off-hand, all Shield and Sigil, and she's better than before.

**The late-game turn: the peak at Twice.** Bet's waiting at the top with the whole mountain prepared, the hardest spar in the game, and every try goes in the cover. Then you take it, cleanly, and she can't stop laughing, and she has conjured a second self to be her right hand and it goes in your copybook because it touched you. She walks down to the harbour and takes the letter. It's a posting a whole season's sail away, and she'd said she would come when somebody beat her and meant it. Every time you lost, you kept her. Last chair.

## The ending

Lantern Night. Hob's brought bread, Ines has come and sits at the far end, Dov's at his span, Bet has one day before her boat, and they are all on the field from the first minute, each with their own stretch, none covering for you. The thing comes up the fen tolling with everything that lives there behind it, and it's a party, the widest friendly fire of the game, until the bell is off its back and it bolts into the reeds, pink and outraged.

Then the two halves of the bridge meet a yard out of true, because each side measured its own way, and the supper table, laid across the gap because there's nowhere else, becomes the last span. Every Dunnock's chair is full. The Pargeters', from the fourth house on the Walk, is laid and stays empty, and their door stays shut. Dov hands you your focus: he'd never have gone near that rail, it was a spoon. Mr Sedge arrives radiant with a draughts board, his first win in forty years, and Mrs Ruddock, who had it in three, studies the ceiling. Ines folds a page into your copybook marked *if you want it*. Nan and Gaffer, in different words, ask you to stay.

In the morning the chairs are stacked and they have all gone. You walk the bridge alone with nothing left to do. On the table, in chalk, in a left-handed scrawl: a five-rune queue you have never seen and two words, *Your go.*

You may stand there as long as you like. If you can read runes, you know exactly what it does. It is the game's last input. You queue it and cast it, and it picks you up and throws you off the bridge into the river, and it writes itself into the copybook, last entry.

**Last image:** you, surfacing under the crooked join, people crossing overhead with baskets, and on the underside of the table, where only someone in the water could read it, *That's one to me.*

After the credits the world stays open, the room at the join has a lamp in it, and every Dunnock but one is at their post getting better. Bet's lines on the slates never change again, so they can be beaten for good.

## The recurring faces

- **Bet Oake.** Grins hardest when losing. Wants to be beaten properly, once.
- **Hob Tansley.** Apologises while casting. Wants to be asked to stay somewhere.
- **Ines Quarrie.** Studies your last fight and brings the answer. Wants a problem she can't solve.
- **Little Dov Ampleforth.** Enormous, afraid of water. Wants his name on something that lasts.
- **Captain Sloe.** Steers with her foot and reads. Wants to know how her novel ends.
- **Mr Sedge and Mrs Ruddock.** Draughts by post across a harbour they could row in ten minutes. He wants one win. She wants the game to go on.
- **Nan and Gaffer Cobbold.** Won't use each other's word for anything. Each wants the last one.
- **The Pargeters.** One lit window. They want their house.

## Why this is fun to play

You lose to someone pressing the same keys you have, and then the spell is in your book and you're trying it on a duck. Every old region holds a number you can now beat. In joint fights you aim through friends who aim through you, and half of what you learn is where to stand. The Dunnocks are four authored repertoires that grow each region, so you come to know who cast what from its shape.

## The biggest weakness

Players have to adore four people who get one line per supper, and with no doom to fall back on, thin barks and repertoires leave the ending a neat trick played on a stranger. The bell-wearing thing must also frighten in the dark for the whole back half, or the finale is a sketch.
