## 1. The weapons section

**The question.** What do beats 2 and 3 teach, now that the wizard has a family of conjured weapons and guards, when the player has no element of their own yet? And where does the section sit in the academy?

**The catch.** Every conjured weapon belongs to an element: the flame sword is Fire, the healing hands Life, the trident Water, the frost spear Cold, the ice bow Ice (Water and Cold together), the whip Lightning, the wand Arcane, the maul Earth. Guards need Shield. In the opening you earn those one stop at a time, and you have none of them in the yard. Canon already says early swords come from an etched focus: Sigil with Conjure, the other gestures locked (`r3-1-swords`).

Pillars: 1 (the moves are the mastery, and they carry over), 2 (a weapon's element is part of the spell language), 4 (a low-pressure place to try things).

### Directions

1. **Hands only; weapons come with their elements.** The yard teaches the moves with your staff and a training buckler, and no focus at all. Each stop's element arrives, and its weapon is shown on the way out. In the hands: the yard is short and has no magic. Cost: the first conjured weapon waits for the battery, and nothing in the yard shows that weapons exist.
2. **The moves, then one charged focus (as built, refined).** The stations teach the grammar every weapon shares (Strike, Heavy, Sweep, Retreat, Defend, the parry) with your staff, the weapon every wizard carries. At the end, the rack gives a focus charged from the academy's stores, enough to conjure one flame sword, and the straw goblins stand again to try it. In the hands: you learn Conjure once, and every later weapon is "the same keys with a new element". Cost: the flame sword comes from stored charge before Fire is yours, which slightly undersells the battery's moment.
3. **The armoury sampler (borrowed from Hades' courtyard of weapons).** A rack of foci, each charged with a different element, and a practice floor. Try any weapon before you leave. In the hands: you see the whole family on day one and pick a favourite. Partner: everyone tries something different side by side. Cost: eight movesets in ten minutes is the same overload as too many new enemies. It also spoils each stop's new weapon, which works against pillar 2's discovery. It does fit the lore: stored charge in a focus is how wizards worked before the shift.
4. **The soldiers' kit is the preview.** The hired soldiers drill in the middle of the yard with plain steel and wood: spears, a maul, longbows, a trident. You spar with two of them (the mallet's slam, the crossbow's bolt). Later, when the frost spear forms in your hand, you recognise it as "the soldier's spear, made of frost". Cost: almost nothing, since the soldiers are already scenery. It needs their figures to carry those weapons.
5. **Sparring with your friends (wild).** The yard ends with a friendly bout: blunt foci, and the players against each other or against a soldier. In the hands: the first lesson in friendly fire, as comedy (pillar 3). Solo spars a soldier. Cost: PvP needs its own rules (no deaths, a winner) and invites players to game it; it also lands before anyone can heal.
6. **Weapons taught on the walks.** Leaving each stop, the road has something to try the new weapon on: straw dummies at a guard post, brambles across a track. Cost: spreads the teaching thin, and every walk gets a lesson in it.
7. **The yard stays open (borrowed from Monster Hunter's training area and Hades' House).** Every road leads back through the courtyard, and the yard sits beside it. Its straw goblins stand again, so after each stop you can step in and try the new weapon on them, or walk past. In the hands: a low-pressure practice space in the middle of the campaign's first hour (pillar 4). Cost: one more thing to keep working as the yard gets reworked; nothing new to build.

### Claude's pick: 2, 4 and 7 together

The yard teaches the moves once with your staff, because every weapon shares them: that's pillar 1, and it's what's built. The single charged focus teaches Conjure with the flame sword, and the flame sword foreshadows the battery. The soldiers carry the weapon family in plain steel, so each later weapon is recognised rather than explained. And since you pass the yard every time you come back to the courtyard, it stays open as the place to try each new weapon as you earn it. Each new weapon gets one line on the walk out ("Sigil, Life, Conjure: healing hands").

Not picked: the sampler (3), because it repeats the "too many new things at once" problem and spoils the stops. Sparring (5) is a good later idea once healing and wards exist, perhaps as an optional bout in the yard on a return visit.

### Where it sits

The round 2 district put the drill yard beside the central courtyard (`area-courtyard-start.jpg`), and the academy concept (2026-11-15) put the lessons along the foot of two real-size ranges. Together:

- **The briefing (beat 1) at the armillary,** in the courtyard rather than the lecture hall: the masters talk on the plinth steps while the column stutters, so the morning's trouble is in view from the first minute. The lecture hall stays an option.
- **The yard opens off the courtyard,** a long walled yard between two ranges, with one worn path from the hall's door, past the stations at the foot of the buildings, back through a passage under a gate tower to the courtyard.
- **Its middle is the soldiers' drill ground,** open ground you look across, not a station.
- **Leaving it, you're back in the courtyard,** and the first road (to the infirmary, just outside the main gate) is short.

The yard as built (`#yard`, 90 × 68, marked too small) becomes the blockout to rework, not thrown away: the stations and their order stay.

## 2. The walks

**The question.** What happens on the roads between the stops, and how long are they? The user's rule from the first segment page: "I don't want just tiny walks between encounters, I think you should sometimes walk a bit before something happens, because it makes the world feel more real" (`general`, 2026-11-09). Pillars: 5 (told through places), 7 (visible approaches), 1 (returning players).

### Directions

1. **Quiet corridors (conservative, Magicka).** Roads with nothing on them, short, to get you to the next fight. Cost: the walks the user asked to be real become filler.
2. **Leave, middle, arrive (the walk has a shape).** *Leaving:* the aftermath of the stop, and something harmless to try the new element on that the world offers (brambles to burn below the battery, a burning bush to douse in the gorge, a frozen net on the harbor stairs). *The middle:* quiet, with people and views: a cart of wounded, guards running for the wall, workers. *Arriving:* a lookout where you see the next stop's trouble from above before you're in it.
3. **Stray fights.** A small fight of enemies you've already met on the longer walks, to practise the new element under low pressure. Cost: a walk that's always a fight is a corridor between arenas; at most one per long walk.
4. **The hub changes (borrowed from Hades' House and Zelda's Hyrule Castle).** Every road goes back through the courtyard, and the courtyard changes each time: one more of the armillary's seven channels lights, the wounded move into the cloister, the masters are somewhere new, someone remarks on what you just did. Progress shown in the world, not in a menu (the user's note on the progress map, `img-area-arcane`).
5. **A landmark on every road (borrowed from Journey and Breath of the Wild).** The armillary's violet column can be seen from every road, so you always know where home is; smoke, spray and the ward's shimmer mark the others.
6. **Short ways back for returning players (borrowed from Dark Souls' shortcuts).** A gate or a lift you open from the far side, so the second time through a walk is quick (the brief's question 6, pillar 1).
7. **The morning worsens (wild).** No timer, but the world on the roads moves on while you're away: the gorse fire spreads toward the cistern, more wounded on the infirmary road, the harbor's ice creeps up the stairs. Cost: state to keep per road; risks reading as a timer.

### Claude's pick: 2, 4 and 5, with 3 sparingly

Each walk has a shape: leave with something to try, a quiet middle with the world in it, and arrive at a lookout. The courtyard changes each time you pass it, and the armillary's column is the landmark from every road. Stray fights only on the two longest walks (the gorge and the harbor stairs), with enemies already met. Shortcuts (6) come later, with the skip for returning players. The worsening world (7) is already in the plan for the gorse fire; keep it to that one.

**How long.** One screen takes about 7 seconds to cross (the segment page's rules of scale). A walk of 20 to 60 seconds is 3 to 8 screens; seven walks at about 40 seconds is about 5 minutes of the opening's 65.

## As built: the yard's blockout (2026-11-22)

- **The plan** is 130 × 100 u (78 × 60 m), about half as big again as the 90 × 68 marked too small. The Great Hall (50 m) and the library wing stand past the west edge, the workshops (85 m, three storeys) and the armoury tower past the north edge, so their faces are the map's edge and nothing collides with a grey box. The client draws them (`art/massing.ts`), on by default in the yard; `?massing=off` hides them.
- **The route** (about 290 u, a minute and a bit at walking pace): the armillary in the courtyard, through the balustrade's west opening, north along the hall (strike lanes, the sack, the slam ring), east along the workshops (the fenced parry lane, the hands ring, the rack at the armoury door), back through the north opening into the courtyard and out by the east gate to the battery.
- **Clear before it acts:** the slam, the bolts and the straw goblins' rush wait until someone has stood in the station for `YARD.ready` (1.5 s). The prompt shows from 8 u away, so you read it before you step in. The parry lane is fenced on both sides, open at your end, with the crossbowman in sight on his platform.
- **The drill ground** holds the soldiers' plain steel with what the rigs have: spears, a mallet, crossbows, practice swords. The maul, longbows and trident in the concept are art still to make.
- **Known limits:** a grey box draws under everything, so props north or west of one show on top of it; the courtyard keeps those sides clear. The briefing (beat 1) isn't built: you start at the armillary with the masters standing there.
