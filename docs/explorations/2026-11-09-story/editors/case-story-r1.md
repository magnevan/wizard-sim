# Story notes: What He Had on Him (round 1)

Evidence is read with spells until the answer matters. Then people tell you. Fix that first.

1. **"Her married name is Greet Vrolijk" cannot work.** You question Hask survivors for half the game, and all of them know who the gateman's apprentice was. A name card is also the weakest reveal available. Direction: she says on day one, "I was his apprentice. I was at my aunt's that night." The reveal becomes where she was.

2. **The solution is told, not read.** Piet says it, Greet confesses it. Direction: at the Fen's first tide-shift she shows you the crank, low, two-handed, "it sticks." After the Ice Road, frost works on anything she has gripped, unprompted. The foundry's order book, relit, shows a new pin ordered the day after he drowned, so he reached her alive. The last reading, on her rail, takes ten seconds of standing still.

3. **No crime for twelve hours, and the Fen gate is a hole.** Nothing suggests anyone let him drown. The body came down the Fen's river, so anyone would go upriver first. Direction: the living cutting proves he went in within a day. Greet, at the slip, says the Fen is shut till spring. Later you read who signed the closure.

4. **The first three lands are one beat three times** (false name, quiet kindness), and free order stops them building. Joost is a saint, so his death costs little. Direction: one land hates him with cause. He also left a seventeen-year-old alone at a gate on a tide night. Let that count against him.

5. **The midpoint runs on facts you lack.** "Every household he paid" arrives two lands later. "Their stories change" is all we get of the promised re-questioning. Direction: write each changed story as a new fact. Let the host name a suspect mid-game, with visible harm when wrong.

6. **What Joost knew contradicts itself, and the title goes unanswered.** He "never knew otherwise", yet comes asking who wound the gate. Why those seven things, that night? Direction: his own case file, one reading short.

7. **Both findings clear him and merge two crimes.** A girl's accident killed 212. A grown woman's count killed one. Direction: two casebook lines, gate and plank, answered separately.

8. **Cas's false confession dies in one sentence.** It is your only rival answer. Let it stand for a land or two. Small: "three-fingered" versus "lost three fingers"; "Readers" collides with "readings".

## Do not touch

- Readings as ordinary spells that can destroy the evidence.
- "I'll tell you after the tide", the boots at cost, Greet on the crank.
- Piet's one fixed line, and the fishmonger's count of ten.

## Ban list

- **28, near:** Greet turning up everywhere. Make it traceable: she reached each place he worked days before you.
- **10, near:** the nicest face did it. Note 1 helps.
- **45, near:** "the Readers".
