# Story brief (2026-11-09)

Everything here is a set of options for the game's creator, who owns the lore and will choose. Nothing written from this brief is canon.

## The game

A cooperative top-down spellcasting game, one to four players, inspired by Magicka but with a slower, more deliberate pace and an RPG-length campaign. You compose spells by queuing element runes (up to five) and picking a cast shape. Friendly fire is on and is half the comedy. Read `docs/vision.md` for the pillars and `docs/world/elements.md` for what each element is.

## The world

Classic, grounded fantasy with a light touch: warmth, the odd dry joke. Not Magicka's slapstick. Not grimdark.

Magic runs through the land like weather and water. A **field** is broad and ambient, like climate. **Veins** are narrow channels within and between fields, like rivers. A **well** is where veins surface and converge. Civilisation grew around these: smiths at fire, farms on water and life, mines on earth, rudimentary electricity from lightning. Seven field elements: Fire, Cold, Water, Lightning, Earth, Life, Arcane. Shield and Sigil are innate arts, not fields. A tenth element is undecided and may appear as one late unlock.

## The frame, which every candidate keeps

**The journeyman's circuit.** After their schooling, young wizards travel out to the great wells in turn and attune to each. It is a tradition. Mastering one aspect at a time, in a new land each time, is the shape of the game. The creator notes it has a slight Pokémon flavour in that one way and finds that fun, but we are explicitly NOT recreating that pattern: no gym-leader formula, no badges for their own sake, no creature collecting, no tournament finale by default.

What the circuit is for, why it matters, and what goes wrong with it are yours to decide.

## Hard requirements

**It is a game, and you get stronger throughout.** Beats have to give the player things to do and reasons to feel growth. Decided so far:
- Rune pools start at 1 per element and grow to at most about 5. The player chooses which pool deepens at each attunement.
- Regen (runes per second) starts low and grows most of all.
- The five-rune queue exists from the start. Complex spells are possible early but slow to repeat.
- Sigil starts limited: an etched focus from school lets you conjure weapons and guards only. The full art (turrets, glyphs, Blink, auras and so on) opens later as a story beat, and Shield's wider arts can follow the same path. The moment is "you could only shape what you hold; now you shape the world around you".
- Regions bend your regen toward their element in three bands: the edge, the heartland, the well. The well is the region's exam.
- Coming back to old regions with new magic opens side paths.

**Opening.** The game needs an opening sequence that teaches and gets you out the door. It does not have to be deeply woven into the plot. A graduation, one last lesson, a series of flashbacks: these are examples of what is possible, not a list to choose from. Keep it short in your document, and easy to swap.

**One player or four.** The party is a cohort sent out together; alone, you are the one sent. Every line is addressed to "you", which does not say how many. No named protagonist. No beat that depends on the party's size. No puzzle that requires two people. Choices, if any, are the host's or a quick vote.

**Told through places.** Many characters met in simple ways, each with one trait and one want. No deep companions, no romance arcs, no long conversations. A recurring handful is welcome (a trader, a cartographer, someone who writes to you). Visible change in the world does the work dialogue would: a town rebuilt, a road reopened, a well weaker or stronger on each visit.

**Encounter types available:** set-piece battles, defences (town, camp, ritual, caravan), gauntlets, ambushes, escorts, native bosses, enemy casters who use the same spell language as you, puzzle rooms, traversal opened by magic, and optional shrines (puzzle, constraint, keeping).

## What to write

A high-level story and progression document of **1,800 to 2,800 words** in this order:

1. **Title and one-sentence pitch.**
2. **The question** the player is carrying from start to finish, and how the ending answers it.
3. **The opening,** short.
4. **The road.** Region by region in order (or explain the order's freedom). For each: where on the map; what attuning there means and asks of you; what you gain in play; the kind of encounters; the one story beat that matters; how the place has changed if you come back.
5. **The turns.** The midpoint and the late-game turn, and the point where the Sigil art opens.
6. **The ending** and the last image.
7. **The recurring faces.** One trait and one want each.
8. **Why this is fun to play,** in three or four sentences, from the player's hands.
9. **The biggest weakness** you can see in your own story.

Write plainly. Prefer specific, surprising, concrete details over atmosphere. Beats, not prose: this is a plan someone will build from, not a story to read.
