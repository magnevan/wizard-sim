# 2026-11-03: Co-op Magicks

Prompted by the user's note on Clothesline in gallery round 2: "We can explore more co-op Magicks." Claude's brainstorm, for the user to rate.

## What makes a co-op Magick
- It needs **two (or more) casters doing their part**, and each part is something a player *does*: an aim, a position, a timing. Pressing the same button together doesn't count.
- The parts can be **asymmetric**, so an experienced player can carry the hard half while a newcomer does the easy half. This is pillar 3: "carry less experienced friends".
- **Casting:** each participant types the Magick's sequence. The first caster *opens* it (a visible glyph appears on them) and partners have a few seconds to *join* by casting the same sequence. If nobody joins, it fizzles harmlessly. This keeps it deliberate, never accidental.
- These are unlocked like Magicka's tomes. They give real power, which fits softened pillar 1.

## Candidates

| Magick | The two parts | When it's the best answer |
|---|---|---|
| **Clothesline** | Each caster is an anchor; a crackling line forms between them. The two *walk* to sweep it through a wave. | A wave rushing through a gap between two well-positioned players |
| **Resurrect Rite** | One player channels over the fallen ally (rooted, interruptible). The partner must keep enemies off them. | A friend is down mid-wave and waiting for the wave to end costs too much (fits the "out until the wave ends" death rule) |
| **Comet Call** | The *spotter* holds a reticle on the target area; the *caller* channels. The comet lands where the spotter is aiming when the channel completes. | A big pack in the open, especially a brute plus swarmers |
| **Mirror Pair** | Each caster raises a mirror; a beam fired at one mirror bounces to the other and out at the second caster's aim. | Reaching a back-line caster around cover |
| **Tandem Leap** | One player plants a glyph; the other sprints into it and is launched to where the first is aiming, landing in a burst. | Escape from a surround, or crossing a gap (traversal puzzles) |
| **Shared Ward** | Both players are linked by a tether; damage either takes is split between them while the link holds. The link snaps if they move too far apart. | Protecting a fragile newcomer while you tank half their hits |
| **Elemental Chorus** | Both channel the *same* element at one point; it grows into a storm of that element. A third player adding a *combining* element (water into fire = steam) changes the storm type. | A boss or a long-held position. A three- or four-player showpiece |
| **Ward Bridge** | Each player places one end; a solid shield bridge forms between the two ends. Enemies can't cross it and allies can. | A traversal puzzle, or cutting a room in half to split a wave |

## Notes
- **Asymmetry:** Resurrect Rite, Comet Call and Tandem Leap have a clear "easy part" (stand guard, aim a reticle, walk into a glyph), which makes them good for experienced + new pairs.
- **The user cut a similar idea before:** Fastball Sling (a solo-cast sling) was cut, so Tandem Leap is only interesting if the *two-player* version feels different. Flag it.
- **Overlap with the spell tree:** Mirror Pair is close to Lens (a structure that redirects beams). The Magick version is bigger and needs both players.
- **Solo play:** single-player needs fallbacks. Either co-op Magicks are unavailable solo, or a "spirit echo" plays the second part badly. Magicka had a similar problem with Revive (it was a Magick).
