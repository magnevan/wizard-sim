# The Flitting Year

**Pitch.** Once in 211 years every field in the world moves one seat around the great sea, in a known order, on a published date, and two million people, a flying school and a herd that takes nine days to pass a fixed point must move with them.

## The question

**Will it all be across before its field goes?** The ending answers with a count, town by town: most of it, and exactly what was lost, shaped by the jobs you took and the jobs you left.

**What everyone knows.** Six fields sit in seven seats. The empty seat has no field on it, so the deep Arcane lies bare to the sky. In a Flit the field beside the gap slides into it, the next field follows, and so on round the sea for a year. Children learn the order from a game called Shove Along.

| Seat | Now | After |
|---|---|---|
| East, the long purple land | bare Arcane | Fire |
| South, the volcano | Fire | Life |
| South-west, the great tree and the arch | Life | Lightning |
| North-west, the crystal island | Lightning | Cold |
| The northern ice | Cold | Earth |
| The ochre mesas | Earth | Water |
| The stilt-city fen | Water | bare Arcane |

**What a front does.** A field leaves in one day, along a wall of weather that crosses its whole land and can be seen from sixty miles. As the line passes, everything the field was holding lets go: forges burst, dams fail, ice roads open, standing crops die within the hour. Behind it lies bare Arcane, where nothing planted sets and nothing heavy stays reliably down, and a person's hands and sleep begin to fail within a season. The land takes a lifetime to follow its field. The people get one day.

**What comes up.** Wherever the ground lies bare, sounders rise: deep-field animals that surface and feed in the open gap as whales do at a krill bloom. They follow it around the world all year, and they grow.

**What the circuit is for.** Each well is ringed by groynes, basalt breakwaters ten generations in the building, running out for miles. A wizard attuned to the well can feed them and turn a front by a few hundred yards, which decides whether a city's granary goes under. Every cohort for 211 years has walked the circuit so that trained hands would exist when the year came. You drew the year.

**The calendar.** Each leg offers eight dated jobs and five stretches of about six days. A job costs a stretch, as does a trip back. The calendar moves only when you commit, and the host or a quick vote picks. A skipped job falls to the class below yours, sixteen months less trained and in tremendous spirits, and returns as a harder salvage on the same map. Each seat is one map whose bands, palette and enemies change with its field, and herds migrate with theirs.

**Growing.** At every well you deepen any pool you choose and take a large regen step. Once a new field has settled, its well offers a second attunement. Jobs pay regen fractions and new workings.

## The opening

The academy is the floating tower, a mountain's worth of masonry adrift over the eastern purple, held by chains a mile long. It floats only on bare Arcane, so it must be towed after the gap. Your last week is the great packing, and every lesson is a load: clear the rune lab, rig the chains, get the dummies down nine hundred steps. You receive your etched focus, weapons and guards only. Graduation is parting the first mooring chain with the blade it conjures, while the whole school cheers on the battlements. Then the road south.

## The road

The order is the Flit's. **Rest** is the tower's roof, a plotting floor forty paces across inlaid with the continent, where you walk to your next job. On the eve of each lift the departing field stands up into the night as a wall of coloured light, and the whole region comes out of doors to look.

### 1. The South: Fire leaves first
- **Asks:** a front crossing the arena, Fire ahead of the line and bare Arcane behind. Feed the groynes to bend it seaward, away from the town.
- **Jobs that collide:** pour the great winch for the tower's tow, which needs more heat than the calendar has, so you are the heat, feeding a crucible between waves. Or clear the skid road for Wenna Tregear's forge trains, ninety anvils on runners. Skip the winch and the class below casts a flawed one. Skip the road and Wenna is lost.
- **Fights:** fire-herds, a fire-mare tall as a church, the first sounders, dog-sized.
- **The beat:** the mountain has lit the southern sky for 211 years, and sailors steer by it from two hundred miles out. On the day, four thousand people set out folding chairs and a brass band. It goes dark on schedule, in a single breath, and they applaud until their hands hurt.
- **Back:** a purple crater with the tower chained above it, and an Arcane constraint trial that exists only until Life comes. Then green ash, as thick as the Life you carried. The black spires are mooring stacks from the last Flit, reached by Blink.

### 2. The South-west: Life has to be carried
- **Asks:** Life goes only where living things take it. Hold the well's front open behind the last wagons, where it flips your regen hard against you.
- **Jobs that collide:** bring the herds through the great arch, or help Hesper Gaunt string copper through the tree's crown before the first storm.
- **Fights:** the largest escort in the game, cattle, deer, hives on poles and seed carts streaming nine days through a stone gate a thousand feet high. A pack-mother works the flanks. Barley farmers defend their fields with Earth and Life.
- **The beat:** the tree cannot walk. It stands higher than any cloud that crosses it, and Lightning is coming. Half the town leaves weeping. Hesper's half climbs. The first strike is visible from the sea. If you strung the copper, a mile of tree blazes blue and stands. If you did not, the crown burns for a month and Hesper is gone.
- **Back:** lamps on every limb or a black crown, storms breaking on it weekly, a Lightning shrine in its roots open only in storm season.

### 3. The North-west island: Lightning, and a plan
- **Asks:** bleed the crystal peak's charge away groyne by groyne as the front tries to leave at once.
- **Jobs that collide:** take the outer coil offline, or ferry Lamp Street's families off.
- **Fights:** chaining swarms, a storm that moves as one body, coil-yard casters, skilled and terrified, turret against turret across a yard of copper.
- **The beat:** the island stays. Ilse Penhallow's coil will hold a charge through 211 years of Cold, and thirty thousand people have chosen to believe her. The peak draws down the sky for three days. It grounds through the town. Whatever you did, a stretch of Lamp Street is gone.
- **Back:** the sea frozen to the mainland, a road where ferries ran. The gap in the street is still there. Ilse is rebuilding the coil and does not mention it. One crystal face stays lit for a winter, and a shrine inside it lasts as long as the light does.

### 4. The northern ice: Cold, and the stores of the world
- **Asks:** chill the road behind you as you go, attuning on the move.
- **Jobs that collide:** run the last seed sledges, or cut Ottoline Brack out of the monastery's ice vault, where she has lain since the last Flit.
- **Fights:** convoy gauntlet over breaking ice, an armoured thing that has waited under the road for two centuries.
- **The beat:** eleven thousand sledges carry the continent's seed and records south across the ice. The road runs for most of a day through the ribs of Late, a sounder that stayed up too long at the last Flit and had Cold close over it. Its skull looks back the way it came. Drivers say the name once and fall silent until the last rib is behind them.
- **Back:** meltwater thundering over bare rock, Late standing clear of the ice for the first time, Earth shrines surfacing in the thaw.

### 5. The ochre mesas: Earth and the great lock
- **Asks:** hold the groynes through a four-day earthquake as the stone herds walk north through your fight.
- **Jobs that collide:** shore the low gorge road as a fallback, or turn the herds from the valley.
- **Fights:** the procession, ending at a lead-beast the size of a hill. Masons on the walls with Earth and Shield. The siege of a lock chamber.
- **The beat:** the mesa-fortress holds the only high road from the fen, behind a gate that fills a canyon, and it will not admit ninety thousand strangers. The Carrows themselves came up this valley behind Earth at the last Flit, let through by those before them. Their founders feared what wealth does to memory, and sent the only key north on ice with their surveyor. Ottoline turns it. The lock takes days to run, and you hold the chamber against the frightened masons while it does. How long you hold sets the days the fen has left.
- **Back:** new rivers cutting the ochre, the fortress rising from a lake, a drowned quarry shrine reachable by freezing the water.

### 6. The fen: Water, last and shortest
- **The beat:** ninety thousand people, in a city on stilts, facing the worst seat of all. Their plan needs sixty days. You bought them between twelve and twenty-four.
- **Back:** see The ending.

## The turns

**Midpoint: the Strait, and the Sigil art.** As the island's field loosens, a strip of bare Arcane opens across forty miles of open sea for nine days, and the tower crosses on it behind Tregear's winch (or the flawed one) and eleven hundred fishing boats. Ishbel Orrin's watchers row alongside in their thousands. Under open sky with no land beneath you, the deep field reaches you unfiltered, and it draws out what the school only hinted at. A chain parts. Your blade is useless against a sea. The art opens, and one forced glyph holds the line: you could only shape what you held, and now you shape the world around you. Hulls hold their place while the water scrolls. Six calm days follow on the decks, teaching turrets, glyphs and Blink, with crews racing one another and singing across the gap. You attune to Arcane on the seventh. Then the strip closes **a day short**, Bettany cannot explain it, and the sounders come up the size of ships. The tower's west wing tears away with every old edition of the almanac, and 212 people from the tow boats do not come home. From here you know the count will have names in it.

**Late turn: the far end of the bench.** On the ice road, Cold lifts eleven days early and the last lifts begin to crowd together. Every child knows the far end of the bench gets shoved hardest. The first almanac carried a figure for it, and a later edition tidied it away as a misprint. The game kept what the book dropped. The stretches visibly shrink on the plotting floor, four to three to two. On that road the monks' crews teach you the old ward-work, and Shield's wider arts open: you hold a piece of the world still while the sledges go over it. If you cut Ottoline out, she walks the line of the road with a measuring stride and tells you exactly how bad it will be.

## The ending

The front does not wait for the last job. The fen's barges and unbolted stilt-houses ride the leading edge of Water up the valley, eleven miles of moving city, and you fight along it in stages while the line of bare Arcane runs behind you and your regen changes with every band. You attune to Water in the middle of it, the pool deepening as the well goes out beneath you. Each job you took is a device on the route that you feed: Hesper's copper line arcs down the barges when charged, Wenna's furnace hulls take Fire, ice booms take Cold, Ilse's last coil takes Lightning. Where you left a job, there is a bare post. Everything alive in the fen stampedes through the middle, and then the sounders arrive, grown all year. The last of them breaches over the fleet a mile long, and you see what Late was. Without the key the barges take the gorge, and it is a worse fight. It ends when the last hull clears the mesa's weir or the front catches it.

Nobody saves the world, because the world is fine. The Flit completes, every colour on the map sits one seat along, and the whole continent is open to walk again.

**The answer** is on the plotting floor, where a lamp for each town moves to where its people ended, and the barges your own hands saved are counted among them. Where nobody arrived, there is no lamp. A new almanac goes to press. If Ottoline lived, her figure is back in it. If not, the misprint stands, and in 211 years someone will pay for it.

**Last image:** the tower hangs at the end of its last chain above the drowned piers of the fen. On the final night all six fields stand up at once in seats nobody living has seen them in, walls of light around the entire sea, horizon to horizon. Far below in the emptied purple, forty windows are lit.

## The recurring faces

- **Bettany Rook, head porter.** A voice that carries two miles over water. Wants to bring the tower in with everyone she left with, and after the Strait she cannot.
- **Ottoline Brack, surveyor of the last Flit.** Paces out every road she walks. Wants her figure back in the book.
- **Wenna Tregear, smith.** Sings at the anvil, badly and at volume. Wants the first fire lit in the east, and if she lives you see that forge grow.
- **Ishbel Orrin, leader of the Following.** Has not looked at the ground in years. Wants to stand nearer each lift than anyone has, and brings thirty thousand watchers who become two hundred thousand spare hands.
- **Pim Akehurst, of the class below.** Has never finished reading an instruction. Wants to be sent on something important, and you will see what happens when Pim is.
- **Oswin Carrow, head of the mesa family.** Afraid, and scrupulously polite about it. Wants the gate shut one more day.
- **Perpetua Lunt, of the fen.** Refuses all advice. Stays. On your return she is tethered to her bed, white-haired, her hands failing, and each evening she hauls herself along a rope to light the lamps in her neighbours' empty houses.

## Why this is fun to play

A wall of changing ground crosses the fight, your regen differs on either side of it, and you bend that line with your own spells. Old roads come back bending your regen another way, and each holds something available only in that season. Choosing five jobs from eight hurts, and in the finale you are feeding the tools you earned or standing at a bare post. With friends, a moving city under attack is friendly fire at its grandest.

## The biggest weakness

Nobody can be beaten for it. The sounders give the finale a body, but the true opponent is a date, and players who want a face get a frightened mason. The repeated shape of arrive, choose, front could also tire by the mesas.
