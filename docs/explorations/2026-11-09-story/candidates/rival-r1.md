# Your Go

Two small schools share one harbour and one ferry, their leavers go round the wells in the same summer, and you spend forty hours trying to beat four people you will miss.

## The question

**Are they better than you?** You ask it the first afternoon, when a stranger beats you with a spell you could have cast yourself. The ending answers twice. At the second-to-last well you finally win properly, and that win is what ends the summer. Then, after everyone has gone, she beats you once more from a long way off, with your own hands.

**What pulls you through forty hours with no doom:**
- **The walls.** Every well has a slate where each cohort chalks its names, the runes spent per head to pass the exam, and the five-rune queue it finished with. The Dunnocks' line is your par. They keep improving it behind your back, so old walls change.
- **The copybook.** Any spell a Dunnock beats you with goes into your copybook as a queue. The best teaching in the game comes from losing.
- **The table.** Each region ends at one long supper, both cohorts, one line each. Loser lays the table. Someone does an impression of the last time you hit yourself or an ally, naming the actual element. There is one chair fewer as the game goes on, and it is never mourned on screen.
- **The bridge.** From the midpoint, a thing you and they are building, visibly, from opposite ends.

The largest stake in the game is a fen town and these four people.

## The opening

Leavers' Fête on the quay at Pennick, on the ochre peninsula in the northern bay, above an Arcane vein that runs under the strait. Each stall is an element, one rune of each: freeze the duck pond, soak the mayor at the dunking booth without soaking the queue. The teacher etches your focus, and you conjure your first blade. Any stall can be skipped. Last stall: a coconut shy, where a stranger from the school across the water casts one enormous five-rune spell, flattens the shy, then stands grinning for a full minute while her runes come back. This is Bet. There is only one ferry out, so both cohorts board it together.

## The road

The circuit runs round the sea. Mesas come first. Forest and Volcano can be swapped, and the Dunnocks take whichever you didn't. After the Crossing the order is fixed, because the back half is a countdown. Each attunement steps your regen and deepens one pool of your choice. Beating an old wall on a return visit deepens one more, so pools end up uneven and yours. Each well's exam is a practical job set by the people who live on it. Attuning means working a place until your casting has its local accent.

### 1. The mesas (north-centre, Earth)
- **Place:** Plumb, the fortress town on the great mesa, wealthy and proud that nothing here has moved in living memory.
- **Trouble:** the terrace wall has moved one inch. Nobody will say so. Scarp bulls have walked in through the inch.
- **Asks and gains:** the exam is holding the terrace against a stampede with walls paid for once. Regen step, first pool choice.
- **Encounters:** town defence, bull herds, a native boss in the cistern, the first spar (first to three touches, retry freely).
- **The beat:** you reach the well to find the Dunnocks have already passed, by accident, while looking for lunch. Their chalk line is on the slate, and low.
- **On return:** the inch has a brass plaque and a small visitor trade. Lightning opens the sealed quarry.

### 2. The great tree (south-west, Life)
- **Place:** Windfall, a village under one enormous tree, living entirely off what drops. Its law is about who owns a branch while it is falling.
- **Trouble:** a bumper year. Everything has bred. Boars are eating the festival.
- **Asks and gains:** Life only heals, so the exam is an escort: get the prize cart and its deeply unpleasant goose through the woods alive. Regen, pool, and the copybook's first healing spells from Hob.
- **Encounters:** escort, swarms, a keeping shrine where you may not harm anything for one room.
- **The beat:** Windfall's baker-healer retires and asks Hob to stay. He says yes at supper, apologising. Everyone cheers. It reads as a joke. It is the first empty chair.
- **On return:** Hob's oven, a queue outside it, and a spar against someone who has spent months doing nothing but odd Life spells.

### 3. The Stirrup and the south (south-west badlands into volcanic south, Fire)
- **Place:** the great rock arch is where the two routes cross. South of it is Wherever, the glassmakers' town, built on sledges because the fire field flares and fades and the kilns must follow. "Where are you from?" "Wherever."
- **Trouble:** the field has shifted early and the town must move in the wrong season, mayor still in the bath.
- **Asks and gains:** Fire is wasteful, so the exam is the caravan's last mile on a par the locals think is funny. Regen, pool. The black spires hold constraint shrines for later.
- **Encounters:** at the arch, the first joint defence, Dunnocks in friendly-fire range for the first time, in both directions. Then caravan defence and ambushes from quick, burning things.
- **The beat:** at the arch, Dov puts a turret on the ground and walks away from it. You can only shape what you hold. You have never wanted anything so much.
- **On return:** Wherever is somewhere else, and you have to find it. Your old camp has been turned into a glass shop.

### Interlude: the Crossing
Three days on the ferry with both cohorts, across the central sea. The deck is the game's low-pressure practice space: spars, copybook drills, dummies, Captain Sloe's mast at risk. You blow up the last chapter of the captain's novel, whatever you cast. The ferry returns as your fast travel.

### 4. Longlegs (north-east fen, Water, with Lightning in the storms)
The midpoint, told below. Gains: regen, pool, the Sigil art. Encounters: a real dispute against the Dunnocks, then an all-night town defence. On return: the Cut, and two mismatched bridge ends that are longer each time.

### 5. The ice and the ribs (north, Cold)
- **Place:** the Racks, an ice-fishing camp that dries its catch on the colossal skeleton. Nobody knows what it was and nobody is curious, because it is excellent for fish. Beside it is the Copying House, whose monks transcribe every well's slate before the weather takes it.
- **Trouble:** huge armoured things have been waiting outside the fish store for eleven years. The camp runs a betting pool on when they'll move. They have moved.
- **Asks and gains:** freeze a lead of open sea into an ice road and run the sledges across it. Regen, pool. The ice road is what carries timber to Longlegs.
- **Encounters:** gauntlet, caravan, native boss (the largest, known as Aunt), traversal over water you freeze yourself.
- **The beat:** in the copied walls you see two hundred years of paired cohorts, Pennick and Dunnock, and your own teacher's name beside the name of the Dunnock teacher. They stayed. That is why there are still two schools on one harbour. Then Ines, who has noticed the skeleton has more ribs on one side, takes a cell here to work out why. Chair.
- **On return:** the road is permanent and has a toll cottage selling fish. Ines has a new theory every visit, and each spar with her is built against whatever you've cast most lately. The captain's novel can be found on these shelves.

### 6. Twice (north-west island, Lightning, with Cold)
- **Place:** an island of lamp-makers and coils around the blue crystal peak, named for how lightning treats it. The town runs on a published timetable of strikes.
- **Trouble:** the strikes are arriving a minute late and everyone on the island is in a foul temper.
- **Asks and gains:** climb to the peak through swarms that chain, and recharge the coil line. Regen, pool, and Blink's first use, on the peak's broken ledges.
- **Encounters:** gauntlet up the mountain, chain swarms that punish standing near a friend, coil devices fed with your own spells, and the late-game turn at the top.
- **On return:** the timetable is reprinted with your names in it, as a cause of delay.

### 7. The Rucks (east, Arcane)
- **Place:** a long purple land where the crust has always been rucked and torn, the way a coast is rocky. The floating tower is Mrs Aumbry's boarding house, tethered by anchor chains and drifting about ten feet a year.
- **Trouble:** a chain has parted. She has six lodgers and will not come down.
- **Asks and gains:** Arcane draws out what is already in you, so this is where Attune stops being a trick and becomes how you fight. The last regen step, the largest. If a tenth element comes in, Air is the lodgers' kite line.
- **Encounters:** puzzle rooms, strange lone creatures, enemy casters from a tenants' dispute, re-anchoring the tower.
- **The beat:** you arrive at the last slate first. It is the only wall in the game with no line above yours. You spent forty hours wanting this. You chalk it and there is nobody to read it.
- **On return:** Mrs Aumbry has let your room to someone else, and kept your hat.

## The turns

**The midpoint: Longlegs.** A town on stilts, split into Upwater and Downwater, led by a brother and sister who refuse to use each other's words for anything. Upwater needs the sluice shut for the eel run. Downwater needs it open before storm season. Both are right. Each hires a cohort. This is the first real dispute: a fight on wet piers in a rainstorm, the Dunnocks casting everything they have taught you, and everyone conducting into everyone. It is the funniest arena in the game until the sluice goes. The storm takes the middle of the town, a gap two hundred yards wide now called the Cut. Nobody dies. Forty homes are gone, and it was both of you. A chain bolt through the planking has burned Bet's casting hand. It could have been yours or Ines's, and she refuses to let anyone find out. There is no supper. It is the only region without one.

**The Sigil art opens that same night.** The piers are going, you are out of runes and hands, and Dov shouts one piece of advice across the Cut: "Put it down and leave it there." You drive your conjured blade into a failing pier, let go, and it stays. You could only shape what you hold; now you shape the world around you. The well's exam is holding what's left of the town until dawn, with turrets and glyphs you are inventing as creatures climb the piles. Nobody chalks that wall. Blink, auras and the rest follow in training on later returns.

**After it,** each half of Longlegs trusts only its own cohort, so each builds out from its own side. Dov, terrified of water, stays to run the Dunnock end. Chair. Every return, your new magic is what the bridge needs next: ice road timber, stone for piers, coils for the crane, all fed with your spells at devices. Their end grows too, and it is better made. Bet comes back casting off-hand, relying on Shield and Sigil, and is harder to beat than before.

**The late-game turn: the peak at Twice.** Bet is waiting at the top. No chalk, no audience. It is the game's hardest spar, with every trick from the copybook coming back at you. Retry as often as you need. When you finally take it she laughs until she has to sit down, and says that was what she'd been waiting for. She teaches you the wider Shield arts as a parting present. A ship leaves for a posting eleven weeks away, and she had only held off accepting until someone beat her properly and meant it. You got what you wanted. It is why she is going. Last chair.

## The ending

Autumn. You return from the Rucks to Longlegs for the last span. Everyone has come for it: Hob with bread, Ines with a rib count, Dov at his end, Bet for one day before her boat. They are on the field from the first minute, each holding their own stretch. No one arrives late and no one covers for you. The autumn surge comes down the fen with everything that lives in it, and the fight is a party, the widest friendly fire of the game, every familiar spell in the air at once.

The surge passes and the two halves of the bridge meet a yard out of true, because each side measured its own way. Everyone looks at it for a long time. The brother and sister each name the kink, using different words. Someone fetches a plank.

Supper is one table laid across the join, every chair full, for the first time since Windfall.

In the morning the chairs are stacked and they have all gone. You walk the bridge alone with nothing left to do. On the plank, in chalk, in a left-handed scrawl: a five-rune queue you have never seen and two words, *Your go.*

You may stand there as long as you like. It is the game's last input. You queue it and cast it, and it picks you up and throws you off the bridge into the river.

**Last image:** you, surfacing under the crooked join, people crossing overhead with baskets, and on the underside of the plank, where only someone in the water could read it, *That's one to me.*

After the credits the world stays open. Every Dunnock but one is at their post, and they keep getting better. Bet's lines on the slates never change again, so they can be beaten for good.

## The recurring faces

- **Bet Oake.** Grins hardest when losing. Wants to be beaten properly, once, by someone who means it.
- **Hob Tansley.** Apologises while casting. Wants to be asked to stay somewhere.
- **Ines Quarrie.** Studies your last fight and brings the answer. Wants a problem she can't solve.
- **Little Dov Ampleforth.** Enormous, afraid of water. Wants to see his name on something that lasts.
- **Captain Sloe.** Steers with her foot and reads. Wants to know how her novel ends.
- **Mr Sedge,** your teacher. Plays draughts by post across a harbour he could row in ten minutes. Wants one win before he retires.
- **Nan and Gaffer Cobbold** of Longlegs. Will not use the other's word for anything. Each wants the last one.

## Why this is fun to play

You lose to someone casting the same keys you have, then open your copybook and try it yourself. Every old region holds a chalked number you can now beat with magic you didn't have, so backtracking feels like a rematch. In joint fights you are aiming through friends who are also aiming through you, and half of what you learn is where to stand. Late on, you and the Dunnocks fill the screen with structures and bolts, and you can tell who cast what because you know how each of them plays.

## The biggest weakness

The whole thing depends on players liking four people who get one line per supper. If the barks, impressions and spar personalities are thin, the empty chairs will mean nothing and the ending will be a neat trick played on a stranger. The Dunnocks' spell AI also has to be good enough to surprise a skilled player with real queues. That is an expensive promise, and the premise has no fallback if it fails, because there is no doom to carry the game instead.
