Your worry about the etched focus: what happens when Sigil is queued but you make a cast other than Conjure? Four ways it could read:

S1

Sigil means a weapon

Until the art is learned, any cast with Sigil queued conjures the weapon, whatever the gesture.

Easiest to parse ("Sigil is a blade"), but the rule changes later: Force with Sigil becomes a turret.

S2

It fizzles

The cast fails with a dull spark and a "the focus only shapes blades" cue; the runes are refunded.

Honest and hints at more, but a failed cast mid-fight feels bad.

S3

Sigil drops out

The rest of the queue casts as if Sigil weren't there; the Sigil rune is refunded.

Smooth, but you get something you didn't ask for without being told why.

S4 · recommended

Locked gestures, with a cue

With Sigil queued, the locked gestures spend nothing and play a soft "the focus can't" spark; the help panel draws them dim.

Never casts something unasked for, and it's the same rule the full art later lifts. The risk: a press that does nothing can feel like a dropped input.

What pools of one mean. Every early spell is a mix, never a stack: Fire + Fire is impossible, Fire + Water + Earth isn't. A five-rune spell is reachable on day one, but only with five different elements, and it empties all five. One weapon and one ward at a time. The first deeper pool is the first time a 2-Fire spray exists, which makes the pool choice at an attunement feel big. Regen sets the whole early pace; about half today's (0.5 runes a second) is a starting guess for a play-test.

The world

Fields shape where people live

Seven elements run through the land as fields and veins. People settle where a field helps them, the way towns grow along rivers. Where two fields meet, the spell rules for that pair show in the landscape, so a player who knows the magic can read the map. A sketch of one possible stretch of the world:

Elements that cancel in a spell can still share ground, case by case: lightning isn't persistent, so storms over a marsh are natural, while fire and frost stay apart. Shield and Sigil aren't on the map: they belong to the caster, so no zone touches them.

Fields, veins and wells

Both are real, at different scales. A field is broad and ambient, like rainfall: its strength is what a zone's band measures. Veins are narrow channels inside and between fields, like rivers; in levels they show on the ground as seams, and devices sit on them (a forge on a fire vein). A well is where veins surface and converge, which is why a region's push peaks there.

Zones

A region pushes harder the deeper you go

Total regen stays the same everywhere. A zone only changes how the shared budget is split: the local element gets a bigger share, its opposite a smaller one. You're never weaker, only steered. The quarter of regen that always reaches every pool still holds, so nothing stops refilling.

The fairness loop: the region's enemies attack mostly with its element, and every guard, ward and heal carrying an element lends resistance to it. So the abundant local element is also your cheapest defence against the local threat. In a fire region you have fire to burn with and fire to ward against fire.

Example for a fire region: Fire is local, Cold its opposite. The multipliers are placeholders for a play-test.

Unlock pacing

When the "press anything" moment comes

The five-rune queue is there from the first hour (your round-2 answer). A big mixed spell is possible on day one; it just empties you and takes a long time to repeat. So the two levers are pool size (burst: how many big spells before you wait) and regen (sustain: how fast you're back).

Early swords without early Sigil: the academy's etched focus. Students get a focus with the binding already etched in: you pour an element in and the focus shapes the blade or the guard. In the rules, Sigil queues normally but only works with Conjure, and each weapon still binds one rune from a small Sigil pool. Later you learn the innate art for real and shape without the etching, which opens turrets, glyphs, orbitals, Blink, auras and the Double: the rune-hungry part, when your regen can carry it. Locked casts show as dim slots from the start, so the rune reads as growing.

The even count: Air, late. It already has a draft, gives Earth an opposite (five clean pairs on the keys), and its shove and carry are a mechanic no other element has. The cost: it must be balanced against nine elements players know, and the draft's changes to Water and the Shield wave would be in from the start.

Practice

The meditation dream

Your idea: sit down anywhere, enter a dream sandbox, experiment, and wake up. It's the Sanctum made reachable from anywhere, and the Sanctum room in the game already has dummies and the tuning panel (readouts, spawning, time control).

  • Entering: a key or menu item; your body stays where it was, in a meditation pose. Pick: only when no enemy is engaged, so it can't dodge a fight. Solo, the world pauses; online it keeps running, and a hit or an enemy noticing you wakes you.
  • Joining: interact with a meditating ally to join their dream; leave with a button at any time.
  • Tech: under rollback every peer runs the whole world, so the dream is a room in the same world, off the map. No new netcode.
  • What it holds: your current unlocks only, dummies with damage and status readouts, a summoner for any enemy you've met, reset, time control.
  • Hub practice yards are flavour on top (the academy's training yard, a city's duelling ground).
Shrines

Optional side challenges

Shrines aren't the practice answer (you corrected that in round 2); they're optional challenges off the main path. One to three rooms, three to eight minutes, one idea. Your picks were the puzzle room, the constraint trial and the keeping trial. Their rules:

  • The room states the problem, not text.
  • Only the spell language. No new controls; any device takes spells as input.
  • Solvable alone, richer together. Co-op can split the work; fights scale to the party.
  • More than one answer where possible: steam or freeze-and-shatter both open the frozen door.
  • The entrance shows the reward, so the detour is a choice. Once solved, it stays lit on the map.

Story

For one wizard or four

Many characters, simply met. Recurring faces with one trait and one want (a trader in every hub, a cartographer, a rival cohort); locals with a request, a rumour or a reaction; a mentor who writes or meets you at wells; an antagonist seen more than heard. The world changing visibly between visits does the work dialogue would.

The party is a cohort. The academy sends journeymen out together; solo, you're the one sent. Every line is written to "you", which in English doesn't say how many. No beat needs a particular number, and no puzzle requires two people. The alternative, one named hero with partners as unnamed companions, is simpler to write but makes partners second-class.

Growth

Carried from round 1

After the unlocks comes the capitalizing phase: more runes and more regen turn one- and two-element casts and melee into a full kit. How far it scales, and whether it needs artifacts or more workings, is for play-testing.
