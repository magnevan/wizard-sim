The principle

A player who has to show its hand

An enemy wizard plays by your rules: runes from pools on the shared regen, the same wind-up and recovery, friendly fire, wet, burning and chill. Its fairness comes from three things you can see. It never reads your keys; it sees the world through a reaction delay, and only what a co-op partner could see.

1

The queue is the tell

Its runes gather at its staff one at a time, as yours do, slower than a skilled player types. Water, Water… you know what's next.

2

The commit

Spells of four or more runes add a focus glow before the wind-up. Once it glows, it's committed. Shield, Blink or stun it.

3

The recovery

Every cast ends in a breath, twice as long after a big one. That's your opening.

Plays

A play is one cast or a short named string of casts. The AI scores and picks plays, not single spells, so they read as intentions you learn to name. Each wizard carries a few. They use the same grammar and casts as yours.

Tactics, kept cheap

How it moves and thinks

  • A range band by style: the duelist keeps 6 to 9 u, the brawler 3 to 5, the summoner stays behind its allies. It strafes inside the band and backs off when you close.
  • Cover while it recovers: a pillar, a wall or its own allies between you.
  • A counter table by tier: an apprentice knows nothing; an adept knows wet → lightning, burning → douse itself, your ward → switch; a master also knows freeze and shatter, cancels and baiting a puddle.
  • Memory that can be wrong: it remembers your ward and your favourite element for a few seconds. Change your ward and it's briefly wrong. Bungie built Halo's surprises on this.
  • Friendly fire: it soaks itself in its own Rain, burns in its own patch and hits its allies. Apprentices never check for allies, adepts half the time, masters always.

Encounters

Where they show up

  • Hedge wizards in a crowd (apprentice): a goblin caster among goblins, with two plays, often hitting its own side. The common case and the first to build.
  • The duel (adept, alone, in a ring or courtyard): a story beat. The opponent is yours to write: a rival, an examiner, anyone.
  • A coven (two or three adepts): they share one set-up and payoff token, so one wets and the other shocks, never both at once.
  • A boss (master, phases, adds): one new play per phase, with adds that pressure you without crowding the read.

It's the same brain as the companions proposed on the Enemy Roster, with the team flipped and different weights. Building one gives you most of the other.

What it costs to build

Casting state is per seat today: four seats hold the runes, pools and cast phase. AI seats (recommended) add seats 4 to 7 for casters driven inside the sim by an input bot, so every rule works unchanged and the queue shows at the staff for free. The cost is that the per-seat arrays double; the pathfinding fields can stay at four. A caster component on enemy bodies is cheaper per enemy but forks the casting code. Either one waits for the engine rework's phase 2.
