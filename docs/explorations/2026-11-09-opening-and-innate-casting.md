# The opening, and casting as something new (2026-11-09)

The user's brief for the opening, and Claude's critique of its twist, as asked for ("be critical, I need the feedback"). Nothing here is decided. Approved story lives in `../campaign/canon.md`, the process in `../campaign/README.md`, the draft of the segment in `../campaign/01-opening/brief.md`.

## The user's brief

- The academy is an analogue to an engineering school, because the world's technology runs on the fields.
- The instability is shifting veins within regions: wells run dry and defences fall, wells overcharge, new territories become magical and get fought over. Wildlife and monsters are affected.
- As a hook, the wizard or group is sent out to fix a reported issue.
- **The twist:** spellcasting in this form hasn't really existed. Wizards use structures that tap fields. The instability is letting them draw on fields innately. It would explain why you start with so little and grow quickly, why trained wizards are so clumsy, and why the early game is melee: fighting without magic and infusing weapons is the old world.
- A crash course in fighting before you're sent out, because (1) the world is more dangerous and/or (2) the academy can't afford escorts. Room for humour.
- **Open:** how to run a tutorial for something the academy never taught ("you might build an arcane turret, but not cast an arcane beam yourself"), and when the first spellcasting comes.
- **Also:** a structure for making and refining campaign segments.

## Verdict

Keep it. It's the strongest story idea so far, because it answers the gap the user found in "What He Had on Him": the connection to you as a wizard. The disaster and your power become the same thing, so each job brings growth for a reason and no circuit is needed. It also fits regions that bend your regen: you get more of what's loose around you.

What it does well:
- Your character's learning curve and the player's are the same curve.
- Pillar 2 becomes literal. Nobody can tell you what a mix does, because nobody has done it.
- The meditation dream gets a reason: it's how you practise something no one can teach.
- The academy's Arcane vein gets a job. Students spent years sitting over the deep field, so they go first. This also limits who else gets it: people who've lived on strong veins, like well-keepers, foundry crews and garrisons. That gives enemy casters a logic.
- "All the core elements at once" works because the deep field lies under the others (`world/elements.md`).

## Problems

**1. Your own example inverts the unlock order.** If academy wizards build arcane turrets, turrets are the familiar part and beams the alien part. But the game holds turrets for mid-game and gives beams early. *Proposed fix:* old-world structures take a workshop, a week and a vein. Standing up a turret in four seconds from nothing is the new thing, and the last to arrive, because it needs both your old training and your new ability. Then the Sigil midpoint is where your engineering finally pays off. This improves the beat, but it has to be said in the story or players will notice.

**2. A spellcasting game that withholds spellcasting.** This is the biggest practical risk. People come for magic, and friends invited to co-op judge it in the first few minutes. Melee here is deliberately the cheap option, and nobody knows yet whether it carries the first 20 minutes. The fix is getting hands on runes almost at once, through a device (below). *Test before committing:* play a few minutes of the Arena with no runes.

**3. Fixing the veins may end your power.** Players will ask this as soon as they see the link. Each job restores the thing whose breaking made you strong. This doesn't need answering yet, but it can't be ignored. It's an opportunity: it gives the ending stakes without needing a villain, adds a fourth theory people can argue about (it's awfully convenient for wizards), and suits the case left open on the cause.

**4. The engineer reversal.** Earlier today the user found "wizard engineer" funny and it was logged that the player isn't one. *Proposed reading:* it's your training, not your gameplay. Pillar 6 rules out engineering minigames, so "fixing" means fighting at the site and feeding devices with spells. The gap between what you trained for and what you're doing is the joke early on. Later, the story has to reckon with what you've become.

**5. Fantasy, or magitech.** "Engineering school" invites lab coats, against pillar 5. *Proposed:* the register of a mason's lodge or guild of cathedral builders, with benches, plumb lines and apprentices, rather than a university lab.

**6. What the lore doesn't excuse.** "They're clumsy" explains friendly fire in hour one, not hour twenty, when you're supposed to be good. It also can't be a reason to keep real misclicks: pillar 3 still wants deliberate ways to avoid hitting friends, and pillar 4 wants readable casting.

**7. Tone.** The user rejected "Your Go" for goofiness, and "engineers drafted as soldiers" can slide the same way. Keep the absurdity in the situation and the institution, with everyone playing it straight: a safety notice, a form, an instructor who's a retired porter. Of the two reasons for the crash course, **the escort budget is the funnier and stronger one**. Danger alone makes sending students look reckless. Together they work: every guard is at a failing wall, so there's nobody left to walk you there.

**8. Things already written that it disturbs.** The "why you" sketch, the words "innate" and the Arcane vein in `world/elements.md`, the academy-as-classes idea from 2026-11-06, and the training grounds' element yards and rune lessons.

## Teaching what the academy never taught

The key: **what the academy teaches is field theory, and the spell grammar is that theory.** Students already know that opposites cancel and the strongest element sets the form, because they design structures that way. So teaching needs no innate casting.

| Option | How it works | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **A. The test bench** (pick) | You operate a training rig with the normal rune keys and cast button, and the rig fires. The hands learn the real inputs, so the later reveal needs no new teaching | Needs a bench device. Must stay limited, or the reveal is hollow |
| B. No lessons before; learn in the field | Everything is taught after the first cast, as small problems | Keeps the reveal pure, but no magic input for 20 minutes |
| C. Someone a week ahead of you | A hurriedly written manual, with errata arriving by post, region after region | Great voice and a recurring tutorial channel, but it needs a writer's touch. Works best after the reveal |

**Pick: A before the reveal, C after it, and the dream as the lab.** The bench is limited to two elements and a mix. The errata are the existing idea of someone who writes to you, used for teaching.

**The focus survives.** The etched focus has to last until the Sigil art, so what dies at the reveal is the *emplacement* at the job site, not the focus.

## When the first cast comes

| Option | When | For | Against |
|---|---|---|---|
| In the academy, during the crash course | ~10 min | Magic soonest | Spends the moment on a training yard; the academy would then know and could teach you |
| **At the first job, when the device dies** (pick) | ~20 min | The structure failing *is* the world's problem. The keys are already habit, so pressing them is your own instinct | 20 minutes needs to hold up; hence the bench |
| At the end of the first job | ~40 min | The biggest build-up | Too long for a spellcasting game |

Everyone in the cohort gets it at the same moment, so it works for one or four.

## Round 2: the user's answers (2026-11-09)

- **The source of "the connection to you as a wizard":** the user's verdict on one candidate, "What He Had on Him" ("I feel like it doesn't quite have the connection to you as a wizard"). It was Claude who generalized it into a requirement for the story, in the decisions log ("The story needs that connection"). The user didn't say that.
- **The Arcane vein link:** liked ("I hadn't thought about that").
- **Problem 1, turrets:** agreed, and it was the user's reasoning too. "Spawning instantly, and you being the 'well' that powers it is fundamentally very different."
- **Problem 2, withholding magic:** agreed. "I don't think the melee play in itself is that fun," so powers unlock quite quickly, and it's "basically the tutorial segment until you have the base set of runes unlocked."
- **Problem 3 was wrong.** You don't fix the veins, only the infrastructure: "An earthquake has broke a dam, and we need to build a new one where the river now goes." Fixing things doesn't threaten your power, so this doesn't apply.
- **Problem 4:** the gameplay isn't engineering, "even if the wizard technically is an engineer".
- **Problem 5 was wrong too.** "Your clumsyness goes down as you get better at playing the game, the learning curve makes sense. Both you and the in-game wizard is new to spellcasting."
- **Problem 6, tone:** "Sure... I guess." Not pressed further.
- **Q1 taken** (the event is why you can now cast; engineering is job, not gameplay). **Q2** "maybe yes, we can try it out". **Q3** "not really sure, maybe we can try a few", with an outline: (a) a presentation or meeting on the strange reports and the overload of jobs, and that you need fighting training ("but don't worry you won't need it wink wink"); (b) a basic control tutorial and fighting; (c) a first job at a misbehaving defence near the academy. Static defences you can control, one element (probably Fire): the camera focuses on it and you cast from its perspective, learning the casting modes. It breaks down or runs dry, and you feel an infusion. It might prompt you to conjure a fire blade and go out, and you get the Fire rune. Repeating this for more elements "may be a bit repetitive".

**Claude's notes on the outline:**
- Controlling the defence replaces the test bench and is better: it teaches every cast from the first job, and no fiction is needed, because operating defences is your job.
- It leaves no road beat, so your own first cast moves up to about 10 minutes.
- The rune bar can tell the story. The defence's deep pool drains in plain sight, it goes dark, and one pip of your own lights up. The contrast also shows how little you have.
- A rule the blade needs: Fire plus the conjure gesture is today's imbued burst, and a blade takes Sigil. Proposal: the rack's focus in the crash course is charged from the academy's supply, the old world's way. After the infusion you charge it yourself: you are the well.
- Repetition: one, two, then the rest (`../campaign/01-opening/brief.md`), and the user wants to try versions.
