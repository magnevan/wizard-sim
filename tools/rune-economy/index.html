Rune Economy

Today every element refills on its own at 0.22 runes a second. A Fire-only wizard sustains 0.22 runes/s, and a wizard who rotates through four elements sustains 0.88, so the resource itself pushes everyone to diversify. The proposal in docs/explorations/2026-11-05-rune-economy.md splits the jobs: max per element is depth and burst, and one total regen rate R is sustain, shared out by a weight α.

The simulator below runs four scripted wizards through the same fight. Each one casts its play style’s 3-rune spells the moment it can, so the chart shows how many runes each style gets out under the rules you set. Dashed lines are today as built, for comparison. Then answer the four questions at the bottom. A question you leave blank means you accept Claude’s recommendation, which is marked on each one.

The rule in the proposal

Each element that isn’t full gets

regen_e = α · R · max_e / Σmax_all          (floor)
        + (1−α) · R · max_e / Σmax_notfull  (shared part)
  • α = 1: per element, proportional to max. Every pool refills in the same time, and diversity multiplies throughput, as it does today.
  • α = 0: one shared budget. Throughput is R whatever you cast; a Fire-only wizard pours all of it into Fire.
  • α ≈ 0.25 (recommended): rotating pays a little without starving a specialist. At R = 1 and pools of 3, a Fire-only wizard sustains about 0.78 runes/s of Fire, not 0.22.

What it costs

  • The coupling is invisible. How fast Fire refills depends on whether your Cold pool is full. The HUD has to show it, for example pools that shimmer brighter the bigger their share (the rate column in the simulator).
  • A specialist’s rhythm changes from burst-then-wait to a steady stream. Fire-heavy enemies and resistances have to carry the counterplay.
  • Attune and Siphon multiply R instead of every element’s regen, which is simpler.
  • Starting at 3 per element means no 4- or 5-stacks of one element early. Drag the pool slider to 2 and the Fire specialist can’t cast at all: max caps depth as well as burst.
