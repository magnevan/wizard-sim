# Your Go

Two tiny schools joined by one old timber bridge send their journeymen round the wells in the same summer, and you spend it trying to beat four people who are cleverer than you, funnier than you, and all going to leave.

## The question

**Are they better than you?** You'll ask it on the very first afternoon, when a total stranger flattens you with a spell you could have cast yourself, and from then on it's chalked inside the front cover of your copybook, in her handwriting: *Bet 1, You 0*. That score never resets and it counts every try. Win a spar early, and you can, and she'll write it down with an asterisk ("I was eating", "you didn't mean it"). The answer, I'm so sorry and so happy to tell you, is yes, by about fifty to one with a great many asterisks, and the one clean win is the best day of your life and also the reason the summer ends.

**What drags you through a forty-hour game when the world is completely fine:**
- **The slates.** Each cohort chalks its names, its finishing queue, and whatever that town actually cares about (goose health, cracks, friends struck), one line per party size. A re-sit is next week's job on the same ground, and the Dunnocks keep re-sitting behind your back, even after they've stopped travelling: Hob's new scores arrive by post. Bet will retake a line the day after you beat it, and Ines goes over your chalk in tidier handwriting, in front of everyone, in blue.
- **The copybook.** Any queue that touches you, anywhere, friend or foe, writes itself in, so every loss teaches you one more of her tricks and anyone can get there in the end.
- **The table.** Each region ends at one long supper, one line each, loser lays it, and somebody does your walk, your wince and your exact face the last time you set fire to yourself. There's a chair fewer as the summer goes on.
- **The letter.** Captain Sloe has been carrying one for Bet since the first week, and every port she holds it out, and every port Bet says "not yet" and comes to find you for another spar.

## The opening

Last-Day Fête at Pennick, on the ochre peninsula in the northern bay, over an Arcane vein, with the Dunnock Point school across the strait at the far end of the Long Walk, a three-hundred-year-old street of timber houses on piles. Every stall is an element with one rune each: freeze the duck pond, soak the mayor without soaking the queue, and Mr Sedge etches your focus so you can conjure your very first blade. Any stall can be skipped. At the coconut shy Bet casts one colossal five-rune spell, obliterates the shy, and stands there grinning for one entire minute while her runes come back, and you decide you're going to get her. There's only one ferry out, so off you all go together.

## The road

The route round the sea is fixed, with side paths free on every return, and after midsummer every leg runs back through the harbour. Each attunement steps your regen and deepens the pool you pick, each re-sit you win deepens one more, and attuning means working a place until your casting picks up its accent, on a job set by the locals.

The spar rule: as many Dunnocks step up as there are of you, and if they run short, what they've built before you arrived makes up the difference, more turrets, more glyphs, never more health. In joint fights they rotate in by the wave, so you always have someone to hit by accident and the screen stays readable.

### 1. Saltcrown (north-centre mesas, Earth)
- **The place:** a fortress so old and rich that its ramparts have ramparts, sitting on a red mesa you can see for two days before you reach it.
- **The trouble:** scarp bulls have discovered that the mortar is salty. They are licking the fortress. A duke's east wing is noticeably thinner and he's in it.
- **Asks and gains:** hold the salt gate with conjured rock walls, your first new verb, and the slate counts stones licked.
- **Encounters:** defence, herds, native boss, first spars.
- **The beat:** the Dunnocks have already passed the exam, by accident, looking for lunch, and their line is outrageously good.
- **On return:** the herd has told its friends, and Lightning opens the sealed quarry.

### 2. The great tree (south-west, Life)
- **The place:** the tree is so enormous that noon comes to the village of Windfall for twenty minutes through one gap in the leaves, and everyone stops work and goes and stands in it. They live on what drops and legislate who owns a bough while it's still in the air.
- **The trouble:** a bumper year, and boars are eating the festival.
- **Asks and gains:** escort the prize cart and its hateful goose through the woods alive, and the slate counts goose. Wards on an ally come from Hob, who walks beside you healing you through your own fire and apologising to you for the fire.
- **Encounters:** escort, swarms, a keeping shrine.
- **On return:** Hob's oven with a queue round the root, and a spar against months of strange Life spells.

### 3. The Stirrup and Cullet (south-west badlands into the volcanic south, Fire)
- **The place:** the great rock arch where both cohorts camp, and then the mountain, which spits molten glass that cools in the air and comes down at dawn as bubbles over a black land split with fire, and the children of Cullet run out with nets.
- **The trouble:** Cullet has staked everything on the largest pane of glass ever made, and it must go down the lava road on a cart.
- **Asks and gains:** an escort where your own rocks and shoves break the thing as fast as anything else, so you learn the parry from Bet, batting fire away from a window while she criticises your elbow. The slate counts cracks.
- **Encounters:** the first joint defence at the arch, friendly fire in both directions, then ambushes. The black spires hold constraint shrines.
- **The beat:** supper under the arch, where Hob, apologising, announces he's been asked to stay at Windfall and everyone screams with joy. First empty chair. Next morning Dov plants a turret and walks away from it, it shoots you in the back, and you have never wanted anything so much.
- **On return:** one tiny pane in Cullet has your names in it, slightly cracked.

### 4. Longlegs (north-east fen, across the sea on Sloe's ferry, Water)
- **The place:** a town on stilts in a storm marsh, washing strung between chimneys, and after every storm the marsh gas lights itself and walks about on the water at night in tall blue flames.
- **The trouble:** the flames have started drifting in under the boards.
- **Asks and gains:** the Douse Round, running the walkways putting out fire with Water, and the slate counts boards scorched.
- **Encounters:** ambushes, defence, amphibians, puzzle rooms in the sunk houses.
- **The Sigil art opens here.** A storm pins everyone in a sail loft for three short, skippable days. Dov's queue sits greyed in your copybook and your etched focus can't do it, so Dov takes the focus, Mr Sedge's own work, walks out to the rail, and you hear the splash. You will be furious. You try the queue at the dummies, it fizzles, it fizzles, and on the third morning the cast you press works and a blade stands in the air by the window with no hand on it. You could only shape what you hold; now you shape the world around you. Opening the art grants Sigil depth outright. Your first turret takes out the last chapter of Captain Sloe's novel.
- **On return:** next week's flames, and Longlegs has hung your focus's empty case in the loft as a warning.

### 5. The Ribs (north, Cold)
- **The place:** the ribs stand out of the ice taller than any tower you've seen, clouds snag on them, and you will not want to be among them at night. The monks of the Copying House live in the skull, transcribing every well's slate, and their oldest book records it plainly: it came up out of the north, lay down here, took nine winters to die, and the first monks sat with it in shifts.
- **The trouble:** armoured things are walking in from everywhere to lie down among the ribs, straight over the sledge line, and they don't stop for sledges.
- **Asks and gains:** freeze a lead of open sea and run the winter stores round them, past the largest, whom the monks call the Prioress. It's the same ice that brings timber home for the bridge. The monks, you discover, have started a rota for the armoured things too.
- **Encounters:** gauntlet, caravan, boss, traversal over water you froze.
- **The beat:** in the copied slates are two hundred years of paired cohorts, and there's Mr Sedge's name beside Mrs Ruddock's, the Dunnock teacher. Then the gossip: Ines says she's here to count the ribs, which won't come out even, but it's the bolt she's been working on, and she has it, and Bet tells her to leave it alone, and you hear a Dunnock shout for the first time. In the morning her chair is empty and nobody does your walk.
- **On return:** she never looks at you. Her spars open on the answer to whatever element you've cast most. The shelves hold a copy of Sloe's novel.

### 6. Twice (north-west island, Lightning)
- **The place:** the blue crystal peak rings like a struck glass with every bolt, and each house in the lamp-makers' town proudly paints its hits on the wall.
- **The trouble:** one great double strike each year fills every lamp on the island for winter, and someone must be standing on the catch-stone for the second bolt.
- **Asks and gains:** climb through swarms that chain, with lamp-carriers roped along who count as friends, and charge the coil line with your own spells. The slate counts friends struck. Bet's line is a zero. Blink, for the ledges.
- **Encounters:** gauntlet, chain swarms, coils, and the top.
- **On return:** the lamps are lit from what you caught, and they're very proud of you and a little scorched.

### 7. The Rucks (east, Arcane)
You go alone now, and I'll keep this short.
- **The place:** purple hills in a different order each morning, stars out at noon, and the tower floating above it all, its great east window missing for years and things coming in through the hole at night. Mrs Aumbry has held that room with a curtain and a terrible temper. Catch one of the things and it's small, and lost.
- **Asks and gains:** the Cullet pane arrives and you fit it through a night of intruders. Attune becomes how you fight here, flooding your pools between waves. The largest regen step. If a tenth element comes in, Air blows through that hole.
- **Encounters:** intruder defence, reshuffled rooms, strange lone creatures.
- **The exam:** Arcane draws out what's already in a caster, so the well room fills with Bet and Hob and Ines and Dov, drawn out of your copybook, as many at once as there are of you, the rest waiting their turn, casting everything they ever hit you with. They're exactly as good as you remember. For the first time all summer, none of them surprises you.
- **The beat:** it's the only slate with no line above yours. You chalk it. Then it's supper, and the loser lays the table, so you lay it, and you sit down.
- **On return:** the window holds, and Mrs Aumbry complains about the view.

## The turns

**The midpoint: home for midsummer.** Tradition brings every journeyman home halfway, and this year Nan Cobbold of Pennick and her brother Gaffer of Dunnock Point each decide the great harbour bell is their bank's for midsummer, so each sets their own school to moving it, three tons of bronze shoved up and down the Long Walk by spells, your whole street watching from the upstairs windows and cheering, everyone conducting into everyone, turrets on the rooftops, until the tar store catches. Every stray spell damages the Walk and the town can name who did the last of it. The Pargeters' house, nearest the tar, is gone before anyone has stopped fighting. The bell goes through the deck into the harbour, and a bolt through wet boards burns Bet's casting hand. It's the last lightning that truly crossed her, yours or Ines's, and she won't let anyone look.

The slate is that night: put it out, in three lulls, each one teaching a turret a new payload to carry Water. Both schools chalk one shared line, houses saved, and it can never be retaken. No supper.

**After it,** each bank trusts only its own school, so each builds out from its own shore, and Dov, frightened of water, stays to run the Dunnock end. Chair. Last Ferry, the night the boats stop for autumn, is chalked on the first pile (Gaffer has it as Boats-In), and that's your clock. Out in the harbour at night you can hear the bell tolling, somewhere new every visit, because something has climbed inside it and is wearing it. Each homecoming is a defence on the newest span, each span needs one new art (glyphs, orbitals, auras) and teaches it by needing it, devices you leave standing release their binding when you sail, and Dov's end is measured against yours and chalked. On the last of those visits you find he'd never have gone near that rail. It was a spoon. Your focus is pinning his handrail, next to his name, and he won't give it back. Bet returns casting off-hand, all Shield and Sigil, and she's better than before.

**The late-game turn: the peak at Twice.** Bet's waiting on the catch-stone, the whole mountain prepared, and the first bolt lands as you start, so the spar is simply who's standing there for the second, in front of the lamp-carriers and an island holding its breath, and every try goes in the cover. Halfway through she conjures a second self to be her right hand and you nearly cry, and it goes in your copybook because it touched you. Then you take the stone, the second bolt comes down through you, and every lamp on the island goes on at once, far below. No asterisk. You meant it: you took something she wanted. She laughs till she has to sit down, walks to the harbour and takes the letter, a posting a whole season's sail away that she'd said she would answer when somebody beat her. Every time you lost, you kept her. Last chair.

## The ending

You come home from the Rucks to Mr Sedge on the Pennick shore with a draughts board and his first win in half a century, radiant, and Mrs Ruddock, who had it in three, studying the sky. Bet never let anyone do that for her. Nan and Gaffer, in different words, ask you to stay.

Last Ferry. Hob's brought bread, Ines sits at the far end, Dov's on his handrail, Bet has one day before her boat, and they're on the bridge with you from the first minute, all in each other's way on one deck, none covering for you. The thing comes up out of the harbour tolling, and it's a party, every turret and bolt you've ever been hit with going off around your ears, until the bell is off its back and it bolts for open water, pink and outraged.

Then the two halves of the bridge meet a yard out of true, because each side measured its own way, and the loser lays the table, so you lay it, across the gap, because there's nowhere else, and it's the last span. Every Dunnock's chair is full. The Pargeters' plot still has a rope round it, and their chair is laid and empty. Ines, not looking up, folds a page into your copybook marked *if you want it*: a queue, no name, the true one.

In the morning the chairs are stacked and they have all gone. You walk the bridge alone with nothing left to do. On the table, in chalk, in a left-handed scrawl: a five-rune queue you have never seen and two words, *Your go.*

You may stand there as long as you like. If you can read runes, you know exactly what it does. It is the game's last input. You queue it and cast it, and it picks you up and throws you off the bridge into the harbour, and it writes itself into the copybook, last entry.

**Last image:** you, surfacing under the crooked join, people crossing overhead with baskets, and on the underside of the table, where only someone in the water could read it, *That's one to me.*

After the credits the world stays open, the room at the join has a lamp in it, and every Dunnock but one is at their post getting better. Bet's lines on the slates never change again, so they can be beaten for good.

## The recurring faces

- **Bet Oake.** Grins hardest when losing. Wants to be beaten properly, once.
- **Hob Tansley.** Apologises while casting. Wants to be asked to stay somewhere.
- **Ines Quarrie.** Studies your last fight and brings the answer. Wants a problem she can't solve.
- **Little Dov Ampleforth.** Enormous, afraid of water. Wants his name on something that lasts.
- **Captain Sloe.** Steers with her foot and reads. Wants to know how her novel ends.
- **Mr Sedge and Mrs Ruddock.** Draughts by post across a bridge they could walk in ten minutes. He wants one win. She wants the game to go on.
- **Nan and Gaffer Cobbold.** Won't use each other's word for anything. Each wants the last one.
- **The Pargeters.** A rope round a bare plot. They want it left alone.

## Why this is fun to play

You lose to someone pressing the same keys you have, and then the spell is in your book and you're trying it on a duck. Every old region holds a number somebody keeps lowering. In joint fights you aim through friends who aim through you, and half of what you learn is where to stand. The Dunnocks are four authored repertoires that grow each region and pick by simple rules, so you come to know who cast what from its shape.

## The biggest weakness

Players have to adore four people who get one line per supper, and with no doom to fall back on, thin mimicry and repertoires leave the ending a neat trick played on a stranger. The bell-wearing thing must also frighten in the dark for the whole back half, or the finale is a sketch.
