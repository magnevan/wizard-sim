# Flitting Year

**Pitch.** Every 211 years the world's fields all shove along one place around the sea, like people making room on a bench, and you graduate just in time to help carry the furniture.

## The question

**Will it all be across before its field goes?** You carry this from the first crate to the last barge. The ending answers it with a list: most of it, and here is exactly what made it. The list differs from party to party, because there were always more jobs than days.

**What everyone knows.** Six fields sit in seven seats around the sea. The empty seat has no field on it, so the deep Arcane lies bare there. In a Flit, the field beside the gap slides into it, then the next field into the place that one left, and so on round. A children's party game, Shove Along, teaches the order. It takes about a year, happens every 211, and is due now. The land follows its field within a lifetime, which is why the ice and the lava look permanent. There are cakes.

| Seat | Now | After the Flit |
|---|---|---|
| East, the long purple land | bare Arcane | Fire |
| South, the volcano | Fire | Life |
| South-west, the great tree and the arch | Life | Lightning |
| North-west, the crystal island | Lightning | Cold |
| The northern ice | Cold | Earth |
| The ochre mesas | Earth | Water |
| The stilt-town fen | Water | bare Arcane |

So the purple moves one seat at a time around the world, against the fields, and you stay one step ahead of it. Every region you leave changes behind you: old field, then a spell of bare Arcane, then a new field with new bands.

**What the circuit is for.** A wizard attuned to a well can read which way its field's front will run, and can turn that front a few yards by feeding the old sluice-stones at the well, the way you cut a ditch ahead of a flood. A few yards decides whether the granary goes under. Every cohort for 211 years has walked the circuit so that trained hands would exist when the year came. You drew the year. So did the cohorts either side of you, and you will see their sandbags.

**The calendar.** It sits on the map screen. It moves only when you take a dated job or sit a well's exam. Each leg has more jobs than days. A job you skip gets done worse by somebody else, and you will see that on your next visit. The host, or a quick vote, picks.

## The opening

The academy is the floating tower, moored over the bare Arcane in the east, where casters' innate gifts come easiest. It floats only on bare Arcane, so when the gap moves, the school must be towed after it. Your final week is the packing. Each last lesson is a porters' problem: clear the rune lab before the crates arrive, shift the practice dummies without setting fire to the porter. A crate from the northern monastery arrives marked KEEP COLD. It contains a person. You receive your etched focus (weapons and guards only), and graduation is cutting the first mooring line with the blade it conjures. Then the basket down, and the road south.

## The road

The order is the Flit's order, so it is fixed. The freedom is in which jobs you take and when you go back.

### 1. The South: Fire leaves first
- **Attuning asks:** hold the rim of the well through a lift, feeding the sluice-stones so the front runs seaward and misses the town.
- **You gain:** Fire pool deepens (or whichever you choose), first regen step.
- **Encounters:** fire-herds, quick and frantic, swarm the well to breed before it goes. Road ambushes. The exam is the first **lift fight**: over its last minute your regen slides from Fire to Arcane as the ground changes under you.
- **The beat:** the smiths of Scarrow have kept every anvil on skids for two centuries, and the skids have rotted. They are moving with their field to the east regardless, but the last casting, the great winch for the tower's tow, needs nine more days of heat than the calendar has. You are the heat: a crucible you feed with Fire between waves. The day the volcano goes out, four thousand people bring folding chairs and a brass band. It goes out on schedule, and they applaud.
- **Coming back:** first a purple crust with the tower moored over the dead crater. Later, green ash, and exactly as much Life as you carried here in the next leg. The black spires are the mooring stacks from the last Flit. Blink opens the way up.

### 2. The South-west: Life has to be carried
- **Attuning asks:** Life is the one field that goes only where living things take it. The exam is the send-off: keep the well's front open behind the last wagons.
- **You gain:** Life pool, regen.
- **Encounters:** the largest escort in the game, a migration of cattle, deer, beehives on poles and seed carts funnelling through the great arch toward the volcano. Predators work its flanks. Barley farmers who do not want two hundred thousand animals crossing their barley fight you with Earth and Life, in the same spell language you use.
- **The beat:** the tree cannot walk. Lightning is coming, and the tallest thing on the continent will be struck weekly for 211 years. Most people are grieving. Hesper Gaunt has wanted to run copper through its crown since she was nine, and this is the happiest year of her life. Town chooses: leave, or string the cable. Half do each.
- **Coming back:** the tree wired and lit, lamps on every limb, rods in the canopy, Lightning bands where you learned to heal. The forest is thinning on stored Life and has centuries left. Storm-charged coils open sealed roots.

### Midpoint: the Strait (see The turns)

### 3. The North-west island: Lightning, and someone with a plan
- **Attuning asks:** bleed the crystal peak's charge into the ground stone by stone while it tries to leave all at once.
- **You gain:** Lightning pool, regen, and now your Sigil art to do it with.
- **Encounters:** chaining swarms, then the coil-yard casters, who are very good and very frightened. Their turrets and yours trade fire over a yard full of copper.
- **The beat:** the island is the one town that decides to stay and keep its lamps. Ilse Penhallow has built a coil to hold a charge in the crystal through 211 years of Cold. The sums are wrong, and she knows they might be. When the lift comes the storm grounds through her own town. You save most of the street. The crystal does hold, for about one winter. She considers this a promising result.
- **Coming back:** Cold arrived, harbour frozen hard, a walkable ice road to the mainland where the ferries used to run. One lamp still lit for a while, then not.

### 4. The northern ice: Cold, and the stores of the world
- **Attuning asks:** chill the road behind you so it lasts until the final sledge passes. Attunement here is done on the move.
- **You gain:** Cold pool, regen, Shield's wider arts (see The turns).
- **Encounters:** convoy gauntlet across breaking ice, slow armoured things that have waited a long time and are annoyed, a puzzle-room shrine inside a melting storehouse.
- **The beat:** the monasteries hold the continent's seed reserves, records and two hundred years of cheese. All of it has to reach the island before the ice goes. The road runs under the colossal skeleton, a huge storm-steppe grazer from when this seat held Lightning. Its field left at the last Flit, it did not, and Cold arrived on top of it. Nobody worships it. Children get lifted up to touch a rib for luck, and the drivers call it "Late". It is the campaign's plainest picture of the stakes.
- **Coming back:** water running on bare rock, Earth arriving, the bones standing on dry stone. Grass by the postgame.

### 5. The ochre mesas: Earth and the shut gate
- **Attuning asks:** Earth moves once in a lifetime and hates it. Hold the sluice-stones through a slow earthquake while walking herds of stone-backed beasts go north through your fight.
- **You gain:** Earth pool, the largest regen step.
- **Encounters:** the procession of the herds as a native boss fight in stages, masons on the walls with Earth and Shield, a siege you are on the wrong side of.
- **The beat:** the mesa-fortress is a family town of wealthy, careful stonemasons who hold the only high road out of the fen. There is no army, only a great many locks. Water is coming to them and they have decided that a mesa is tall, which is true. They will not let forty thousand wet strangers through. You fight the gate's casters to a standstill and it changes nothing. Then Ottoline Brack walks up with a 211-year-old wager against this family, compounded (see The turns).
- **Coming back:** the first rain in living memory, gullies running, the fortress turning into an island with a harbour its builders never planned.

### 6. The fen: Water, last and shortest
- **Attuning asks:** everything at once. See The ending.
- **You gain:** Water pool, and the final regen step, partway through the last fight.
- **Encounters:** evacuation jobs with a calendar nearly used up, three defences, and the last lift.
- **The beat:** the fen draws the worst seat. Its town is forty thousand people on stilts, and bare Arcane is coming to stay. They have a good plan, and it needed sixty days. They have nineteen.
- **Coming back:** purple over the old piers, the tower moored there, and Perpetua Lunt, who stayed.

**Arcane** has no region. Its well is wherever the gap is, so its exam comes at the midpoint.

## The turns

**Midpoint: the Strait, and the Sigil art.** As the island's Lightning starts to loosen, a strip of bare Arcane opens across the water for nine days. The hop from east to south was short. This one is forty miles, and that window is the only way the tower can cross. It goes over towed by Scarrow's winch and every fishing boat in the south-west, with the whole world's eclipse-chasers rowing alongside. You are aboard when the gap overtakes you. For the first time you are standing in bare Arcane, which draws out a caster's innate gifts, as everyone was taught. A line parts, the tower swings, and the blade on your focus is no use against a sea. The art opens: you could only shape what you held, and now you set turrets on the boat decks, glyphs on the tow-lines, and Blink between hulls. You attune to Arcane here, in the worst possible place, with the head porter shouting. Friendly fire at this moment is extremely funny and costs a boat.

**Late turn: the far end of the bench.** On the ice road, Cold lifts eleven days early. Every child who has played Shove Along knows that the far end of the bench gets shoved hardest. Nobody put that in the almanac's sum. The last lifts come closer and closer together, and the convoy that had weeks has hours. Nobody did anything wrong. Every town's plan is now short, and the calendar's legs visibly shrink on the map screen. Ottoline Brack, unfrozen for this, is delighted. She bet the Carrow family of the mesas that the almanac would run early, then had herself put on ice to collect. On that road, with the ice going, she also teaches you the old ward-work that fell out of fashion, and Shield's wider arts open: you learn to hold a piece of the world still while the sledges go over it. If Air comes in as a late unlock, this is its door: the one field with no seat, blowing through wherever a gap is moving.

## The ending

The bet, with 211 years of interest, exceeds the value of the fortress. The Carrows can pay by opening the gate and the high road, so they do, scowling.

The last lift is one long defence along a moving town. Fen barges and unbolted stilt-houses ride the leading edge of Water up the valley toward the mesas, and you fight along them in stages as the front runs. Your regen moves with it, Water draining to Arcane band by band. Strange deep-field creatures come up behind, and everything alive in the fen stampedes through the middle. Everyone you helped arrives at a station on the route: Scarrow's skids and winch, the copper line, seed-wagon drivers, sledge crews, Ilse with one working coil. Those you did not help are missing, and you notice the empty stations. You attune to Water in the middle of it, a pool deepening while the well goes out from under you. The fight ends when the last barge clears the mesa's sluice, or when the front catches it.

Nobody saves the world, because the world is fine. The Flit completes. The map screen redraws with every colour one seat along, and every region has new bands. The postgame is the whole continent to explore again, with every side path open.

**The answer:** the almanac's last page, read out at the last feast, lists what got across and what did not, by name, from the jobs you took and the ones you left. Most of it. Never all.

**Last image:** the first morning of rain on the mesas. The fortress children have never seen it and stand very still. A fen woman, who has never once been dry, hands a mason a hat.

## The recurring faces

- **Bettany Rook, head porter of the tower.** Counts everything twice. Wants one crossing with no broken crockery.
- **Ottoline Brack, thawed gambler from the last Flit.** Certain of everything, 211 years out of date, asks what bread costs and is appalled. Wants to collect.
- **Joss Scarrow, smith.** Cannot stop improving a skid. Wants the first fire lit in the east before anyone else's, and you see that forge grow visit by visit.
- **Dodie Sprake, who runs a pub on a barge in the tower's wake.** Has never closed. Wants to hold every Leaving Feast, one for each field the night before it goes, each with a dish that can only be cooked on that field's last night. The feasts are where you rest, prepare and choose which pool deepens.
- **Oswin Carrow, heir to the mesa.** Reads everything aloud slowly in the hope that it changes. Wants the bet to be illegal.
- **Perpetua Lunt, fen widow.** Has never been told what to do successfully. Wants to stay in her house, and does. In the postgame her chairs need tying down, and she says the soup is improved.

## Why this is fun to play

Your best spell stops being cheap in the middle of the fight, and you rebuild your plan with your hands while the ground changes colour. You learn a region, leave, and come back to the same hills bending your regen a different way, so every old road tests a different part of your language. Between fights you are choosing which town's problem gets your last eight days, and you meet the result of that choice later, in the last battle, standing at its post or absent from it. With friends, the tower crossing and the barges are the same chaos as any fight, with furniture in it.

## The biggest weakness

There is nobody to beat. The final fight is a defence against weather and arithmetic, and a player who wants a face to hit gets herds, frightened masons and a river. Close behind it: the fixed order and the repeated shape (arrive, help, feast, lift fight) could feel like six runs of one chapter by the mesas, and the calendar only frightens anyone if skipped jobs visibly hurt.
