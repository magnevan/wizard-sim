# Learning an element by spillover

## One-liner
You fix a broken machine that draws on a field, and as it comes back its field spills into you: that element is yours.

## In the hands
Each stop has a machine, a fix made with what you already have (your hands, or the element you learned last), and an environmental effect of its own. You cut the vines off the healers' well, relight a cold boiler with Fire, wash an anchor stone clear with Water. The machine relights and the new rune lands on everyone's bar at once. Then the place asks you to use it: heal the wounded, douse the fire, freeze the surf. Shield comes the same way from the ward pylons, which store the academy casters' will rather than a field. (Round 2, 2026-11-16; round 1's overflow and drought arrivals were replaced.)

## Why it's interesting
It gives each element a place and a reason, out of the world's own infrastructure (pillar 5). It explains innate casting without lore dumps. Each fix practises the last element while it hands you the next, and each place's effect (spreading fire, surf on a rhythm, a cistern filling mid-fight) makes the stops feel different though the rule is the same.

## Costs & risks
- Seven places, each with a machine and art. The honest length is about an hour.
- The battery is the second element now, so its "you are the well" beat is a second time, not a discovery (accepted by the user: Life first is the lower-pressure first cast).
- Fixes must stay spells or the hands grammar. Carrying, cranking and timing puzzles would be minigames (pillar 6).
