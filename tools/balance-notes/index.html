Balance Notes

Every number in the engine is placeholder. Before you play-test, here is what the numbers actually do, measured by a harness that casts each spell at a target and runs bot pilots through the waves (npm run balance in game/). Below that are the problems the measurements show, a proposed first pass, and how to tune by feel with the new in-game panel. Questions have answer boxes beside them; anything left blank means you go with my recommendation.

What we tune toward

Balance should serve the pillars, so these are the targets the numbers should hit, before any feel pass:

  • A rune is worth about the same however you spend it. Spells that are harder to land, or that commit you longer, earn a little more per rune; spells that hit a crowd earn less per target. Without this, one way of spending runes dominates and the language collapses into a single sentence (pillar 2).
  • The pace comes from runes, not from cooldowns. You should cast often, but big spells should need saving up (pillar 4). That only works if the rune budget actually binds.
  • Every enemy asks a question you can read. Goblins die to anything that hits a crowd; brutes take a commitment; telegraphs give you time to answer (pillar 7).
  • Co-op multiplies, it doesn't just add. Combos between two players (soak then shock, freeze then shatter) should beat two players casting alone.

What that shows

The big one

Channels are about ten times cheaper than everything else

A beam or spray costs its runes once, then runs free for 5 seconds. One Arcane rune holds a beam for 154 damage; one Earth rune throws a rock for 16; a nova or a patch does 14–29. So the right play is almost always to hold a beam, and the rune budget barely binds: in the wave runs, faster regen didn't help the bots at all.

Options:

  1. Shorter free window, steeper upkeep: free for 2.5 s, then 0.4 runes a second, and lower channel damage (combat.channelRate 2.2 → 1.0). A one-rune beam becomes about 35 damage, the same per rune as a held beam's upkeep.
  2. Pay as you go: no free window. A channel costs its form element per second from the start. Clear, but every channel then drains the pool in your hands.
  3. Make one-shots stronger instead (rocks, novas, blasts up 4–6x). Keeps beams as they are, but inflates everything around them.
How should channels pay?

Extra runes of the same element scale unevenly

Doubling the form element should be a clear step up, by the same amount for every form. Today it isn't:

  • Arcane beam ×2: +100% damage (154 → 306).
  • Rock ×2: +94% (16 → 31), but ×3 only +50% more (46).
  • Flamethrower ×2: +38% (120 → 166), because most of its damage is the burn, which doesn't stack.
  • Frost spray ×2: +98%, but almost all of its value is the freeze, not damage.

My pick: each extra rune of the form adds a fixed share of the first rune's power, the same for all forms. Linear (+100%) keeps the arithmetic obvious. A smaller step (+70%) makes spreading runes across elements better than stacking one.

What should a second rune of the same element add?

Crowd spells are strong per rune, which is right, but sprays hit everyone at full

Against a pack of five goblin-sized targets, a one-rune flamethrower deals 542 (4.5× its single-target damage) and a lightning arc 312 (2.6×, because it chains). A beam stays at 154. That's the right shape (sprays answer swarms, beams answer brutes), but sprays hitting every body in the cone at full strength makes them the answer to nearly everything. A falloff (the second and later bodies take, say, 60%) would keep the role and cut the dominance.

Some spells don't pull their weight

  • Ice shards (Water + Cold as Force): 8 damage for 2 runes. The freeze needs most of the volley to land, and at range most shards miss. Either more shards land (tighter spread) or each shard chills more.
  • Frost spray: low damage by design (its job is the freeze), but the freeze takes about 2 s of spraying to land. Fine for now; watch it in play.
  • Discharge and Lightning strike: 8–12 per rune. Strike is meant to be the payoff on water (it surges), so it should stay low on dry ground, but Discharge has no such payoff.
  • Water spray does no damage on purpose (soak and push). It needs to feel useful through the combo (soak → lightning ×2), which is a co-op question for play-testing.

Enemies are soft, and slow to kill you

Every goblin dies to any one hit (16 hp). A brute needs about 4 seconds of beam. Standing still, a single goblin needs 25 s to bring a wizard down, a brute about 10 s. In the wave runs, survival moved most when enemies got tougher (8.5 → 6 waves cleared) or hit harder (→ 7), and least when regen got faster.

A proposed first pass

If you agree with the recommendations above, these are the starting numbers I'd put in before you play. They are not applied yet. Each is one row in the tuning panel, so you can try them live first.

rhythm.naturalChannel 5 → 2.5 · rhythm.upkeep 0.2 → 0.4 · combat.channelRate 2.2 → 1.0

damage.earth 16 → 22 · damage.arcane 14 → 16 (one-shots catch up a little)

combat.sprayFalloff 1 → 0.6 (each body behind the nearest takes 60%; already in the panel)

Form scaling: +70% per extra rune for every form (needs a small sim change)

Enemy hp ×1.5 and damage ×1.25 across the board; goblins stay at one hit

Then re-run the harness and look again before any play-test, so the first time you play it is already in the right neighbourhood.

Tuning by feel: how to use the panel

The game now has a tuning panel (press F1, or the Tune button at the top right). Every number above is a slider, changes apply on the next tick, and your changes are kept in your browser. While it's open, N spawns the chosen enemy at the cursor, P pauses and . steps one tick. Add #arena to the game's link for the waves.

  1. Pick one question per session, e.g. "do beams feel too free?" or "is wave 3 a real fight?". Changing five knobs at once tells you nothing.
  2. Play two or three waves on the current numbers first, then change one knob and play the same waves again. Start wave and Skip lull in the panel get you there fast.
  3. Watch the meters while you play: your damage per second, damage per rune by spell, runes per minute, and time-to-kill per enemy. They tie what you feel to a number.
  4. When something feels right, press Export and paste the JSON into our chat. I'll put it into the code, re-run the harness, and log it in the decisions.
  5. The Feel section at the top holds the knobs that change the game's pace most: rune regen, wind-up, recovery, channel damage, walk speed, the lull and enemy speed.

Tuned runs are marked "tuned" (and "dev-edited" once you've used a cheat), because a replay only reproduces a run with the same numbers. When we go online, every peer has to agree on the tuning, so the panel shows its hash.
