# The port to 3D: a plan (2026-11-25)

A plan for moving the playable game from the PixiJS pixel-art renderer to the 3D kit renderer, and then deleting the pixel version. Nothing here is built yet. The thread that tracks it: [port-3d](../threads/port-3d.md).

**What the user has already said** (2026-11-25):
- The game's mechanics don't change. The spell effects get a pass of their own.
- The Sanctum, the Arena, Survive and the tutorial are legacy. They won't be migrated; what replaces them is built new.
- The battery's art goes. Its scenario stays as the reference for a 3D battery, whose layout may change.
- The work will be done by a team of agents, with one lead holding the overall picture.

## What it should feel like when it's done

You open the page and stand in the courtyard: the same wizard, the same keys, the same spells, a friend beside you over the same netcode. The ground under you is the kit's court, with its terraces, stairs and sea wall. Goblins are the models you already know, without the pixel filter. Spells are lit, have depth, and land on the ground where the sim says they hit.

What it costs: every spell effect is redrawn, a tall world can hide a wizard behind a wall (the flat pixel rooms never could), and the page gets heavier to load and to run.

## Where things stand (main at `0b43ef6`)

| Part | Today | For the port |
|---|---|---|
| Sim, rules, net (`packages/sim`, `rules`, `net`) | No renderer dependency. 2D only: bodies have x and y, no height. Height exists as cliffs baked into walls, with stairs as gaps | Unchanged, except where rooms come from |
| Rooms | LDtk maps compiled to `rooms.gen.ts` as `RoomDef`. The sim's court (261 × 260 u, from plan D) is a different map from the kit's court scene | The kit scene becomes the source (decision 1) |
| World drawing | `view.ts`, `view-structures.ts`, `art/*`: about 14,000 lines, all 2D | Replaced |
| Spell effects | `art/vfx.ts` and friends: pixel art drawn on two canvases, under and over the actors. Every particle is a pure function of time and seed; nothing is stored. Inputs are sim state, plus sim events turned into short client records | Redrawn in 3D, keeping the same inputs and the stateless design |
| Characters | `art/live3d.ts` poses the real 3D models each frame, then pixelates them into an atlas for Pixi | The posing stays; the pixel pass and the atlas go |
| HUD | Pixi sprites fed by Canvas2D pixel drawing (`hud.ts`, `edge-marks.ts`, `tutorial-view.ts`, `help-preview.ts`) | Moves to a plain 2D canvas over the 3D view |
| Menus, panels, lobby, report box | DOM | Kept as they are |
| Audio, input, netplay, saves, scenarios | Renderer-free; input and audio need only a camera that turns screen points into world points | Kept; the camera is new |
| The 3D kit | `lab/kit-render.ts` builds a three.js group from a compiled scene. It owns no canvas and no loop, so the game can host it. Lab-only today: no sim, net or input. No compiled court is checked in; the editor compiles on demand | Becomes the game's world |
| Deploy | Pages runs `npm run check` and builds `index.html` only. No browser check gates a deploy | A second page first, then a swap |

## The decisions

Each has my recommendation. Step 0 of the plan shows 2, 3 and the effects' look as pictures before they're final; I build on the recommendation meanwhile.

### 1. One map: the kit scene feeds the sim

**Recommended: the kit scene is the only source.** `tools/kit/compile.py` gets a second output next to the picture's data: the room the sim walks on.

- Walkable cells come from each terrace's floor polygon and its `walk` flag.
- Walls come from the edge sections (wall, parapet, retaining wall) and from drops with no flight or steps.
- Blocking props come from set pieces' and dressing's footprints in `kit.json`.
- Heights come from each terrace's metres; flights become stair cells.
- Starts, gates, markers and regions become new object kinds in the scene, placed in the map editor.

The LDtk court and the pipeline's "agreement check" (does the scene match what the sim walks on) both go away: there's nothing left to disagree.

What it costs:
- **Determinism.** The exporter must write byte-identical data on every machine: coordinates snapped to the 0.05 u grid, a fixed order for every list, curves cut into segments by a fixed rule. A test compiles the court twice and compares.
- **Python in the build.** `content:build` is Node; the kit compiler is Python with shapely. The exporter writes a committed file (`game/content/generated/rooms/<scene>.room.json`) holding the room and a hash of its scene. `content:build` folds it into `rooms.gen.ts`, and `content:check` fails when the scene's hash has moved. CI never runs Python.
- **Hashes.** The golden hash covers the Sanctum only and doesn't move. The court's room hash changes, so the content hash changes and an old page can't join a new one (as intended). The court has no hash trail today; it gets one.
- **Editing.** Every scene save changes the room, so a save in the editor must re-run the exporter. Gameplay markers need editor support (a small addition; the editor is another session's work).

The sim stays 2D during the port. A parapet stops a spell exactly as a wall does, and nothing flies over a drop. That is today's behaviour in the LDtk court too. What that does and doesn't rule out is the next section.

*The alternative:* keep LDtk for the sim and match the scene to it by hand. No pipeline work, but every edit is made twice and the two drift. Not recommended.

### Height in a 2D sim: what we're committing to

The user asked (2026-11-25): with real height in the scenes, what does a 2D sim mean for shooting up and down a ledge, line of sight, knocking someone off a ledge, and a bridge over another part of the map? Are we locked out of any of them?

**Short answer: none of them is locked out.** "2D" here means bodies move on the ground plane (x, y), and their height comes from the map under their feet, not from a third axis they move along. That's how Magicka works, and most top-down action games. Each of the four needs some sim work later, and none needs a 3D sim. What the port commits to is a smaller thing: **during the port, every height edge stays a wall both ways.** That is the court's rule today.

What changes now is only the exporter: it writes the height data each feature will need, so adding one later is sim work and never a redo of the map pipeline. For each cell it records its height and which surface it belongs to. For each edge it records its kind (a wall, a parapet, a bare drop, a flight), how high its top stands, and the heights on its two sides.

| Situation | During the port | Later, still a 2D sim | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Shooting up or down a ledge** | A ledge or parapet stops every shot from both sides | A shot carries the height it was fired from. An edge stops it only if the edge's top stands above that height. From the terrace you fire over the parapet and down onto the plaza; from the plaza you hit the retaining wall, which is cover. Beams and sprays use the same rule. This is phase 3 of the [height thread](../threads/height.md) | Medium: one height test in the shot and wall code |
| **Line of sight** | Walls block sight both ways, as today | The same test as shots, so enemies on high ground see over the edge and those below don't. The camera already sees everything, so this only matters for AI and for what a spell can target | Small, once shots have it |
| **Lobbed spells** (a meteor over a wall) | Not lobbed | A missile can follow an arc, its height worked out from how far it has flown. It clears walls lower than the arc at that point. The map stays 2D | Small |
| **Knocked off a ledge** | Can't happen: the edge is a wall | A bare drop or a low parapet becomes one-way. It still stops you from below. From above, a strong enough knock carries you over, you're airborne for a moment and land on the lower surface: fall damage, a stun, maybe death into the sea. This is very much Magicka's game. Walking off on purpose can stay blocked, or become a way down | Medium: an airborne state, edges that block by direction, AI that avoids ledges. Design questions: which edges allow it, how a fall hurts, how co-op rescues someone who fell |
| **A bridge over walkable ground** (walking under it) | Not possible. The court's bridge spans the stream, which isn't walkable, so the court doesn't need it | Each body carries the surface it stands on. Walls, nav and hit tests are kept per surface, and the two meet only at flights and ramps. A fireball on the bridge doesn't hit the goblin underneath unless it's aimed down. Many 2D games do this with "floors" | Largest: about the size of the height thread's phases 1 and 2 together, plus how to show who's under what. Until then, maps keep walkable ground off the area under a bridge (water, a chasm, a sealed arch) |

**What a 2D sim does rule out**, and none of it looks like this game: free jumping and climbing anywhere, flying bodies, tumbling physics (barrels rolling down stairs), and aiming at a point in the air. Each of these could be faked with an arc or a short airborne state if a moment needs it.

**The trade-off.** The port is the cheapest moment to settle the room format and the most expensive moment to change the sim's rules. Settling the format now and adding behaviour later keeps both small. The one thing to watch: a map designed around "edges are walls" may play differently once shots pass over parapets. The court's terraces will feel different when that lands, which is a reason to do phase 3 soon after the port rather than much later.

### 2. Characters: the models as they are, no pixel pass

**Recommended: put the live models straight in the scene.** `live3d.ts` splits in two: the posing (clips, blends, move sets, held weapons, guards) is kept, and the atlas and pixel shader are dropped. Each body is a pooled copy of its model, lit by the scene's sun. Seat colours, status tints and the white hit flash become material settings.

What it costs:
- The imp and the tutorial's straw goblin have only pixel rigs. The court needs neither; the yard needs a straw goblin model before it can be played.
- Elemental deaths (1,315 lines of pixel art) are redone as part of the effects pass. Until then every death is the knock-down clip.
- Models made to be seen at 29 pixels are now seen whole. Some will need better textures or hands.

*The alternative:* keep a pixel look by running the whole 3D frame through a pixel filter. It keeps the old effects' style closer, but the 3D direction was chosen on 2026-11-23 as a stylised render, and a filter would fight the kit's painted stone.

### 3. Spell effects: redrawn in 3D, family by family

Nothing ports as it is: the effects are pixel art on flat canvases. What does carry over is everything underneath: the sim's shapes and timings, the event records, the stateless rule, the [VFX workflow](../vfx-workflow.md) (spec, lab, contact sheet, rubric) and the knob panel.

**Recommended:**
- **Stand-ins first.** As soon as casting works, every spell draws the sim's own hit shape on the ground in its element's colour (the VFX Lab's `hit=1` overlay, in 3D). The game is playable and readable for testing from that day, and the effects pass isn't on the critical path.
- **One small effects kit of our own**: camera-facing particles drawn in one batch whose motion is computed from time and seed in the shader, ribbons for beams and arcs, decals that follow the ground for floors and marks, and a pool of spell lights. It keeps the stateless rule, so rollback and "freeze at t" in the lab keep working. A general particle library would store state and break both.
- **Mediums in code, solids as models.** This carries over the split from [mediums are 2D particles, solids go through 3D](../decisions/README.md#2026-11-22-mediums-are-2d-particles-solids-go-through-3d), with the mediums now in 3D space because the pixel canvases they were kept for are gone. Fire, water, mist, light and sparks are particles moved and coloured by code, as the user expected of spells during the art trial ("super-dynamic things like spells will probably always be code drawn"). A boulder, an ice spike, a rock wall or a turret is a model (Meshy, from a Codex picture). Where a medium looks too plain as soft dots, its particle shapes come from a painted Codex sheet. That decision gets a superseding entry when the user agrees.
- **A picture before each family.** One Codex frame of the family in the court at the game camera, rated by the user, before any code.
- **The VFX Lab moves to 3D** early, so each effect is built and judged where it will be seen.

The families, in the order I'd do them: sprays (Water, Fire, Cold, Steam) · beams (Arcane, Life) · missiles (Earth's six, Ice shards) · rings, novas and bursts · Lightning (arcs, forks, strikes) · ground (fire floor, puddle, ice, caltrops, fog, rain, spring) · walls and barriers (rock, force field, coated, ice wall, wards) · Sigil devices (turrets, glyphs, orbitals, Lens, Double, auras) · statuses on bodies · weapon and guard effects · enemy attacks and ground warnings · deaths · placement previews.

What it costs: this is the largest part of the port, about a dozen families each going through the spec-and-rubric loop with the user. It's also where the game gets its new look, so it shouldn't be hurried.

**Lighting** is replaced outright: the sun, sky light and shadows are the kit's; a room's mood becomes a light preset; spell lights are real lights from a small pool.

### 4. The HUD: the same HUD on a 2D canvas

**Recommended: keep the pixel HUD for now**, drawn on a transparent 2D canvas over the 3D view. Its drawing code is already Canvas2D (`art/px.ts`); only the last step, handing a texture to Pixi, changes. The rune bar, the queue and health keep their look. Things attached to the world (health bars, edge arrows, the rally ring, prompts) are placed by projecting their 3D point to the screen.

The help panel's live preview draws a tiny pixel world; it gets a still picture per gesture until the 3D view can draw into a small box.

A new HUD look is a separate question for later. It doesn't block anything.

### 5. The camera: the kit's game camera

**Recommended: the kit's game camera** (from the south, 46° down, a narrow 28° lens), fixed, never turning. North is up, so W walks north; the pixel game's 45° turn goes.

- **Distance.** I'd match how much ground the pixel game shows today, so spell ranges read the same, and compare a closer and a further setting in step 0 with two wizards and a warband in frame.
- **Aiming.** The cursor's ray is dropped onto the ground at the height it hits, falling back to the wizard's own level. The sim still receives a 2D aim point.
- **Being hidden.** Walls and buildings south of a wizard cover them. Anything between the camera and a local wizard fades to a dither, and a covered wizard or enemy shows as a silhouette. This is new work with no pixel-game equivalent, and it must be judged in the court's tightest corners.
- **Two players.** As today: local seats share a camera aimed at their middle; online each screen follows its own wizard; arrows at the edge point to the others.

### 6. What's retired, and when

**Recommended:**

| Thing | On the 3D page | In the sim |
|---|---|---|
| Arena, tutorial, Academy art test, Terraces | Never offered | Deleted with Pixi (modes, tests, hash trails) |
| Survive | Never offered | Kept headless until a kit arena replaces it: the balance tools and the encounter runner's tests run on it |
| Sanctum | Replaced by a **practice ground**: a small flat kit scene with dummies, the Tune panel and dev waves | Kept headless at first, because the golden hash and the audit, balance and scaling tools run in it. Moved to the practice ground later, with the golden hash re-recorded once, on purpose |
| Battery | Not offered until it's rebuilt on the kit | Kept whole: `battery.ts`, `battery-waves.ts`, their tests, `battery-text.ts`. It finds its map by tags, so a new layout only needs the tags. Its pixel art goes |
| Yard, infirmary | Not offered until rebuilt on the kit | Kept |

The practice ground is new work the user didn't ask for. I recommend it because spells, balance tools and the VFX Lab all need a flat, quiet place, and the court's mode allows no spells at all today.

### 7. The order of the switch

**Decided: a second page, an early swap, one delete.** Nobody plays the public site yet (the user, 2026-11-25), so the swap waits only until the 3D page works, not until it matches the pixel game.

1. `play3d.html` is added to the build and deployed beside the game, so half-built work can merge to main without breaking the page.
2. Once you can walk and cast in the court and the practice ground (the end of step 2), `play3d.html` becomes `index.html`. The pixel game moves to `classic.html`, kept as the battery's and the yard's reference until the delete.
3. When the battery and the yard exist on the kit, the pixel game, Pixi and its art are deleted in one change.

Since the legacy rooms aren't migrating, there's no "room by room" delete: one swap and one delete are simpler and keep every check green in between.

## The steps

Each step ends with something to look at or play, and with `npm run check` green on a main that still deploys a working game.

**Step 0. See it before building it.** One review page with:
- the court at three camera distances, with two wizards and six goblins standing in it;
- the wizard and the warband unpixelated, lit by the court's sun;
- one effect made twice, a Fire spray and its floor, to settle the effects kit's look;
- the court's derived walk grid drawn over the scene, to show that decision 1 works on the real map.

**Step 1. Walk the court.** One wizard, local input, the sim driving, the room made from the scene. The view shell (page, loop, camera, cursor), the scene hosted in the game, the exporter, the character in the scene.

**Step 2. Cast.** The effects kit, stand-ins for every spell, the first real family (sprays), the HUD canvas, the practice ground, the VFX Lab in 3D. The court's mode keeps its no-spells rule; casting is tested in the practice ground and with a dev switch.

**The swap** happens here, at the end of step 2: `play3d.html` becomes `index.html`, the pixel game moves to `classic.html`, and `current-state.md` is rewritten for the 3D game. Steps 3 on are built on the deployed page.

**Step 3. Two players.** The rollback hooks in the 3D view, the lobby on the new page, `check:net` and `check:webrtc` against it. Netcode itself doesn't change, so this is small.

**Step 4. Goblins.** The warband in the scene, attacks and their warnings, the knock-down death, Stage tools, fading walls and silhouettes.

**Step 5. The effects pass.** The remaining families through the workflow, statuses and deaths included. Runs alongside steps 3, 4 and 6.

**Step 6. Make it shippable.** Static pieces batched (the court is 1,000+ separate pieces today, each drawn on its own), models loaded per room and not all at once (the kit's models are 63 MB), a loading screen, a fallback message for machines without a usable GPU.

**Step 7. Height in the sim, phase 3.** Shots, beams and sight pass over edges lower than where they were fired from (see [height in a 2D sim](#height-in-a-2d-sim-what-were-committing-to)). It belongs to the [height thread](../threads/height.md), and comes soon after the port because the court's terraces play differently once it lands. Knock-offs and bridges you can walk under wait for a design round of their own.

**Step 8. The opening on the kit.** The yard, the battery and the infirmary, each through [making a place](../campaign/making-a-place.md). This belongs to the [opening thread](../threads/opening.md); the port only supplies what they need (a straw goblin model, the battery's well, towers and flame in 3D).

**Step 9. Delete.** The pixel view, `art/`, Pixi, the LDtk rooms nothing uses, the retired tests and scripts. Assets and tools pages are listed for the user before anything is removed.

## The team

The lead (this session) keeps the plan, the interfaces between the parts, the merges into `port-3d`, and looks at every shot before the user does. Each agent works in its own worktree on a branch off `port-3d`, owns a set of files, and reports with shots or check output.

| Workstream | Owns | Starts |
|---|---|---|
| **Map to sim** | `tools/kit/compile.py`'s room output, `packages/content`, the court's mode and tests | Step 0 |
| **View shell** | the new page, the view interface `main.ts` talks to, the camera, the cursor, hosting the kit scene, fading walls | Step 0 |
| **Characters** | the posing half of `live3d.ts`, bodies in the scene, weapons and guards, tints | Step 0 |
| **Effects kit**, then one agent per family | the effects kit, the 3D VFX Lab, each family's files | Step 0 (spike), step 2 on |
| **HUD and panels** | the HUD canvas, world-attached marks, the help panel | Step 2 |
| **Checks and build** | scripts, tests, `vite.config.ts`, the Pages workflow, perf | Step 1 |
| **Places** | the practice ground, later the opening's places | Step 2 |

Rules for the team:
- Sonnet for sweeps and mechanical ports; the default model for design, effects and reviews.
- The first thing built is the **view interface**: the short list of calls `main.ts` makes (`beforeStep`, `afterStep`, `draw`, the rollback hooks, the camera's two conversions). Both renderers implement it, and every other workstream builds against it.
- `kit-render.ts` and its siblings are used where they are, in `lab/`, until the swap. The map editor's session edits those files on `kit-clean`; moving them now would collide with it.
- `port-3d` merges to main often. The new page is separate, so a merge can't break the deployed game.
- Paid generation follows the standing rules: Codex freely, Meshy freely until 2026-12-16 with costs reported, PixelLab and ElevenLabs only on sign-off.

## Risks

- **Hidden wizards.** If fading and silhouettes don't read well in the court's narrow places, the camera or the map has to give. Found in step 0's shots, not step 4.
- **Weight.** 63 MB of kit models and no batching. Fine on the user's machine, not on a friend's laptop. Step 6 has a budget; if step 1 already runs badly, batching moves earlier.
- **Headless checks.** The scripts run Chrome on a software renderer, where the 3D view is slow. Checks that only compare sim hashes run with drawing off.
- **The effects pass is long.** The stand-ins keep it from blocking the swap; the swap needs "readable", not "finished".
- **Two sessions on the kit.** The map editor's session and this port both touch the kit. Scene markers and the exporter's hook on save need agreeing with it.
- **A spell over a parapet.** In 3D it will look wrong that a low wall stops a fireball. That's a sim rule (step 7), noted here so it isn't mistaken for a port bug.
