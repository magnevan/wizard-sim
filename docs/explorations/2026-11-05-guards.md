# 2026-11-05: Guards, and the round-2 weapon redesigns

Follows `2026-11-05-weapons-review-1.md` (grammar v2, no two-handed weapons). The user, on guards: "we should find the set of things that guards do. I think some of the moves could be defined by the buckler, and they could have some passive effect as well, and various degrees/types of damage resistance." Shown as round 2 of the Weapon Moveset Review (`tools/weapon-moves/`).

## What a guard is

A guard is Shield + a lead element, conjured once, and it lasts until its HP runs out. It owns two gestures and two always-on traits:

| Part | Gesture | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Defend** | Space (hold = block, tap as a hit lands = parry) | Block stops frontal hits into the guard's HP. Each guard adds its own **block effect** and **parry effect**. |
| **Retreat** | Shift+LMB | A quick move away from where you face. Each guard moves you differently, and one doesn't move you at all. |
| **Passive** | none | Always on while you carry the guard, blocking or not. |
| **Resistances** | none | Always on while carried, from any direction. Blocking is the frontal 100 %, and resistances are what still protects you from the side or mid-swing. |

**Rules for all guards.** Any weapon move drops the block for its wind-up and recovery. Retreat is free, but it has a fixed 0.5 s recovery during which you can't Retreat or Defend again. That's an explicit rhythm, not a hidden cooldown (pillar 4). Shield alone is the baseline, and every other guard trades something for its edge.

**Readability rule.** Each Retreat has its own silhouette **and** its own ground mark: hop dust, a planted slab, a fire line, an ice strip, a blink scorch, a wave, a hex afterimage, a leaf trail. Your partner reads where you went and what you left behind without knowing the guard's name. Every passive that changes what your partner can do to you is drawn on your body, for example the always-wet drip of the Tide shield.

## The eight guards

| Guard (queue) | Defend: block / parry | Retreat (Shift+LMB) | Passive | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Mirror buckler** (Shield) | Narrow block / the parry **reflects** a missile back at its shooter | **Backstep**: a crisp 2-unit hop | **Light**: full speed while blocking, and the widest parry window (0.2 s) | The baseline: no special resistance |
| **Bulwark** (+Earth) | Wide block that also stops melee, and you walk slowly / the parry **bashes** the attacker down | **Brace**: you don't move. You plant the slab and nothing moves you for 1 s | **Anchored**: no knockback while carried | You walk 15 % slower all the time |
| **Ember targe** (+Fire) | Block with **thorns**: melee attackers catch fire / the parry blows a short burst of flame | **Flame step**: a dash back that leaves a 1 s line of fire where you stood | **Warm**: you can't be chilled or frozen, and you dry at once | Water douses it: double guard damage from Water |
| **Rime kite** (+Cold) | Blocking frosts the floor in front, so chargers slip / the parry **freezes** a melee attacker for a moment | **Ice slide**: a long slide back on an ice strip that stays, and chasers slip on it | **Sure-footed**: you never slip on ice, anyone's | Fire melts it (−25 %) |
| **Spark ring** (+Lightning) | The weakest block / the parry **discharges** a chain arc to the nearest enemies and stuns casters | **Blink**: a 3-unit teleport back that leaves a spark, which shocks whoever steps on your old spot | **Grounded**: chain lightning skips you, so your partner's lightning never jumps through you | Fragile: −25 % physical, lowest HP |

### Resistances (while carried)

Physical covers melee, arrows and rocks. Water and Life deal no damage, so they're listed under the trade-off column above.

| Mirror buckler | 25 % | | | | |
| Bulwark | **50 %** | 25 % | 25 % | | |
| Ember targe | | **immune** | 50 % | | |
| Rime kite | 25 % | −25 % | **immune** | | |
| Spark ring | −25 % | | | **immune** | |

| Prism pane | −25 % | | | | **50 %**, plus half of every frontal beam |

The pattern is that each guard is **immune or strong against its own element and weak against its opposite**. A player who knows the element wheel can read the table without memorising it, which fits pillar 2. Numbers are placeholders for the sim, but keep the steps coarse (25 / 50 / immune) so they read as tiers, not stats.

### Why these, and what they cost

- **Retreat is where guards diverge most, on purpose.** Blocks are similar by nature (you hold a thing up). A retreat changes position, which a co-op partner has to track. So the shapes are as different as possible: a hop, no move at all, a dash, a long slide, a teleport, a ride, a phase, a curve towards a friend.
- **Two retreats leave something on the floor that hits friends:** the Flame step's fire line and the Blink spark. That's friendly fire by design (pillar 3), and it's the cost of the best disengages. The Ice slide's strip makes anyone slip, your partner too.
- **The two support guards (Leaf, Spark) do support through movement and immunity, not numbers.** Regroup moves you to your partner, and Grounded lets a lightning partner chain freely around you. That's a support role you play with your hands (pillar 3).
- **Finger cost: none added.** Space and Shift+LMB were already given to the guard in v2. The cost is knowledge: 8 retreats, 8 parries and 8 passives. That's why each passive is one word and one rule, and why the resistance steps are coarse.
- **Readability cost.** The parry effects are the hardest to read, because they happen in 0.2 s. Each parry needs a big, brief flash in the guard's colour plus a one-word pop on the first few uses.

## Weapon redesigns (round 2)

The empty-queue weapon slots are now Strike (RMB tap), Heavy (RMB hold) and Special (Shift+RMB).

| Weapon | Strike | Heavy | Special |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bare staff | shoves (kept) | big shove (kept) | Sweep (kept) |
| **Tide trident** (was the glaive) | wide shoving arcs (kept) | wave slash along the floor (kept) | Sweep, a wash (kept) |
| Healing hands | touch heal (kept) | **Healing link (new):** a continuous channel with no release. The link holds while the ally walks away, until about 8 units or a wall. It costs 1 use a second | Sweep, touch-heal as you turn (kept) |
| Frost spear | three pokes (kept) | **Impaling dash, much further (new):** about 2.5× the old reach, straight through a frozen target, which shatters | **Javelin (moved here):** an ice javelin splits off the spear, flies, and pins the target. The spear stays in your hand, so there's no recall. 3 uses |
| Lightning whip | tip cracks (kept) | chain lash (kept) | Sweep, a wide weak whirl (kept) |
| **Echo focus** (Arcane, new) | a weak orb bolt | **Echo:** re-casts the spell it remembers at half power. 5 uses | **Copy:** the orb takes the last spell an ally cast near you (within about 6 units, in the last 2 s) as the one it remembers |
| Flame sword | fast slashes, 3rd throws flame (kept) | **Fire wave (new):** a rolling crescent of flame that travels forward and ignites what it passes. It leaves scorch, not lingering fire | Sweep, a ring of flame (kept) |
| Stone maul | slow, guard-breaking (kept) | **Directed quake (new):** the slam sends a fissure down one lane. Rocks burst up in sequence and knock down everything in the line, nothing beside it | Sweep, a huge spin (kept) |

**The arcane redesign: the Echo focus.** It's a violet orb over your palm that remembers the last spell you cast, and that spell's runes orbit it, so you and your partner can see what's loaded. Its Heavy re-casts that spell at half power for uses instead of runes. Its Special copies an ally's last spell instead, which makes it a co-op amplifier, not another stick.

*Why this one:* the alternatives were (a) a focus whose Strike marks targets for your next spell to home on, and (b) a focus that adds power to your next real cast. (a) adds a second targeting system, which costs readability. (b) is a flat "+X %", which pillar 1 warns against. Echo is the only one that gives the partner something to read (the orbiting runes) and something to set up: cast a good spell and your arcane friend copies it. *Pillar 4 risk, said plainly:* Echo casts a strong spell with one button. Half power, 5 uses (about six echoes per conjure) and the runes you had to cast first are what keep it honest. If play shows echo spam, lower it to a third of the power, or make Echo spend the memory.

## Open questions (with my recommendation)

1. **Do resistances apply while you carry the guard, or only while you block?** *Recommend: while carried.* Blocking already stops frontal hits completely. Resistances give the guard an identity in the 80 % of the fight when you're swinging or casting. The alternative ("only while blocking") is simpler to balance but makes guards matter only when Space is down.
2. **What does Retreat cost?** *Recommend: nothing, with a fixed 0.5 s recovery* (no Retreat or Defend during it). The alternatives are a cooldown timer, which is a hidden-cooldown smell (pillar 4), or guard HP per retreat, which punishes the fragile guards twice.
3. **What does a second element do in a guard (Shield + Fire + Cold)?** *Recommend: the lead element picks Defend, Retreat and passive, and the second element adds only its own resistance at half strength plus a coat colour.* The alternatives are no effect, or mixing the traits (the Ember thorns on a Rime kite). Mixing is fun to discover, but with 8 retreats it becomes unreadable for the partner.
4. **The Bulwark's Retreat doesn't move you (Brace). Keep that exception?** *Recommend: yes.* The tank's way out is to stop being moved, and the slab planted in the ground is a clear tell. The alternative is a slow shove-back step, which reads like a weak Backstep.
