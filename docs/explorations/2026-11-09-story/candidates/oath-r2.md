# Before All the People

To attune at each great well you give your word before the people who live on it, and the circuit becomes the long road back to pay them, until what you have sworn to one people you can keep only by failing another.

## The question

**When the circuit is walked, which gates will open to you?**

The ending answers it literally. The last road of the game passes every town and village you swore to, and each gate stands as your deeds left it.

## The opening

The school stands on a tidal islet in the bay beneath the southern badlands, on an Arcane vein. By old right, the shore-people may ask one service of each departing class. On your last night a boat is lost beyond the bar with the tide coming in, and a mother asks it of you on the school steps. You swear aloud to bring that boat home. A hazel stick is notched and split between you. The run along the drowning causeway teaches the queue, the casts, the conjured weapon and the danger of your own friends. At dawn both halves burn on the quay. Any opening that ends with one stick cut and burned may replace this.

## The road

**How the word is given.** The wells stand on common ground and belong to those who live around them. They have always admitted journeymen, because a wizard in their debt is the only wizard a village can hope for. The school teaches this on the first day. No magic binds an oath, and no attunement can be taken back. There is only what you said, before all the people.

- **Wording.** Each great oath is offered in two or three wordings, and the host chooses, or a quick vote. A generous wording earns more at once. A narrow one leaves room later.
- **Three records.** *Kept:* both halves burn at the hearth, and gates and hearths open. *Kept to the letter:* your half of the stick is given back unburned, the gate opens, and no one asks you in. *Broken:* the town nails its half to the gate.
- **Instalments.** Every great oath has a smaller due on each return, sized to what you can now cast. Each well you take with a due unpaid cuts a new notch in that stick and enlarges the debt.
- **Small oaths.** Some forty, each with its own village gate, built from the shrine kits as restrictions and tasks: no Lightning in the mill valley, this lantern kept lit across the marsh. These move most of the gates on the final road.
- **Power.** Only the ford and the dyke ever split the paths. Each offers the same total power either way.

Each attunement grants a regen step and deepens the pool you choose. The order is fixed, for debts must be scheduled. Paths reopened on return accept two different answers or a regen step, never one pool's depth.

### 1. Ighrem n'Aman (Earth), the mesas

- **Place:** a fortress older than its histories, its cisterns cut so deep that stars show in the water at noon.
- **Oath:** *"When the cisterns fail, bring water to us,"* or *"bring us to water."* They have not failed in two hundred years, and the people cheer as at a wedding.
- **Exam:** drive the burrowers from the well shaft.
- **Growth:** walls and stone you can afford to keep raising in a long fight.
- **Encounters:** switchback gauntlets, burrowers, a defence of the great lift.
- **The beat:** Tiziri lets down the plumb line and reads the depth aloud. Below the fortress, children play on a ruined stair that runs north toward the fen, carved with the names of those who climbed it.
- **On return:** the reading is painted on the lift wall, lower at each visit. Rain banked in the cisterns is the instalment, and it raises the number.

### 2. Aukmedis (Life), the great forest

- **Place:** a tree so vast it makes its own rain. The parish lays its dead among the root-halls, and from each grave a green shoot rises the same spring.
- **Oath:** *"Never bring fire past the ford, and lay Shushan at the roots when her day comes."* A Fire spell in ford country counts only if something is still burning and someone could see it. If nobody saw it burning, you never did it.
- **Exam:** escort the root-tenders through the halls, against pests that beg to be burned.
- **Growth:** healing you can sustain between fights, and every answer you own besides Fire.
- **Encounters:** escorts, swarms, regenerating wildlife.
- **The beat:** Shushan, eighty years old, holds the ford and searches your pockets for flint. Hers is a southern name among the people of the tree.
- **On return:** she is slower each time and misses nothing. The instalment is a season's firebreak cut without flame.

### 3. Ulan Khalga (Lightning), the red badlands

- **Place:** a great arch of stone. On storm nights a single bolt stands in the span for minutes together, and the herders swear by its light.
- **Oath:** *"Stand for our side when the boundary is heard."* The boundary is the summer pasture against Aukmedis, to whom you swore last season.
- **Exam:** cross the open ground in storm, among swarms that chain.
- **Growth:** chains you can repeat, and the first great regen step.
- **Encounters:** steppe ambushes, and your first enemy casters, claim-jumpers fighting for a living.
- **The beat:** Odval explains the Hearing. Each side's sworn wizard stands on the line beneath the bolt until one has stepped off. A nailed stick on the herders' gate bears the name of Ilarion Kask, your classmate. It is the first broken word you see.
- **On return:** the instalment is a herd brought through a storm, and the pasture shows the Hearing's verdict.

### 4. Nijeterp (Water), the fen

- **Place:** a city on stilts, holding back the sea and the melt by the labour of nine generations.
- **Oath:** *"Come back and hold the dyke when the spring melt comes,"* or *"stand on the dyke."*
- **Exam:** a flooded gauntlet that teaches conduction without mercy.
- **Growth:** Rain you can keep falling across a whole encounter.
- **Encounters:** amphibious packs, a native boss in the great sluice, gate-rooms fed by your spells.
- **The beat:** the people walk you the length of the dyke, each family naming its stretch. On the oldest piles are the names from the mesa's stair.
- **On return:** see the midpoint. Afterwards, the mesa's first households camp outside the dyke, in twos and threes.

### 5. Krakadzor (Fire), the volcanic south

- **Place:** a city of smiths cut into the crater's inner wall. Its dead go to the vent, and their burning can be seen from the sea. They hold that those left unburned go on walking home.
- **Oath:** Zabel, Shushan's sister, asks it: *"Bring our sister home to the fire,"* or *"bring the fire to her."* You know the name, and you know what you swore at the ford.
- **Exam:** cross the caldera floor between two breathings of the lava, on a crust your own Fire will break.
- **Growth:** burning you can maintain, with Fire at last unforbidden.
- **Encounters:** quick and hungry packs, channel puzzles, a stand on the crater road.
- **The beat:** Zabel shows you fifty years of unopened gifts laid by for a sister's homecoming.
- **On return:** the galleries or the crater road, according to the late turn.

### 6. Minar-e Shab (Arcane), the warped east

The fen's boats are the only way down the warped river. See the midpoint.

- **Place:** a black tower hanging in the air above the well, with rain falling upward around it. Each year someone must climb and watch one night. No chronicle says what is watched. Each year a child is chosen by lot.
- **Oath:** *"Stand this year's watch in place of Golnar."*
- **Exam:** the ascent through rooms where stones fall upward, then the night's defence of the topmost chamber.
- **Growth:** the largest regen step in the game, enough to hold the deep beam.
- **Encounters:** strange things, few and terrible. Enemy casters who are masters of their art.
- **The beat:** you come down at dawn and a full year has passed. Golnar, a head taller, meets you at the foot of the tower. She has had the year you spent.
- **On return:** each year's lot is drawn, and the doorposts bear the heights of the children who were spared.

### 7. Davgi (Cold), the ice

- **Place:** the ribs of something no singer has named rise from the ice higher than any tower built by hands, and a people live in their shelter. For centuries their singers have held every oath of the circuit by heart.
- **Oath:** *"Whatever you decide, you will say it aloud before the singers, and change no word of what they sing."*
- **Exam:** a long ice road against large and patient things.
- **Growth:** ice and shatter you can use twice in any fight.
- **The beat:** Máret sings you the circuit so far, each oath in your own wording.
- **On return:** the free road after the end.

### Optional: the crystal island (north-west, after the midpoint)

On a peak of blue crystal live wizards who finished the circuit and swore never to swear again. They teach Shield's wider arts, the will to refuse. Thereafter you may decline any small oath without cost.

## The turns

**The midpoint, and the opening of Sigil.** The melt and the Hearing fall due in one week at opposite ends of the world. There is one boat and one party.

On the voyage you put in at Sev Karer, the forest of black columns east of the volcano. There the cutters carve stones that go on working when the carver has gone home. Hovik sets you a trial against a small practice flood, and the full art opens: turrets, glyphs, Blink, auras. *"You have shaped what you hold. Shape the wall."*

Now you choose. **Stay,** and the dyke is truly kept, with the herders' half nailed up for your absence. **Sail,** and you build first, family by family. Floes ram the dyke and eel-packs climb the breaches: turrets take the packs, Earth and Cold stop the ice. Stretches scale with the party.

Every device you leave holds a Sigil rune from you, so whatever you give the dyke is missing from your duel. At the Hearing you face Ilarion Kask, sworn to Aukmedis and glad to see you. The party holds the line until the last of you steps off it, and the oath is kept win or lose. If the herders win, the parish loses summer grazing and its people will tell you so.

Then you sail back for the last night and find the dyke exactly as your layout left it. Stretches that failed cost those families their houses, visibly. Aukje Terpstra gives you back your half unburned. She says only, "You left."

The boatmen, as Nijeterp gives thanks, set the price of the river: *"No one shall settle the dyke lands,"* or *"no one shall settle them against the will of those who hold them."* You have seen the camps. You have read the names on the stair. You swear, or you never reach the seventh well.

**The late turn: Shushan's day.** You come down from the tower into the year you lost. Every open stick has taken a notch. Shushan has died. She lies in the root-halls through the forty days of watching, and Krakadzor's fire-bearers are on the road.

- **Carry her home.** Escort her bier through a parish that bars your way and will not strike to kill. The ford's gate takes the stick, and the tree roads close. At the vent, Zabel opens the gifts and with them fifty years of letters from Shushan, each promising to come home next spring. To the parish, she swore she never would. This is learned only here.
- **Lay her at the roots.** The fire-bearers and their sworn casters come to the ford with torches. You hold it without Fire, for you swore that also. Krakadzor's gate takes the stick, the upper halls open, and in spring a shoot rises.
- **Bring the fire to her,** if that was your wording. Burn her by night beyond the ford, and turn back with wall and frost anyone who comes near enough to see. If nobody saw it burning, you never did it. Both sticks come back unburned. At the end you will stand before the singers.

## The ending

Ighrem's reading comes to nothing, plainly a matter of water in stone. Two thousand people go down the old stair and walk north to the fen in the season of melt, carrying their children, with the names of their forebears on the piles. Aukje gives the numbers once: the dyke lands feed so many, and a third more would starve every household in a bad year. Neither people lies.

You stand on the dyke, your own devices about you, and you say aloud before the singers whom you stand with. The melt comes either way, and the dyke must hold.

- **Your deeds decide who comes.** Each kept town sends what it can: herders riding the storm, ice-cutters to the seaward flank, root-tenders with their healing. Those you failed stay home.
- **The other side's sworn casters** come against you, Ilarion among them, who swore to both peoples and has stayed with the one you left.
- **Banked Rain** decides how the losers lose: how many walked the road alive, and how far they had to go.

**If you stand with Nijeterp,** the column is turned back to a camp on the southern strand, and the dyke lands stay as nine generations made them. **If you stand with the mesa-folk,** they are brought in, the old families are crowded and hungry, and the inner terps draw up their planks. In either case the emptied cisterns of Ighrem open as a deep new road.

The singers sing it there, on the dyke, every oath and what came of it. Then comes the long road home, past each gate.

**Last image:** you stop at the closed place of the people you chose against, a drawn plank or a rope across the strand path. A shutter opens. It belongs to the family whose stretch of the dyke held, or whose terrace you filled with Rain. They look at you for a long while. They set out a bowl on the last post, and the shutter closes. If you left them nothing to be grateful for, the shutter stays closed. You eat, or you do not, and you go on with the unburned half at your belt.

## The recurring faces

- **Máret, singer of Davgi.** Hears everything once. Walks the circuit behind you to get your words exact.
- **Ilarion Kask, of your class.** Gives their word as others give greetings. Wants to be loved by everyone.
- **Shushan, at the ford.** Her hands go through every pocket. Wants to be spared the choosing.
- **Aukje Terpstra, Speaker of the Stretches.** Knows every family's length of dyke by heart. Wants the fen's children to stay.
- **Tiziri n'Aman, of the cisterns.** Thirty-one years at the plumb line. Wants the last reading to fall to someone else.
- **Zabel of Krakadzor.** Lays by a gift each year. Wants her sister home.
- **Hovik of the black columns.** Speaks only while the chisel moves. Wants someone to carry on the stones.

## Why this is fun to play

You return to places where you were weak and settle in moments of well-chosen runes what you once swore to settle someday. Your oaths reach into your hands: forty sworn restrictions, a ford held against torches with Fire forbidden, a friend's mistake to be doused before anyone can see it. Each rune you leave on the dyke is one you lack in the duel, so preparation and promise are one decision. By the end, the world differs from gate to gate according to what you did.

## The biggest weakness

The seven great oaths are required, so haggling their wording is the only freedom at the moment of swearing, and a sharp player will see the dyke coming hours ahead and may resent being made to swear. Foresight changes how the losers lose and never who loses. Some players will feel it as a locked door.
