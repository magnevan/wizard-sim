Research

What Magicka did

Magicka 1 has about fifty enemy types in families (goblins, orcs, trolls, dwarves, beastmen, undead, demons, treants). Magicka 2 reuses the families and tells them apart mostly by elemental weakness. Magicka 2 has almost no wiki coverage, so it's thinly sourced here.

Your tools are the answerPush throws the goblin bomber's bombs back. Fire stops a troll's regeneration. A shield beats the dwarf priest's one-shot boulder. Life hurts the undead and Arcane heals them.
Enemies hurt each otherGoblin shamans and wizards cast so loosely they mostly hit goblins. The captain's freezing spin and a troll's hammer hit their own side. Cheap, readable and funny.
Small rules with big readsGoblins panic when burning. A troll won't grab a wizard who's on fire. Demons split in three unless exploded. A wight leaves a skeleton. Alpha beastmen lend nearby monsters a resistance.
What went wrong in 2Reviewers liked the variety but not the clutter: swarms from every side, hits from off screen, little room for tactics. Resistances you can't see read as homework.
Magicka 1 enemies that stand out (Magickapedia, carlsguides)
  • Goblin bomber: bombs lie on the ground, then burst (knockdown, burning); fire on the bomber sets them off; it drops 4 to 5 bombs when it dies.
  • Goblin shaman: water beam, then lightning on the wet wizard; random teleports; a fire ward that also covers nearby goblins.
  • Goblin wizards ("the Aristocrats"): robes in their element, set repertoires, all self-heal and use fire to dry themselves and water when burning; weak to the opposite of their colour.
  • Goblin warrior and captain: a shield with its own HP; they speed up once it breaks.
  • Trolls: 6,000+ HP, regenerate unless burning; hammers, cannons that lob or shoot straight, and grabbing a wizard to eat (mash to escape). The snow troll won't grab a wizard who's on fire or wearing rock or ice.
  • Beastmen: raiders leap across the map and sidestep; brutes charge when you're close; Alphas carry a resistance aura.
  • Dwarves: warriors teleport into melee; priests charge a one-shot 5× earth boulder, shield themselves, heal others and are pushed into lava.
  • Undead: Life hurts, Arcane heals. Wights lunge; Darksouls come in four elements, weak to their opposite; skeletons ignore sprays; the necromancer hides in its zombies.
  • Demons phase out and in, and split into demonlings unless finished with a big explosion.
  • Grimnir: glows before a tornado or a conflagration; a shield absorbs the conflagration; wet then shock hurts him.

Today the game has six fighting enemies: goblin, brute, shieldbearer, archer, grabber and fire imp. Each runs a small state machine (approach, telegraphed wind-up, strike, cooldown) and chases the nearest wizard. Nothing coordinates them, so every goblin in reach winds up at once.

AI · enemies

A fair fight, not a clever one

Keep the per-enemy state machine, which already drives the four-beat telegraph, and add small layers on top. The first is the one that changes the feel most.

Attack tokens

An enemy takes a token from its target before it winds up. Without one, it waits on a ring about 3 u out, shuffles sideways and feints. Batman Arkham, Assassin's Creed and DOOM (2016) all keep crowds readable this way.

AI · companions

Seems smart, isn't clever

There's no companion design yet. The canon says the cohort is "you" and the world carries the story more than deep companions do, so a companion is probably a classmate or a summoned helper rather than a personality-driven partner.

Out of your line of fire

The player's aim sweeps across a crowd. The companion keeps to a follow ring and slides round it to stay out of the cone you aim through. Before it casts, it checks that no ally is in the spell's footprint. A glyph over its head shows what it's about to do.
