# The modular kit

Places are built from made pieces on a square grid ([flexible building](../../docs/explorations/2026-11-23-flexible-building.md)). A **scene** says, per grid corner, which terrace it belongs to, and names the flights, curves, edge overrides and props. The **compiler** turns it into placed pieces and checks it. The [map editor](../map-editor/README.md) edits scenes live, in 3D, with Claude.

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `kit.json` | The asset contract: each piece's class, size, pivot and front |
| `scenes/<name>.scene.json` | The scenes: the source of truth (beside each, the editor's `.pins.json`, and its undo history `.history.jsonl`, not in git) |
| `compile.py` | Scene → pieces and findings. `python tools/kit/compile.py patch-b` writes `game/packages/client/src/lab/kit-patch-b.json` for the greybox page (`kit3d.html`) |
| `room.py` | Scene → the room the sim walks on: `python tools/kit/room.py court` writes `game/content/generated/rooms/court.room.json` (or `compile.py court --room`); `--check` exports twice and compares with the committed file. What it derives and how: `game/content/README.md`, "Rooms from kit scenes" |
| `room_shot.py` | `python tools/kit/room_shot.py court` draws that room's walk grid over the scene's plan (`game/shots/port-3d/map/court-walk.png`; `--scale`, `--crop` in sim units) |
| `scene.py` | `convert <layout>` turns an old Python layout into a scene; `check <scene>` prints its findings with their places |
| `worker.py` | The map editor's long-lived compiler (JSON lines in and out) |
| `fit.py` | Blender: fits a generated model to its piece's box; write the result to `game/content/generated/meshy/kit-full/` (full size, kept for later uses such as LoD) |
| `split_sheet.py` | Blender: a bundled Meshy model (several objects drawn apart on one Codex sheet, one call) split into one model per object, by grid position; then `fit.py` each (`dress.<name>` takes its box from kit.json, `part:<w u>,<d u>,<h m>` an explicit one). The small dressing sheet (8 parts, 30 credits) came back as 8 clean parts of 64k to 190k faces each, so a sheet of small objects can hold more. Split into a scratch folder, not the repo: the parts are rebuilt from the committed sheet (`*-parts/` folders are gitignored) |
| `banners.py` | Cuts the Codex banner sheet (`textures/src/banners.png`, seven element banners on magenta) into `textures/banner-<emblem>.png`; the renderer hangs each as a cut-out from a pole (`dress.banner`, its `parts.emblem` named on the vignette) |
| `slim.py` | After `fit.py`: copies each model from `meshy/kit-full/` to `meshy/kit/` (what the game loads) with smaller textures (JPEG; base colour 2048 px for landmark buildings, 1024 px otherwise; normal and metal-roughness half) |
| `trace_plan.py` | `python tools/kit/trace_plan.py court-d court` re-runs a place plan (`tools/place-plans/plans/`) on the kit's corner lattice and writes a first-draft scene, plus `<scene>.gaps.json`: what the kit can't say yet (set pieces, facades, curved flights). A one-off draft: after it, the scene is the source |
| `court_pins.py` | The courtyard's gaps as map-editor pins (`scenes/court.pins.json`), named by hand |
| `layouts/*.py` | The first two test patches as written by hand, kept as history; their scenes are the live copies |

## The scene format

```json
{
  "schema": 1, "name": "patch-b", "G": 2, "notes": "free text",
  "terraces": [
    {"id": "t-plaza", "glyph": "P", "name": "the plaza", "h": 0, "mat": "plaza", "edge": "parapet"},
    {"id": "t-cliff-below", "glyph": "C", "name": "the cliff below", "h": -4, "mat": "rock", "edge": "open", "walk": false}
  ],
  "corners": ["CPPP", "CPPP", "CCCC"],
  "objects": [
    {"id": "f1", "kind": "flight", "name": "the grand stair", "head": [[13, 8.5], [16, 8.5]]},
    {"id": "c1", "kind": "curve", "name": "the fountain bastion", "terrace": "t-fountain-landing", "centre": [24, 13.5], "r": 2, "ends": [[22, 13.5], [26, 13.5]], "out": "S"},
    {"id": "e1", "kind": "edge", "name": "the hall door", "at": [15.5, 2.5], "v": "door"},
    {"id": "v1", "kind": "vignette", "piece": "vignette.lamp", "at": [10, 13.5], "front": "S"}
  ]
}
```

- **Coordinates** are corners: corner `(x, y)` is `corners[y][x]`. Cells lie between corners; edges sit on half-coordinates.
- **Kerbs** are drawn as blocks (`docs/explorations/2026-11-24-road-stones.md`): `game/packages/client/src/lab/kit-road.ts` joins a scene's kerb pieces into lines and lays each as one row of rounded blocks, mitred where it turns. Road and paving floors are laid stone by stone: `tess_layout.py` (run by `compile.py` after each compile, skipped when nothing it lays changed) writes `game/content/generated/layouts/<scene>-stones.json`, and the floor shader paints it (`kit-stones.ts`). `tess_proof.py` holds the pattern's code and draws a still of it; `python tools/kit/stones.py <sheet.png> <name>` cuts a Codex sheet of separate stones on magenta into the atlas it takes its grain from (`textures/src/stones-<name>.png`, listed in `textures/src/stones.json`). **Weathered paving** (`mat: weathered`, the infirmary since 2026-11-27) is laid the same way with mixed flags and cobbles in wide joints, its grain from its own Codex sheet (`stones-weathered`, prompts in `tools/concept-gen/prompts/2026-11-27-weathered/`): `tess_layout.py` (`WEAR`) gives each stone a wear from how near it lies to lawn and from noise, and leaves stones out (kept as holes) along the lawn, in patches of trodden earth and a few anywhere; the shader (`WEATHERED` in `kit-stones.ts`) chips and stains them, grows grass and moss in the joints and over the rims, shows the floor's own tile (`weathered.png`, packed earth) where stones are missing, and goes over to the lawn's own texture toward the lawn, so the edge has no seam. Road and cobble look as before.
- **Terraces:** `h` in metres; `mat` is the floor (paving, plaza, road, lawn, roof, rock, water, deck, woods, dirt, sand); a floor wears the tiling texture named after its mat (`game/content/generated/textures/`, tile sizes in `tiles.json`, made by `textures.py`); `edge` is how the terrace meets lower ground: `wall` (a building), `parapet` (a balustrade on a retaining wall), `retain` (a retaining wall), `open` (a drop left open, as for a cliff), `steps` (a drop of 0.3 to 1 m you can step over anywhere along it, as round a dais; over 1 m it is a parapet). A drop of 0.3 m or less is a kerb whatever the edge, and two levels at the same height meet in a seam with no edge piece. Steps also go wherever a lower `steps` level meets a walkable level at most 1 m above it. `walk: false` for ground nobody walks on (beds, roofs, cliffs). Each corner character is a terrace's `glyph`; its `id` is what everything else refers to.
- **Flights:** `head` is the line the flight starts from, along a terrace's edge (a half-coordinate). Its top, bottom and direction come from the corners either side, and its length from the drop (0.15 m per step).
- **Curves:** a terrace bulging past its grid edge along a circle from `ends[0]` to `ends[1]` round `centre`, bulging toward `out`.
- **Rounds:** `{"kind": "round", "centre": [x, y], "r": cells, "tol"?: 0.75, "terrace"?: id, "from"?: deg, "to"?: deg}` says a circle is a true curve. Every terrace edge lying within `tol` of it (runs of three or more segments; `terrace` limits it to one level's edges, `from`/`to` to an arc, 0 = east, 90 = south) is snapped onto the arc, convex or concave, and the floors either side are re-cut. Paint the grid roughly round; the round makes it true. A curve, by contrast, bulges one straight run.
- **Set pieces:** `{"kind": "setpiece", "piece": "setpiece.bell-tower", "at": [x, y], "front": "S"}`: one unique model on a footprint (a landmark, a tower, a gate pier), from `kit.json`'s `setpiece` class (`shape` round or box, `footprint` in u, `height` in m). Like a vignette, but it may stand on ground within 0.35 m (its plinth takes up a kerb) and may flank a flight, never stand in its path. A set piece marked `"paving": "edge"` in `kit.json` (the buildings and towers) is an edge for the laid stones: `tess_layout.py` lays the paving up to its model's base (`model_base.py`) and not under it.
- **Edge overrides:** `at` is the midpoint of one straight edge section; `v` is `plain`, `window` or `door` on a wall, `plain` or `niche` on a parapet or retaining wall. Without one, the compiler chooses (windows every other bay, by position; niches on tall walls seen from walkable ground). **Arches** are built to size by the renderer, never modelled: blind arches on tall walls rising from ground no one walks (the sea), each over a bay of three sections (the middle `v: 'blind'` with `bay: 3`, its neighbours `v: 'blind-side'`), a plain section between bays; and `arch.through`, wherever a walkable level's two parallel faces stand over the same water that the level divides in two (a bridge: one arch per 6 m of span; the faces' sections there draw only coping and railing, `v: 'bridge'`). Banks facing across water and piers are not crossings. A crossing less than 1.2 m above its water is a warning (`arch-low`).
- **Falls** are painted, not placed: wherever a level of `water` meets a lower level of `water` across an edge, with a drop of 0.5 m or more, the compiler emits one `fall.curtain` per unbroken run of that edge (`pts`: the lip's line in u, the lower water on its right; `h` and `low`: the two waters), and that edge gets no wall or parapet whatever the level's `edge` says. The renderer draws it to size (`kit-fall.ts`: a ribbon over the lip, foam and puffs at its foot), and the upper water flows toward the lip. Two falls chain through a pond painted at a height between (`scenes/patch-falls.scene.json` has a tall fall, a short one, a cascade and a lip that turns a corner).
- **Cliffs** are found, not placed: every `open` edge dropping 2 m or more (`CLIFF_MIN`) onto water, or the plinth, rock or sand at its foot, becomes one `cliff.face` per unbroken run (`pts`, the sea on its right; `h`, `low`). The renderer (`cliffFace` in `kit-render.ts`) lays the cliff kit's run (`meshy/kit/cliff-run.glb`, one Meshy model from `kit-src/court/sheet-cliff.png`) along each straight stretch: stretched to fill it, scaled to the drop, its depth held to 3.2 u, every other copy mirrored, each stretch carried past its ends so outside corners close.
- **Quays:** a `deck` floor over water gets a fascia beam, piles every 2.4 m along its water edges and mooring posts at its corners, drawn to size in the deck's paint; the quay props (`dress.bollard`, `dress.hook-lamp`, `dress.crates`, `dress.barrel`, `dress.rope-coil`, `dress.mooring-post`, `dress.mooring-ring`) come from one bundled Meshy call (`kit-src/court/sheet-quay.png`).
- **Inlays:** a set piece whose `kit.json` class is `inlay` (`setpiece.plaza-inlay`) is a stone slab set into its floor, `height` m proud inside a `rim` [width u, height m], with recessed `channels` cut into it (`w` u, `depth` m, radial `runs` `{a, from, to, tint}` or lines `{pts}`) filled with a painted `texture` (magic-flow) scrolled along each channel at `speed` u/s, tinted per run, glowing by `glow`. Built by `game/packages/client/src/lab/kit-inlay.ts`; the scene's decal paints its top. Dress pieces keep off it.
- **Grown plants:** a set piece whose `kit.json` entry has `"grow": {"kind": "bush" / "bed" / "cone" / "tree", ...}` is grown in code (`game/packages/client/src/lab/foliage.ts`, sizes in metres; `box: [w, d, h]` adds a stone trough under it) instead of loaded as a model: `setpiece.bush-gold`, `setpiece.flowerbed`, `setpiece.topiary`, `setpiece.tree-bloom` and their kin. Each differs by where it stands. A `vine.*` piece with `"grow": {"kind": "cascade"}` is a grown vine as wide as its wall section.
- **Pipes** are drawn to size, not modelled: `{"kind": "pipe", "id", "name", "pts": [[x, y] or [x, y, z], ...], "h"?: 0.5, "r"?: 0.22}`, its route in corners. A point with `z` is at that absolute height (m), so two points with the same `x, y` make a vertical run (down a wall's face: stand it a little off the face, about 0.15 to 0.2 cells, past the coping); without it, the point sits `h` m above the floor there. A pipe end on another pipe's run makes a T-junction there; an end on (or in) a set piece's or vignette's footprint stops at it (the run is cut at its face: it plugs in); any other end is an open mouth. The compiler emits one `pipe.run` per pipe (`pts` resolved to `[x u, y u, z m]`, `ends`: open, plug or tee, `tees` with the main's point and direction, `saddles` `[x u, y u, floor m, z m, angle]` every 3 u under each level run where the floor is within 1.2 m), and the renderer (`game/packages/client/src/lab/kit-pipe.ts`) draws a brass tube with short rounded bends, riveted collars at its ends, joints and every 2.5 u, a blue enamel band with the academy's eight-point star every 5 u or so, a collar on the main at each T and sandstone saddles, all in Codex-painted textures (`brass`, `pipe-collar`, `pipe-band`; prompts in `tools/concept-gen/prompts/2026-11-26-pipes/`; the saddles in the smooth stone). Pipes are dressing: the room the sim walks on ignores them, so they block no one for now. `scenes/patch-pipes.scene.json` has corners, a T, a climb over a parapet and down a 3 m wall, an open end and an end in a set piece (views in `scenes/refs/patch-pipes/`).
- **Markers:** `{"kind": "marker", "id": "gate-yard", "at": [x, y], "tag"?: "landmark", "name"?: "..."}`: a named spot for the game (`at` in corners), drawn as nothing. `start-0` to `start-3` are the seats' starts; `dummy-0`, `dummy-1` ... the room's practice dummies; `gate-*` lead out of the room. `room.py` reads them; `compile.py` ignores them.
- **Vignettes:** a prop from `kit.json` (`vignette.lamp`, `vignette.bench-planter`) at `at`, facing `front` (N, E, S or W); a `dress.*` piece placed by hand may name its `parts` (a banner's `{"emblem": "fire"}`) and a `turn`.
- **Dressing styles** (`kit.json` `styles`, applied by a `dress` object): rules put pieces on pier caps (`on`), at flights' heads and feet, along roads (`along: road`, lamps), on walls (`drape`, `grow`: vines) and behind parapets over a drop (`along: view`: benches facing out, `edges`, `min_drop`, `spacing`, `inset`).
- The file is written one terrace, corner row or object per line (`scene.py` and the editor's server write the same layout), so git diffs stay readable.

## Findings

Every check reports `{code, severity, msg, at, refs}`: `at` is where (in corners), `refs` the ids involved. Errors: `saddle` (two opposite corners of a cell on a terrace and two not), `flight-head`, `flight-landing`, `flight-overlap`, `curve-ends`, `unguarded` (a drop over 1 m without a parapet), `run-end` (a railing or wall run stopping in the air), `vignette-ground`, `vignette-flight`, `unreachable`, `piece`, `piece-fit`, `pipe-point` (a pipe's point off the map, over no floor without a `z`, or fewer than two), `pipe-wall` (a run through a building below its roof, away from the pipe's ends), `pipe-setpiece` (a run through a set piece other than where an end plugs into it). Warnings: `flight-runout` (no clear ground in front of a flight's foot), `round` (a round no edge lies along), `edge-variant`, `edge-missing`, `pipe-float` (a run over one floor more than 1 m above it, away from its ends; a short hop over a drop is fine), `pipe-sink` (a run dipping into its floor). A scene with errors still compiles as far as it can.
