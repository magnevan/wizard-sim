# Story notes: Your Go, round 1

## Notes, most important first

1. **Nobody ever wrongs you.** After the lunch-break chalk line at Plumb, the Dunnocks are only lovely. A rival you merely enjoy stops pulling around hour 10. Give them small, real offences: Ines correcting your chalk in public, Bet retaking a line the day after you beat it. Stay petty, clear of ban 26. You should want to beat them before you miss them.

2. **Retry rigs the question, and Bet's departure comes from nowhere.** "Retry as often as you need" guarantees the Twice win, so "are they better?" has no real answer. The posting is unplanted. Chalk a running score at every spar, never reset, tries counted. Put the offer in plain sight: Sloe carries the same unanswered letter for her, region after region. Every loss has been keeping her here. Then *That's one to me* is an actual score.

3. **Longlegs' guilt evaporates.** "It could have been yours or Ines's" is the best line in the plan and never returns. The forty homes have no face, and "every chair full" lets everyone off. Ines studies fights, so she would know whose bolt it was. Let her theories drift from ribs toward the bolt, unanswered. Give one Longlegs household that skips the last supper.

4. **Three chairs are one beat.** Hob, Dov and Ines each find a place and stay. By Ines the player has it, and hers is thinnest. Let one leave badly, or have someone ask *you* to stay. Everyone finds somewhere except you, and the plan never touches that.

5. **The paired-teachers reveal stops at the premise.** "They stayed" changes nothing, and Mr Sedge is missing from the ending. He is the other way this rivalry can go: a lifetime of draughts instead of a ship. Pay his want: one win, possibly thrown, exactly what Bet refused to accept.

6. **The countdown is never set.** The road promises one, but the surge first appears in the ending. Set it at the Cut: a mark on a pile, a date Nan and Gaffer word differently.
7. **The Rucks follows the climax with only comedy.** Twice takes the last chair, then a boarding house, and the empty slate gets one line. The Rucks has no stated supper. Loser lays the table: you lay it, alone.

8. **The last spell should obey the first rule.** Anything a Dunnock beats you with goes in the copybook. The river queue should be its final entry.

## Do not touch

- The ending from *Your go* onward, including that a player who can read runes knows what it does and casts it anyway.
- The slates, and Bet's lines freezing forever.
- The burned hand returning her harder to beat.

## Ban list

- Near 32: supper impressions. Keep them mimicry, never a tally read aloud.
- Near 49: the strike timetable naming you as a delay.
- Watch 38: Ines's ribs must never resolve.
