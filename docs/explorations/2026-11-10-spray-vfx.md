# Spray effects, redone (2026-11-10)

The first pass of a spell-effects review. The user's notes on the three sprays (right click with Water, Fire or Cold):

- **General:** more runes should look like an upgrade: larger, hotter or more intense, more particles. A five-rune spell should give some awe.
- **Water:** the middle of the spray looks static, and adding Water barely changes it: the cone grows a little, and the push doesn't show.
- **Frost:** the middle looks static, a ground patch at the tip is dragged around as you turn, and more runes don't look impressive.
- **Fire:** the middle is static too; use the defence's flamethrower from the opening (they should look the same anyway).

## What was wrong underneath

- The drawn cone was a fixed 16° half-angle whatever the runes, but the sim's hit cone is 32° and grows 25 % a rune, to 65° at five (`effects.ts` channelTick). Reach grows from 4.5 to 6.5 tiles. So most of the real growth never showed, and the spray hit things it didn't seem to touch.
- The middle was three jittering lines fixed to the staff and the aim, so it looked like a picture laid over the floor.
- Every particle was placed from the current aim, so anything at the end (the frost patch) swung round with the staff.

## The redesign (`game/packages/client/src/art/spray-jet.ts`)

All three sprays now share the turret flamethrower's model: each particle has a ground spot and a height, leaves the staff at its height and comes down toward chest height. Three changes on top of that:

1. **Each particle keeps the aim it was born with.** The view keeps a short history of each spray's aim (`AimTrail`), so a sweep bends the stream like a hose and the old flame trails behind. What lands, like droplets and settling frost, stays where it fell.
2. **The fan is the sim's fan.** The drawn half-angle and reach are the hit cone's. Particles crowd the middle, so the edges read as thin spray.
3. **Runes show.** The form's runes (1 to 5) raise density, speed, size and heat, and the top tiers add their own features:

| Runes | Water | Fire | Frost |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | a thin rope that frays into droplet streaks | the turret's jet: white core, cooling to red, smoke along the far two thirds | a pale breath, wind streaks, glitter, mist |
| 2–3 | a thicker rope, more droplets, mist over the far half | wider and denser, hotter | ice shards fly point first from 3 |
| 4 | a 3 px rope; pressure rings ride it out | the root burns blue-white | thicker gale |
| 5 | blowback spatters round the caster; a rainbow hangs in the mist | fireballs roll up out of the far end; an ember shower | snowflakes tumble in it; a frost ring pulses off the staff |

The light the spray casts also grows with its runes.

Frost's ground patch at the tip is gone. Frost now settles in specks where the gale actually blew, and they stay put and melt away.

Doubles, Echoes and Sigil turrets use the same jet, thinner. The healing sprays (Life + Water, Life + Fire) keep their old cones. A defence's flamethrower in the opening keeps its own `flame-jet.ts` for now: it lives on the opening-segments branch. Once both are merged, it could be drawn by `sprayJet` too.

## The Spray Lab

`game/packages/client/spray-lab.html` (dev server only, never built) draws every spray at one to five runes side by side, with the game's own code at the game's scale. `?old=1` puts the old cone beside each, `?sweep=1` swings the aim, `?hit=1` dots the sim's hit sector, `?t=1.3` freezes the clock for screenshots.

## Open

- Is a 65° fan at five runes right? It's what the sim hits, so the drawing follows it. If it reads too wide, the sim's angle should change, not just the drawing.
- Fire at five is a wall of flame between the caster and the target. Is that awe or clutter in a crowded fight?
- The water rainbow is the quietest five-rune touch; it may need more presence, or a different idea.

## Round 2: the user's notes on the sprays, and beams and the rock

The user played the sprays:
- **Frost:** perfect, no notes.
- **Water:** very good, but the central jet is too strong against the spray, more so with more runes. The rainbow looked like a static sprite hanging in the air. If there's a rainbow, it should be subtler, like droplets glimmering with colour in parts of the spray.
- **Fire:** very good, but each flame particle looked too round and distinct. The blue-white root didn't show, and the fireballs looked like odd spheres bubbling up.
- **The 65° fan feels right.**

Changes:
- Water's jet is at most 2 px thick, frays as early whatever the runes, and has fewer white surges, so the droplets carry the look. The rainbow sprite is gone. At five runes, droplets crossing a slowly drifting band of the fan glint in prism colours.
- Fire's flames are soft smears: a dithered body stretched back along each flame's path, with a solid heart. The fireballs are gone; five runes throws a heavier shower of embers instead. From four runes, a blue-white inner flame burns at the staff, drawn over the rest.

### Beams (Life, Arcane)

The notes: the range should be much longer, so the beam just runs until it hits something. More runes should look more intense. More runes didn't heal or hurt faster either.

- **Range:** 30 tiles, past the edge of the view.
- **Damage and healing:** this was a logged rule ("beams last n× longer instead of hitting harder", the rune scaling audit). It now splits the runes into power and time, √n each, so a whole beam is still worth n runes, but a 4-rune beam hits twice as hard. Logged in `decisions/README.md`.
- **Look** (`K.beamSurge`, tier = all the spell's runes, so Arcane + Fire + Fire is above Arcane + Fire): thicker with each rune; bright surges race from the staff to the end, more and longer with more runes; sparks shed off the sides from three; a halo along the length from three; a white-hot core from four; rings pulsing off the staff at five; a bigger splash where it hits. The Spray Lab shows them: `?kinds=arcane-beam,life-beam,fire-beam`.

### The Earth rock

The note: it moves but doesn't feel like it's flying, a little too slow, and needs a trail.

- A fully charged rock flies at 16 u/s, up from 12; a quick one at 60 % of that.
- In flight it tumbles, faster when it's fast, with the light kept on its upper left so the turning reads. Three air streaks trail off its edges, a wake of dust hangs where it passed, and grit falls out of it. Ice, mud and other rocks get the wake in their own colours.
- Not seen mid-flight in a screenshot: headless Chrome stalls on some frames of the Earth demo (it did before this change too), so only the launch frame was checked.
