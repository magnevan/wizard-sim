# Before All the People

To attune at each great well you give your word before the people who live on it, and the circuit becomes the long road back to pay them, until what you have sworn to one people you can keep only by failing another.

## The question

**When the circuit is walked, which gates will open to you?**

The last road of the game answers it literally, passing every town you swore to, each gate as your deeds left it.

## The opening

The school stands on a tidal islet in the bay beneath the southern badlands, on an Arcane vein. On your last night a boat is lost beyond the bar, and a shore-mother asks you on the school steps to bring it home. You swear aloud. A hazel stick is notched and split between you. The causeway, dry at first and then drowning, teaches the queue, the conjured weapon and the danger of your friends. At dawn both halves burn on the quay. Any opening ending in one burned stick may replace this.

## The road

**How the word is given.** The wells stand on common ground and belong to those who live around them. They have always admitted journeymen, because a wizard in their debt is the only wizard a village can hope for. No magic binds an oath, and no attunement can be taken back. There is only what you said, before all the people.

- **Three records.** *Kept:* both halves burn at the hearth, and gates and hearths open. *Kept to the letter:* your half is given back unburned, the gate opens, and no one asks you in. *Broken:* the town nails its half to the gate.
- **Wording.** Three great oaths are offered in two wordings; the host chooses, or a quick vote. The generous wording opens that town's keeping shrine on the day you swear. The narrow one waits until the debt is paid, and leaves room later.
- **Instalments.** Each great oath has a smaller due on return. At most two stand open at once. A well taken while one is open cuts a notch: the debt grows by one encounter, and that town's shrine closes until it is paid. The journeymen's boats make any region one crossing away.
- **Small oaths.** Two or three to a village across some fifteen villages, built from the shrine kits as restrictions and tasks: no Lightning in the mill valley, for the sake of a goose of great age. Each village has one face and one gate.
- **Power.** Only the ford and the last Hearing split the paths, and each offers the same total power either way.

Each attunement grants a regen step and deepens the pool you choose. The order is fixed, for debts must be scheduled. Return paths accept two answers or a regen step.

### 1. Ighrem n'Aman (Earth), the mesas

- **Place:** a fortress, its cisterns cut so deep that stars show in the water at noon.
- **Oath:** *"When the cisterns fail, bring water to us,"* or *"bring us to water."* They have not failed in two hundred years, and the people cheer as at a wedding.
- **Exam:** drive the burrowers from the well shaft.
- **Growth:** walls you can afford to keep raising in a long fight.
- **The beat:** Tiziri lets down the plumb line and reads the depth aloud. Below the fortress, children play on a ruined stair running north toward the fen, carved with the names of those who climbed it.
- **On return:** the reading is painted on the lift wall, lower at each visit. Rain banked in the cisterns is the instalment and raises the number. Tiziri says at once what Rain can do: it buys seasons, and will never fill the cisterns.

### 2. Aukmedis (Life), the great forest

- **Place:** a tree so vast it makes its own rain. The parish lays its dead among the root-halls, and from each grave a green shoot rises the same spring.
- **Oath:** *"Never bring fire past the ford, and lay Shushan at the roots when her day comes."* A Fire spell in ford country counts only if something is still burning and someone could see it. If nobody saw it burning, you never did it.
- **Exam:** escort the root-tenders through the halls, against pests that beg to be burned.
- **Growth:** healing you can sustain between fights.
- **The beat:** Shushan, eighty years old, holds the ford and searches your pockets for flint. Hers is a southern name among the people of the tree.
- **On return:** she is slower each time and misses nothing. The instalment is a nest turned out of the root-halls without flame.

### 3. Ulan Khalga (Lightning), the red badlands

- **Place:** a great arch of stone. On storm nights a single bolt stands in the span for minutes together, and the herders swear by its light.
- **Oath:** *"Stand for our side when the boundary is heard."* The boundary is the summer pasture against Aukmedis, to whom you swore last season.
- **Exam:** cross the open ground in storm, among swarms that chain.
- **Growth:** chains you can repeat.
- **Encounters:** steppe ambushes, and your first enemy casters, claim-jumpers fighting for a living.
- **The beat:** Odval explains the Hearing. Each side's sworn wizard stands on the line beneath the bolt until one has stepped off. The herders' gate bears a nailed stick in the name of your classmate Ilarion Kask, the first broken word you see.
- **On return:** the first due is a wedding. By herders' custom the two families' guests assail one another with all they possess until the bride's mother cries peace, and it is held a discourtesy to leave a friend unburnt. It is the happiest day of the circuit. The bride sets you alight, and the chronicle records it.

### 4. Rawa Tanggul (Water), the fen

- **Place:** a city on stilts behind a dyke raised by more forebears than anyone can name.
- **Oath:** *"Come back and hold the dyke when the spring melt comes,"* or *"come back and stand on the dyke."*
- **Exam:** a flooded gauntlet that teaches conduction without mercy.
- **Growth:** Rain you can keep falling across a whole encounter.
- **Encounters:** amphibious packs, a native boss in the great sluice.
- **The beat:** the people walk you the length of the dyke, each family naming its stretch. On the oldest piles are the names from the mesa's stair.
- **On return:** by the melt, the mesa's first households are camped outside the dyke, in twos and threes.

### 5. Krakadzor (Fire), the volcanic south

- **Place:** a city of smiths cut into the crater's inner wall. Its dead go to the vent. They hold that those left unburned go on walking home.
- **Oath:** Zabel, Shushan's sister, asks it: *"Bring our sister home to the fire."*
- **Exam:** cross the caldera floor between two breathings of the lava, on a crust your own Fire will break.
- **Growth:** burning you can maintain.
- **The beat:** Zabel shows you fifty years of gifts laid by for a homecoming. You carry Zabel's half to the ford on your belt. Shushan's hands go through your pockets, find it, read the notches, and put it back. She lets you pass.
- **On return:** the instalment is one more length of the crater road cleared for a woman who has not yet come.

### 6. Minar-e Shab (Arcane), the warped east

- **Place:** a black tower hanging unsupported above the well, its shadow falling toward the sun. Each year someone must climb and watch one night. No chronicle says what is watched. Each year a child is chosen by lot.
- **Oath:** *"Stand this year's watch in place of Golnar."*
- **Exam:** the ascent through rooms that hold your own footprints ahead of you, then the night's defence of the topmost chamber.
- **Growth:** the largest regen step in the game, enough to hold the deep beam.
- **Encounters:** strange things, few and terrible; masterful enemy casters.
- **The beat:** you come down at dawn and a full year has passed. Golnar, a head taller, meets you at the foot of the tower. She has had the year you spent.
- **On return:** the instalment is clearing the lower rooms before the next child climbs.

### 7. Davgi (Cold), the ice

- **Place:** the ribs of something no singer has named rise from the ice higher than any tower built by hands, and a people live in their shelter. For centuries their singers have held every oath of the circuit by heart. Claims too great for any arch are heard here, on the ice between the ribs.
- **Oath:** *"Whatever you decide, you will say it aloud before the singers, and change no word of what they sing."*
- **Exam:** a long ice road against large and patient things.
- **Growth:** ice and shatter you can use twice in any fight.
- **The beat:** Máret sings you the circuit so far, each oath in your own wording.

**Optional: the crystal island, north-west.** Wizards who swore never to swear again teach Shield's wider arts, the will to refuse.

## The turns

**The midpoint, and the opening of Sigil.** The melt and the Hearing fall due in one week at opposite ends of the world. There is one boat and one party.

On the voyage you put in at Sev Karer, the forest of black columns, where the cutters carve stones that go on working when the carver has gone home. Hovik sets you a trial against a small practice flood. The full art opens, and your Sigil pool rises to three: turrets, glyphs, Blink, auras. *"You have shaped what you hold. Shape the wall."*

Floes ram the dyke and eel-packs climb the breaches. Only carved work stays, so turrets take the packs and Earth and Cold go in as glyph payloads against the ice. The stretches scale to the devices you hold. Now you choose.

**Stay.** You fight the whole melt at the families' sides, the greatest battle of the first half, and every house stands. The stick burns and every hearth opens. The herders' half is nailed up, and the parish's flocks graze their pasture bare.

**Sail.** You build first, family by family. Every device you leave holds a Sigil rune from you, so whatever you give the dyke is missing from your duel. At the Hearing you face Ilarion Kask, sworn to Aukmedis and glad to see you. The party holds the line until the last of you steps off it, and the oath is kept win or lose. Win, and the parish loses its summer grazing.

Then you sail back for the last night and find the dyke exactly as your layout left it. Stretches that failed cost those families their houses, visibly. If too many fell, or if you swore to stand on the dyke, the stick is nailed. If enough held, Kemala Rawi gives you back your half unburned. She says only, "You left."

The boatmen set the price of the warped river, the only way east: *"No one shall settle the dyke lands,"* or *"no one shall settle them against the will of those who hold them."* You have seen the camps. You have read the names on the stair. You swear, or you never reach the sixth well.

**The late turn: Shushan's day.** You come down from the tower into the year you lost. Every open stick has taken a notch. Shushan has died. She lies in the root-halls through the forty days of watching, and Krakadzor's fire-bearers are on the road.

- **Carry her home.** Escort her bier through a parish that bars your way. You swear on the bank to shed no parish blood, so everyone you fell must be raised by Life before you may cross. The ford's gate takes the stick, and the tree roads close. At the vent, Zabel opens the gifts and with them fifty years of letters from Shushan, each promising to come home next spring. To the parish, she swore she never would. This is learned only here.
- **Lay her at the roots.** The fire-bearers and their sworn casters come to the ford with torches. You hold it without Fire, for you swore that also. Krakadzor's gate takes the stick, the upper halls open, and in spring a shoot rises.

## The ending

Ighrem's reading comes to nothing. Two thousand people go down the old stair and walk north, carrying their children, and halt at the fen's border stones. Kemala gives the numbers once: the dyke lands feed so many, and a third more would starve every household in a bad year. Neither people lies. A claim so great can be heard in one place only.

You go north with the sworn of both peoples. On the ice at Davgi you step to the line and say aloud, before the singers, whom you stand with.

**The last Hearing** is fought across the seven spans between the ribs. The line moves one span with each side driven back. Every caster across the line uses your own language, and you know their faces.

- **Your deeds decide the field.** Each great town you kept sends its sworn to your side, and each removes one threat from one span. Those you failed stand across from you.
- **Ilarion Kask** holds the last span. They swore to both peoples and stayed with the one you left.
- **Banked Rain** decides how many of the column reached the stones with strength to go on, whichever way the verdict falls.

**If you stand with Rawa Tanggul,** the column is turned toward the southern strand. If you swore *"bring us to water,"* you may walk them there yourself, and Ighrem gives your half back unburned. **If you stand with the mesa-folk,** they are brought in, and the old families grow crowded and hungry. If you swore *"against the will of those who hold them,"* and enough families whose houses stood give leave, the city gives your half back unburned. Failing that, the stick is nailed. One stick burns and one never does. The emptied cisterns of Ighrem open as a deep new road.

**The singing** is played. You stand on the ice, and verse by verse every oath is sung to the faces it was said to, in your wording, with what came of it. Each town answers by burning its half, or holding it up. "You left" is sung to the city that heard it. At the ford, the singer records only that nobody saw anything, and the parish looks at you. The wedding is sung, and both peoples laugh, and for that verse no one is on any side. At any verse you may stop the song. That verse goes unsung, and Davgi nails your stick.

Then comes the long road home, past each gate.

**Last image:** you stop at the closed place of the people you chose against, a walkway drawn in or a rope across the strand path. A shutter opens. It belongs to the family whose stretch of the dyke held, or whose terrace you filled with Rain. They look at you for a long while. They set out a bowl on the last post, and the shutter closes. If you left them nothing to be grateful for, the shutter stays closed. You eat, or you do not, and you go on with the unburned half at your belt.

## The recurring faces

- **Máret, singer of Davgi.** Hears everything once. Walks the circuit behind you to get your words exact.
- **Ilarion Kask, of your class.** Gives their word as others give greetings. Wants to be loved by everyone.
- **Shushan, at the ford.** Her hands go through every pocket. Wants to be spared the choosing.
- **Tiziri n'Aman, of the cisterns.** Thirty-one years at the plumb line. Wants the last reading to fall to someone else.

## Why this is fun to play

You return to places where you were weak and settle in moments of well-chosen runes what you once swore to settle someday. Your oaths reach into your hands: a ford held against torches with Fire forbidden, a friend's mistake doused before anyone can see it, a wedding where setting your comrades alight is good manners. Each rune you leave on the dyke is one you lack in the duel, so preparation and promise are one decision.

## The biggest weakness

The seven great oaths are required, and a sharp player will see the last Hearing coming hours ahead and may resent being made to swear. Foresight changes how the losers lose and never who loses.
