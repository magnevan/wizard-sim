## What they add up to (my read, for this thread to test)

1. **Not enough is happening.** Every build is too still and too sparse: one shape doing one thing. The pixel effects, which the user rated better, had many small moving parts. Effects need rapid secondary motion: sparks, licks, crackle, things flung off, flicker.
2. **No sprite clusters, and no opaque solids for mediums.** Main bodies were made meshes to fix the first fire, and became cloth and tubes: opaque, smooth, slow. Fire, frost and dust need translucency, layering and churn.
3. **Impacts must answer what was hit.** An impact drawn as a decal at the end point reads as a stamp. It should come from the hit: sparks spraying off the struck surface, a wall chipping, a body reacting. It should be oriented by the direction of travel and the surface, and different for ground, wall and body.
4. **Contrast on pale stone** (from the concept round): each effect brings a white or a dark the court's warm floor lacks.
5. **Sizes and shapes come from the sim**: hit shapes stay true. Painted concepts show the mood, not the dimensions.
6. **Judge in motion, at the game camera, in the court.** Stills and lab paving hid every one of these problems.

The [study](../explorations/2026-11-25-spell-effects-study.md) tested these against the pixel versions and found the biggest gap none of them named: **half the pixel effect is on the bodies it hits** (a white flash, then the element's status drawn on the body; thrown bodies tumble). The 3D models get only a status tint. After that come grain inside the body, stepped flicker at 7 to 30 Hz, full saturation and opacity, and size; the raw amount of motion on screen is about the same. Its method (seven rules) is how every build in this thread is made and checked; it revises the tone round's draft rules 4 (floor light small) and 5 (thin sizes).

## How to work here

- **Machine budget:** the user's machine maxed out with four agents rendering clips at once (2026-11-25), and one agent's server was stopped for low memory. Run at most two heavy agents (dev servers, Chrome, clips) at a time.
- **The review page is [Spell Effects Review](https://claude.ai/artifact/Kwh9yrDCTVmnXQRcHQ6vxT)** (`tools/spell-fx-review/`, from 2026-11-26): one round per page, a new round appended to `rounds.js`; answers `<round>-<item>` in its db `answers`. Read it before each round. The Court in 3D holds rounds 1 to 16.
- **Batches with clips:** one review page per batch, with looping clips (court at 30 u, lab at 1 and 5 runes) and a keep / redo box per effect, as on The Court in 3D. Read its db before each round.
- **Concept art is free** (Codex; see `tools/concept-gen/README.md`), and so are motion references: the pixel version in `vfx-lab.html`, and the reference games in `docs/reference/`.
- **Spell rules don't change here.** Shapes, sizes and timings are the sim's. A look that needs a different hit shape is a separate design question for the user.

### The work order (the user, 2026-11-26)

> "One element at a time (excl sigil, shield), so we can lock down that elements visual identity. Some visual elements per shape can be re-used across spells. Then cover rune combinations once we know the visual identity across spells, do this per shape instead. Lastly we can go over sigil and shield, since these have more unique presentations."

This replaces the shape-first order ("every other family in batches: sprays, beams, missiles…"). The reason: a family batch builds Water's spray beside Fire's, so each element gets settled one shape at a time and never as a whole. Combinations can't be judged until each element they mix is settled (the look follows the carried element, and the payload borrows from each element's identity). Shield and Sigil are mostly structures and devices, not elemental mediums.

**How each element pass runs.**
- **One element, five casts, plus what it leaves on bodies.** Every pure element has a Force, a Ring, a Place, a Self and a burst (Shift+LMB). The pass also covers its status on a body, its aftermath (residue that is duller, cooler and slower than the live effect, per `fx/phase.ts`) and its impacts on ground, wall and body.
- **Concept art first, as a breakdown** (lessons, section 2): one storyboard per cast (start, run, end, aftermath at the game camera) and one parts sheet for the whole element (each building block alone, with its life and motion). The parts sheet *is* the element's identity, and every cast is made from it.
- **Split the shape from the look.** Each cast is two halves: the **shape** (its footprint, timing and phases from the sim: the raindrop, the expanding ring, the placed patch, the body aura, the burst cone) and the **look** (the element's parts: textures, palette, motion). The first element to need a shape builds it as a shared module in `view3d/fx/` (the ring from `cold.ts`'s blast wave, the burst, the placed patch, the self aura), and later elements only supply their parts. So the first pass costs the most and the later ones should go faster.
- **One review page per element**, with clips of every cast (the court at 30 u; the lab at 1 and 5 runes; the sparse scene for reactions) and a keep / tune / redo box per cast, plus one box for the identity as a whole.
- **It closes with an identity card** in the spell-look guide: palette, materials, the motion signature, its residue and its status, with the user's verdict. Combinations (phase 2) read from these cards.

**Phase 1: elements, in this order.** Fire first (the user's pick). Then the three with an anchor already built. Then the three without one. Ice is last because its look borrows from Cold and Water.
