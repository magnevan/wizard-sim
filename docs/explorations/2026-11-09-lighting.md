# Lighting, and what the research said (2026-11-09)

The user asked for lighting work while unable to review, after finding the look "a bit intense", and asked Claude to research techniques rather than work from memory. Built behind the isometric camera (`?camera=iso`), so the public game is unchanged. The page with the moods side by side is the [Opening Blueprint](https://claude.ai/artifact/22rU6WpjmMRHrToc5YbiPi).

## What a player sees

A room has a mood. The Sanctum is day, Survive and the Arena dusk, the Academy indoors. Things cast flat, hard-edged shadows in the light's direction, cloud shade drifts over outdoor ground, lamps and crystals glow, spells light up what's near them, and each wizard carries a small light where it's dark. A partner sees the same.

## Research

| Finding | Source | What we did |
|---|---|---|
| The standard is a screen-space lightmap multiplied over the scene. It's a tint rather than true lighting, and values clip without HDR | [slembcke](https://www.slembcke.net/blog/2DLightingTechniques/), [spritegeek](https://spritegeek.com/glossary) | Used it. Existing additive glows stay on top for brightness past 1 |
| Graveyard Keeper grades each time of day with its own LUT (10 of them, from an artist) and varies ambient from a gradient | [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/graveyard-keeper-how-the-graphics-effects-are-made) | Four moods, each with ambient, shadow colour and a saturation grade. A real LUT is the upgrade if the moods want more |
| Its shadows are sprites turned around a pivot, at most one from the sun and three from the nearest lights. Stretched objects like fences shadow poorly | Same | Sheared silhouettes from the sun alone for now; long walls use their own shape |
| A shadow on the ground shouldn't darken what stands in front of it | From the layer order, not a source | Two maps: shadows under the actors, light over them |
| Pixel-art shadows are hard-edged, hue-shifted rather than grey; dither belongs on band edges only | [tsubu](https://tsubu.art/blog/pixel-art-shading), [pixnote](https://pixnote.net/en/learn/shading) | Flat shadow colours that lean blue, dithered band seams |
| Terrain blends look natural when each texture's height picks the winner, so sand settles in cracks and stone tops stay clean | [Habr](https://habr.com/en/articles/442924), [Gamedev.net](https://gamedev.net/articles/programming/graphics/advanced-terrain-texture-splatting-r3287) | Added to the ground's edges, using each texture's brightness as its height |
| Dwerve casts shadows from object bases and uses a warm torch colour with a flicker | [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/art-design-deep-dive-dynamic-2d-lighting-in-dwerve) | Lamps on props flicker slightly and sit a little above the foot |

Research gaps: no technical breakdowns turned up for Eastward, Hyper Light Drifter, Children of Morta or Sea of Stars, and nothing authoritative on PixiJS v8 lightmaps.

## How it's built

`client/src/art/lighting.ts`. Both maps are drawn at art resolution into render textures, so they stay crisp, then multiplied in. An earlier note in `view.ts` says a low-res render target never reached the screen; that isn't happening with this setup, in headless Chrome at least.

- **Moods** are data (`MOODS`), and each room has one (`ROOM_MOOD`). `?mood=day|dusk|night|interior` overrides it, and `?lighting=0` turns it all off.
- **Lamps** are data too (`PROP_LIGHTS`, by prop id).
- **The ground** stops baking in its own dusk tint and falloff when the scene is lit.

## Costs and what's open

- **Frame cost isn't measured on a real graphics card.** It adds two small render passes and a full-screen colour pass. `npm run perf` covers neither the isometric camera nor lighting yet.
- Rock walls cast from a fixed height, so a shadow can look blocky at a wall's corner.
- Light goes through walls: a lamp lights both sides. Blocking it is the next big step in quality, and it costs per-light shadow work.
- The sprites have no normal maps, so they're lit flat. Graveyard Keeper painted normals for every sprite; that's a heavy art cost, and the look may not need it.
- **For the user, when there's time:** the moods; whether the shadows are too strong; whether the overall look is calmer now.
