# What He Had on Him

*A three-fingered old man washes up dead on your school's boat slip with seven things in his pockets, one from each land on the circuit, and you are the only people going to all of them.*

## The question

**Who was he, and who let him drown?**

He was Joost Tolhuis, gateman of the drowned town of Low Hask, blamed for two hundred and twelve deaths nineteen years ago. The person who let him drown is Greet Vrolijk, who rebuilt that town on stilts and has been kind to you all game. When he fell through her walkway, she stood with a lantern and counted to ten before she shouted.

## The opening

The school sits at the mouth of the river that drains the fen, on an Arcane vein at the edge of the purple land. Leavers get one last chore: clearing the boat slip, where everything from upriver ends up. This morning that includes a body.

Reeve Sole, the local law, needs hands. Short practical beats teach the runes, one of each: Fire dries the frozen coat loose, Shield keeps the crabs off, the school focus conjures a blade to cut the pockets open, Water keeps a live plant cutting from dying. The seven objects go in your satchel. The body goes on ice in the fishmonger's cold-house, which the fishmonger resents. "You're going round anyway," says the Reeve. "Ask after him."

## The road

**Every well gives the same three things:** one pool deepened (your choice), a regen step, and a *reading*, the local trade's way of getting evidence out of its element. Readings are ordinary spells aimed at evidence, with no separate controls. You hold one rune of everything from the start, so the gate is depth and regen: a two-Fire spell warms a burnt page, four Fire held steady for ten seconds relights a whole ledger. Too much destroys it. Your friend's stray Lightning can destroy it too.

The main line never needs more than two of one rune, and each key fact has three routes, so a burnt clue costs you a route and never the case. Depth three and up opens side evidence. Portable evidence can be carried to whichever land bends regen toward what it needs. Your casebook is a page per object, filling in.

The first three lands come in any order.

**The Orchard Country** (south-west, Life). *Object: a grafting knife and that living cutting.*
- **The well** lies under the giant tree, whose own fruit is inedible. Every good orchard on the map is a graft from its rootstock. To attune, you graft a scion and keep it alive through one night.
- **Reading, *grow it out*:** Life on disturbed soil raises seedlings along whatever was buried; on old timber it brings up the rings.
- **Encounters:** boar raids, an orchard defence, a keeping shrine.
- **The beat:** he was "Uncle Cob" here, a hired hand who paid a Hask orphan's apprenticeship and bred a pear, Cob's Late. The orphan, now a grown woman, swears he was very tall. She was eight.
- **Return:** after the midpoint the pear's labels have been scraped off every crate. Later readings raise the strongbox he buried under the rows.

**The Spark Island** (north-west, Lightning). *Object: a brass signal key.*
- **The well** is the crystal peak, which draws storms that strike the same spots every time. A signal wire crosses the island. Attuning means walking the summit gauntlet by reading where the next bolt must land.
- **Reading, *wake it*:** Lightning into a dead device makes it repeat the last thing it did.
- **Encounters:** spark swarms, gauntlets, coil-puzzle rooms.
- **The beat:** as "Long Hob" he walked the wire for eleven years. The key's last message, sent to the fen: TIDE DUE. PIN BAD. COMING. The operator swears nobody ever touches her set.
- **Return:** deep Lightning wakes the main relay and prints every message he ever sent. Almost all of them are money orders.

**The Mesa** (north-centre badlands, Earth). *Object: a plumb bob.*
- **The well** is under a fortress that has never been attacked, whose town has rehearsed its defence for four hundred years. Attuning means holding the breach in the annual drill, which rock beasts have started attending.
- **Reading, *settle it*:** Earth lifts or packs rubble so you can look under a collapse.
- **Encounters:** set-piece siege, native boss, ambushes in the arroyos.
- **The beat:** as "Mister Sill" he built the town a retaining wall for free, then told the master mason it was bad. The master mason calls him a drunk and a liar.
- **Return:** the wall has fallen exactly where he said. The master mason has hung a tarpaulin over it and will not discuss it.

**The Fen** (north-east, Water; always fourth). *Object: an iron door key.*
- **The well** rises at the river sluice, beside High Hask, the stilt town. Attuning means working a tide-shift on the piles.
- **Reading, *bail and lift*:** freeze a breach with Water and Cold, bail the room, then soak dried ink until it lifts.
- **Encounters:** barge escorts, defending the piling crew, a big eel-thing as native boss.
- **The beat** is the midpoint, below.
- **Return:** Greet's walkways grow by a row each visit, partly thanks to you. After the midpoint, doors shut on you.

**The Burning South** (Fire; fifth). *Object: half a sheared bronze pin.*
- **The well** feeds the foundries on the lava seams. Attuning means feeding a furnace by hand through a pour, in Fire's heartland, while the kiln-things come for the heat.
- **Reading, *relight*:** careful Fire makes char glow its old ink, and shows the flaws inside metal.
- **Encounters:** kiln gauntlets, foundry defence, enemy hedge-casters, and the black spires as an optional constraint shrine.
- **The beat:** he swept kilns here for four years under a fourth name, to learn how the pin was cast. Relit, it shows a gas bubble. The founder is outraged and also, on the second visit, sorry. The new gate at High Hask uses the same pattern.
- **Return:** the foundry recasts the pin and the pour lights the whole coast at night.

**The Ice Road** (the far north, Cold; sixth). *Object: toe-cleats and a storehouse chit.*
- **The well** is out on the ice past the colossal skeleton. Nobody knows what the skeleton was and the town camped in the lee of its ribs argues about it nightly (the whale party is winning). Attuning means walking out and standing still for a night while things that wait come looking.
- **Reading, *take the frost*:** chill a surface and frost forms wherever hands once gripped it. Thaw brings out what the ice holds.
- **Encounters:** slow armoured beasts, a caravan escort on the ropes, a thaw puzzle in the storehouse. The monks here teach Shield's wider arts, the ward that holds where you left it.
- **The beat:** the chit releases his box: nineteen years of payments to two hundred and twelve households, every one ticked. The storehouse also keeps the gate's crank from the inquest. Frost shows his big hands on it, and underneath, an older grip: small, low down, two-handed.
- **Return:** the box's addresses open a last round of small errands, delivering what he still owed.

**The Purple Land** (east, Arcane; seventh). *Object: a rope-token.*
- **The well** is torn open below the floating tower, a retired wizards' hospice where you are hauled up in a basket. The air eases old joints. Attuning means the raw well-band gauntlet, where your regen floods and your aim had better be good.
- **Reading, *look through*:** a thin Arcane beam shows what is inside a sealed thing without opening it.
- **Encounters:** strange lone beasts, puzzle rooms in the torn ground.
- **The beat** is the late-game turn, below.
- **Return:** there is no return before the end; afterward, the basket-winder asks after your health.

## The turns

**The midpoint (the Fen).** The key opens a door in the drowned quarter. You bail it out and find a gateman's house, a name on the lintel, and the stone in High Hask's square: 212 names, and at the bottom, cut small and spat on every year, *Joost Tolhuis, who was drunk at his gate*.

Seven kind strangers were one hated man, and every household he paid is a Hask family who might have found him out. The case flips. Everyone you liked in the first three lands now has a motive, so you go back and ask again, with stronger hands. The orphan knew. The master mason knew. Their stories change.

The Readers, Hask survivors who recite the names yearly, start hunting you for asking. Hedge-casters, using your own spell language badly, setting each other on fire. Their leader confesses to killing Joost, loudly and with pride. Evidence puts him in a tavern two lands away that week.

**The Sigil art (the Burning South).** To test the pin you need force applied somewhere you cannot stand. The old pattern-maker looks at your school focus, says the etching on it is the stabiliser every leaver gets, and takes a rasp to it. Nothing secret about it. "You've been shaping what you hold. Put something over there." The furnace yard becomes your first room of turrets, glyphs and Blink, and you run the test: a pin of that casting only shears if the gate is lifted against a full head of water. So someone wound that gate up by hand.

**The late-game turn (the tower).** The token leads to Piet Oudt, eighty-one, once a barge-owner, whose room is paid for by High Hask timber credit. He tells a different story every visit, because he is eighty-one. One line stays put across every telling: *she floated me off.* Nineteen years ago his grain barge went on the mud at dusk and would have ruined him. His daughter, the gateman's seventeen-year-old apprentice, wound the gate up for twenty minutes to float it, while Joost was at a christening with witnesses and drink on his breath. The pin sheared. The gate would not close. The tide came in behind it. Joost lost three fingers trying, took the blame believing the fault was his, and never knew otherwise.

Her married name is Greet Vrolijk.

## The ending

The great tide comes every nineteen years. Greet has told you so since the day you met, and it is the deadline on her piles.

You go back knowing. She knows too; she has known since you bailed the cellar, and she went on selling you boots at cost. The Columbo walk: you ask about one plank. Then the lantern ring on the rail. You set your Double where the man fell and stand where she stood, and the rail is within arm's reach. He had come to warn her about the pin, and to ask her, of all people, to help him find who wound the gate. She says: "I'll tell you after the tide."

**The finale** is a preparation-heavy defence in the fen's well band. You seat the recast pin, then hold the sluice with everything you have built up: frost the gate shut, wall the breaches, stand turrets on the walkways, fight what the surge drives in. The Readers join on the piles, still terrible at it. Greet is on the crank, because nobody else knows how it sticks.

Afterward she tells you the rest. "I counted. Ten. Then I shouted."

The host, or a quick vote, writes the finding in the casebook:
- **The whole of it.** Joost's name goes up among the 212. Greet goes with the Reeve, unresisting. A year on, a plank in High Hask is loose and nobody has counted it.
- **The pin failed.** True, and incomplete. Joost's name goes up among the 212. Greet stays, and the town stays sound. Each year she reads his name at the stone herself, and you know what that is.

Either way you plant the cutting, which you have kept alive all game and which has outgrown the satchel, on his grave above the slip.

**Last image:** the fishmonger gets the cold-house back. They stand at the empty slab, take off their hat, hold it for a count of ten, put it back on, and start shovelling in herring.

## The recurring faces

- **Reeve Sole.** Writes only in pencil because ink runs. Wants a name for the grave.
- **The fishmonger.** Mentions the cold-house at every return. Wants their ice back.
- **Greet Vrolijk.** Counts everything twice. Turns up in every land buying timber, bronze or rope. Wants the last row of piles driven before the tide.
- **Ulla Breck, skipper of the sea packet.** Bets on anything, and chalks up odds on every suspect in the galley, updated from your casebook. Wants to win one bet off a wizard.
- **Cas Hoekstra, head of the Readers.** Can recite all 212 names in under four minutes and will show you. Wants the credit.
- **Piet Oudt.** Thanks everyone for something. Wants to go home, which is under water.

## Why this is fun to play

You walk back into a room you cleared twelve hours ago with a deeper Fire pool and the burnt desk in the corner is suddenly a lead. Every rune you bank has two jobs, killing boars and relighting a dead man's handwriting, and the second job needs a soft touch that the first job never taught you. In co-op, one of you is delicately frosting a door handle while another is holding off eels with a spell that absolutely could reach the door handle. The skipper's chalkboard and the casebook make progress visible even on a night when you only went back and asked one person one more question.

## The biggest weakness

The killing resolves into a woman standing still, on a plank, for ten seconds, and it arrives at the end of a game whose hands have spent forty hours blowing things up. A flood defence carries the climax, but the emotional payoff lives in three quiet sentences, in a game told through places and short conversations, with a party that may be four friends setting each other alight. If Greet has not been earned through what her town visibly becomes, the last line is just a stranger's confession. Also, a sharp player will suspect the nicest recurring face by the midpoint, so the case must stay interesting once they know who, which means the skipper's odds, the false confessor and the second-visit reversals have to carry real doubt about *how* and *why*.
