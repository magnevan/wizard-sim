# The 3D effects kit spike: Fire spray and fire floor, two looks (2026-11-25)

Step 0 of the [port to 3D](2026-11-25-port-3d-plan.md), decision 3: a small effects kit of our own, and one family (the Fire spray and the floor it burns) made twice, so the user can pick the look before the effects pass starts. Built on branch `p3d-fx`. Nothing here is in the game yet; the lab is dev only.

## What was built

- **The kit** (`game/packages/client/src/view3d/fx/`):
  - `particles.ts`: `ParticleBatch`, one draw call of camera-facing quads (an instanced quad). Every particle's position, size, colour and stretch are computed **in the vertex shader from (time, particle index, seed, emitter uniforms)**. Nothing is stored, so setting the time freezes or rewinds exactly. A stream emits particle k at k / rate and keeps it for `life` (the same rule as `art/util.ts` `emit()`). A family writes a GLSL motion function and a GLSL look function. Quads can face the camera (optionally stretched along their own motion, which the shader works out by evaluating the same particle 0.03 s earlier) or stand upright on their base.
  - `stage.ts`: `FxStage`, the frame. The world renders into an HDR multisampled target that keeps its depth as a texture. The effects draw into the same target, depth-tested in hardware and soft-faded against the resolved depth. three's OutputPass then tone-maps with the kit's ACES. A host calls `fx.render(renderer, world, camera)` instead of `renderer.render`.
  - `aim-trail.ts`: a channel's recent aim, resampled into a 64-entry uniform array (1/60 s apart, about 1 s back). Each particle reads the aim from its own birth, so a sweep bends like a hose.
  - `lights.ts`: `SpellLights`, a fixed pool of 8 point lights. Each frame they go to the brightest requests. The pool never changes size, so no materials recompile.
  - `decal.ts`: `GroundDecal`, a grid sheet whose vertices are lifted to `heightAt(x, z)`, so floors drape over steps. The family colours it with a GLSL function of the world point.
  - `from-sim.ts`: everything the effects read from the sim. `sprays()` gives each channelling spray with the sim's own range, half-angle and stop point. `SurfGrid` turns `w.surf` into textures: the whole grid, linear filtered, for decals, plus a packed list of burning cells for the flames. Mapping: sim (x, y) is three's (x, z), as in `kit-render.ts`.
  - `glsl.ts`: an integer hash (the same on every GPU), value noise and fbm, and the fire's heat ramp.
  - `fire.ts`: the family. `FireSpray` has core, flames, smoke and embers, plus a warm fan of light on the floor. `FireFloor` has a charred bed with ember cracks (a decal), flames standing on every burning cell and embers rising.
- **The lab** `fx3d.html` (`src/lab/fx3d/main.ts`): the real sim in the Sanctum, with wizard 0 casting through `demoInput`. It shows the kit's paving and lighting (`setupStage`), the game camera (28°, 46° down, 26 u of ground) and the wizard GLB, standing still. URL options: `look=a|b`, `n=1..5`, `cast=spray|floor|both`, `hold=`, `t=`, `times=` + `tile=`, `sweep=1`, `hit=1`, `view=`, `x=`/`y=`, `wiz=0`.
- **Shots**: `npm run shot:fx3d -- [--gl soft] [--only …]` (`scripts/fx3d-shots.mjs`) writes `game/shots/port-3d/fx/` (gitignored).
- **Painted sheets** (Codex, kept whole in `game/content/generated/fx/`): `fire-wisps.png` (4 × 4 flame wisps) and `fire-tongues.png` (4 × 4 clusters of ground flames).

The sim keeps sprays off the floor ("Sprays leave the floor alone", `effects.ts`), so the fire floor in these shots comes from a Fire Place patch, which is how the game makes one. `cast=both` places the patch and then sprays beside it.

## The spec (the Fire spray and its floor)

| Field | |
|---|---|
| **Job** | Who is burning what: a hose of flame from the staff, exactly as wide and long as the sim's cone, stopping where a wall stops it. The floor says "don't stand here" until it dies. |
| **Silhouette** | Spray: a fan widening from the staff and billowing at its far end. Floor: a ragged disc of standing flame on a charred rim. |
| **Leading element** | Fire alone. Mixes are later rounds. |
| **Movement** | Fast at the staff, slowing and billowing at the end. Flames rise as they cool, embers carry past and fall. Each particle keeps the aim it was born with. The floor's flames flicker in place: they lean, grow and die per cell and never travel. |
| **Colour** | White-gold at the staff, through orange to red at the tips. From four runes a flame is born blue-white. The floor's flames are gold at the root and red at the tip, over dark char with orange cracks. |
| **Timing** | The spray reaches full length in about 0.3 s. Flames live about 0.5 s and embers 0.9 s. When the channel stops, what's in flight finishes (up to 1.5 s). The floor fades over its last second (`FLOOR_FADE`): the flames shrink and the char stays to the end. |
| **Layers** | 1. the flame body; 2. the hot core at the staff; 3. embers; 4. smoke over the far half; 5. the warm fan and light on the floor. |
| **Runes** | 1 → 5: wider and longer (the sim's numbers), more and bigger flames, hotter colour. From 4 the flames are born blue-white; at 5, nearly twice the embers. |
| **Budget** | Spray: at most 413 quads in 5 draw calls (smoke 44, flames 146, core 74, embers 149) plus 1 for the fan. Floor: 3 flames and 2 embers per burning cell. A five-rune patch is about 160 cells, so about 800 quads in 3 calls. Lights: 2 per spray and up to 4 per floor, from a pool of 8. Cut first in a crowd: smoke, then embers. |
| **Not** | Not orange energy or a glare. Not smoke (look A's current failure). Not a field of separate objects. |

## Step 0 shots and the rubric

The shots are in `game/shots/port-3d/fx/`, taken on the GPU at 560 × 420 tiles, `view=13` (closer than the game camera so a strip reads), and at the game camera for `*-game.png`. Strips run t = 0.1, 0.3, 0.6, 1, 2 s.

**B (painted sheet) is clearly ahead.**
- Readable, element, alive: yes. It reads as fire at once, on the court's warm orange paving, which is the hardest floor for fire.
- Runes: from one rune to five is a real step up. The five-rune fan is a wall of flame, which is the sim's 130° cone, and gives some awe.
- The sweep strip bends like a hose, and flames already flying keep their line.
- Its floor (`b-floor-n3-strip`) is a dense, lively carpet that dies to char and embers at 16.6 s.
- Problems, biggest first:
  1. **The spray is lumpy, not one material.** Individual painted wisps show as separate balls at the far end (n = 5), with a hollow in the middle of the fan.
  2. The floor's carpet is so uniform that it reads as a texture of flames more than as a fire. The rim cells are no lower than the middle ones by eye.
  3. At four and five runes the blue-white birth reads as pale pink smudges at the staff.
  4. The floor appears at full height on its first frame, with no grow-in.

**A (code mediums) is not there.**
- On sunlit stone the spray reads as an orange dust or smoke cloud (`a-spray-*-strip`).
- The floor reads as pale gold crystals or straw (`a-floor-n3-strip`).
- Rubric fails: Element ("orange energy"), A material, and Restraint (the smoke is too heavy).
- The reason is physical. In a bright HDR scene, additive particles wash to white and covering ones lose their glow. Code-shaped blobs then have nothing that says "flame" except colour. A would need sharper hand-tuned shapes (tongues, licks) to compete, which is what the painted sheet gives for free.

**Faithful to the sim.**
- `*-spray-n3-hit.png` dots the sim's cone. The flames fill it out to where the channel stops.
- The warm fan on the floor marks the cone's ground footprint. The flames themselves fly at chest height, so on screen they sit above the ground cone, as anything with height will.
- Every floor cell drawn is a burning cell of `w.surf`.

**GPU vs SwiftShader.** `soft/*-both-n5-game.png` match the GPU frames: same pipeline, same colours. Multisampling and half-float targets work in SwiftShader, so the checks can run headless. A strip takes 1 to 5 s per page on either.

## What the kit can and can't do yet

**Can:**
- stateless streams and per-cell emitters
- billboards, stretched streaks and upright quads
- premultiplied blending, which covers both additive and alpha-blended effects
- soft depth fade
- hardware occlusion by the world (the wizard hides flames behind him)
- floors that follow `heightAt`
- 8 pooled spell lights
- aim trails for anything aimed

**Can't yet:**
- **Ribbons** (beams, arcs), which the next families need.
- **Bloom.** Hot cores have no halo; HDR only rolls off in the tone map.
- **A grow-in for floors.** The view doesn't know a cell's age. It would need a client record of when the cell lit, or the sim's initial life.
- Per-spray interpolation between ticks: the lab draws on ticks.
- Several sprays at once: one `FireSpray` per seat is expected. A pool per seat is easy.
- **Particle counts are caps.** Live counts aren't read back from the GPU.
- Knobs (`fx-knobs.ts`) aren't wired in.

**Perf**, measured in the lab:
- A five-rune spray plus a five-rune patch is 8 draw calls for the effects, with at most about 1,200 quads.
- The HDR multisampled target is the larger fixed cost. It is one extra full-screen pass, and it replaces the renderer's own MSAA.

## For the lead

- `FxStage.render` owns the frame: world, effects, then the tone map. The view shell should call it instead of `renderer.render`. It needs `antialias: false` on the renderer, since the target is multisampled itself.
- The spray's nozzle (`NOZZLE` in `fire.ts`) is a fixed offset and height. It should come from the posed staff tip once the characters work lands.

## Open questions for the user

1. **Look B (painted sheets) as the direction for fire?** Recommended: yes, with the next round on the spray's far end (fewer, bigger wisps blended into one body) and on a lower, raggeder floor edge.
2. **Keep look A at all?** Recommended: drop A as a whole look, but keep the code layers that work in both (embers, the char bed, the fan of light on the floor). This supersedes nothing yet; the decision "mediums are code-driven particles" stands, with painted shapes for the particles, as the plan foresaw.
