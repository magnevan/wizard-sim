# The obvious answers (2026-11-09)

This is what a capable AI writer would most likely produce for `00-brief.md` on a first attempt. It is a list of things to avoid. Nothing here is a proposal.

## Five most likely plots

**1. The Fading Wells.** The wells are dimming, so this year's circuit becomes a rescue mission. At each region you find a sick well and a missing master. A former star graduate, working from the floating tower, is draining them to power one great ritual. At the midpoint you learn the academy knew and said nothing. The trail ends in the northern ice, where you must choose between restoring the old flow and letting magic change. You find a third way, and the land visibly heals.

**2. The Circuit Is a Lock.** Every attunement secretly renews a seal on something buried beneath the central sea. The academy has hidden this for centuries, sending each generation out under the name of tradition. A journeyman from a lost cohort found out and now breaks wells so no one else gets used. At the midpoint you realise your own attunements have been turning the key. The last well asks you to become the final seal. You refuse, and share the burden among every town you saved.

**3. The Sleeping Titan.** The colossal skeleton in the ice is a dead god, the veins are its blood vessels and the wells are its wounds. All magic is its slow bleeding. The bones are stirring, and a robed cult feeds it the wells. Each attunement shows you a vision of its death. At the late-game turn the ice cracks and the skeleton moves. You face it at its heart, and either lay it to rest or wake it gently, ending magic as anyone knew it.

**4. The Teacher's Footsteps.** Your beloved teacher walked the circuit forty years ago and vanished. Notes left at each well guide you onward, and each region remembers them a little differently. The purple land turns out to be the scar of their failed attempt to fix something, and the cataclysm was their fault. At the midpoint you find them alive, changed, and determined to try again at any cost. You fight the person who taught you everything, using what they taught you, and finish the circuit they could not.

**5. Whose Wells Are They?** The mesa fortress is the seat of a growing guild-state that is buying, seizing and metering the wells to feed its lightning-driven industry. The old journeyman's circuit is being outlawed as a quaint obstacle. Region by region you free wells, reopen roads and win over farmers, smiths and fen folk. A guild chancellor with a reasonable argument is the final opponent. The land is sick from extraction. In the ending, wells return to their communities and you found a new kind of school.

## The ban list

### Causes of what goes wrong

1. The wells are dimming, drying up or going silent one region at a time.
2. A blight or corruption spreads along the veins, drawn as glowing purple or black lines.
3. Someone is draining the wells to fuel one enormous ritual.
4. An ancient seal is weakening after exactly a thousand years, or some other round number.
5. Civilisation has overused magic and the land is sick, with "balance" needing to be restored.
6. The purple land is the scar of a past cataclysm caused by wizards' hubris.

### Twists

7. The academy has been lying to its students about what the circuit is for.
8. The circuit is itself a ritual, and each attunement has been advancing the villain's plan or turning a lock.
9. The antagonist is a former journeyman, from a lost or erased cohort.
10. The kindly teacher is the villain, or was the one who caused the original disaster.
11. The helpful letters you have been receiving were written by the antagonist, or by someone long dead.
12. The veins and wells are literally the circulatory system of a living or dead giant.
13. You are the chosen one, the last of a line, or were selected in secret for your special aptitude.
14. The seven elements were once a single force that was split apart, and the finale reunites them.

### Antagonists

15. A disgraced archmage who wants to save the world by terrible means and is framed as "not entirely wrong".
16. A robed cult that worships whatever is under the ice and speaks of awakening it.
17. A militarised empire, inquisition or guild-state operating out of the mesa fortress.
18. A void, nothingness or anti-magic entity, with the tenth element revealed as Void or Shadow.

### What attunement secretly is or costs

19. Each attunement costs you a memory.
20. Attuning takes power out of the well, so you turn out to be the cause of its weakening.
21. The well is sentient, speaks as the spirit of its element and tests your heart.
22. Attuning is slowly turning you into part of the land, with crystal, bark or ash creeping over your skin.
23. Each element teaches its matching virtue: Fire passion, Water adaptability, Earth patience, Life compassion.

### Mentors and teachers

24. A wry old wizard with a pipe or endless tea who dies at the midpoint holding a door so you can escape.
25. A stern headmistress who hid the truth to protect you and shows a warm heart at the end.

### Rivals

26. A haughty noble top-of-the-class rival who needles you all game and arrives to save you in the final battle.
27. A rival who attunes too fast or takes a forbidden shortcut, is consumed by it, and must be fought or redeemed by sacrifice.

### Recurring side characters and their quirks

28. A trader who always arrives somewhere impossibly ahead of you, with a running "how did you get here first?" joke.
29. A cartographer obsessed with the blank northern edge of the map, or whose maps are cheerfully wrong.
30. A younger sibling or grandmother who writes home-front letters, which stop arriving at the midpoint.
31. A sardonic talking familiar, usually a cat or a raven.
32. A nervous school clerk who keeps a ledger of friendly-fire incidents and reads your tally aloud.

### Endings and last images

33. A third way between the two offered choices, typically sharing the burden or giving magic to everyone.
34. The final well rises out of the central sea, or was at the bottom of it all along.
35. The last image is a new class of young wizards setting out, with you now the teacher.
36. The last image is the party walking north off the edge of the map, or the blank area of the map filling in.
37. You give up your magic to save the world, ending with one rune or an empty pool.

### Uses of the skeleton, the floating tower, the giant tree and the volcano

38. The skeleton is a dead god, titan or dragon whose death created magic, and it moves in the last act.
39. The floating tower is the villain's lair and the source of the warped land, and it comes crashing down in the climax.
40. The giant tree is the oldest living thing, remembers everything, and is dying from its roots up.
41. The volcano erupts at a story turn, or holds a forge of creation or a sleeping fire being.

### Names, words and phrases

42. Mood words: echo, hollow, resonance, whisper, thrum, hum, ember, tapestry, thread, weave, ache, shimmer.
43. Capitalised dooms: the Unmaking, the Sundering, the Silence, the Hollowing, the Fading, the Quiet, the Long Dark, the Reckoning.
44. Character names: Elara, Thorne, Lyra, Kael, Seraphine, Aldric, Mira, Vex, Maren, Corvin, Elias, Voss, Old Tam, Sister Anselm.
45. Place and title formulas: Emberfall, Frosthold, Hollowmere, Stillwater, the Spire, the Athenaeum, the Keeper, the Warden, the Archivist, the Weaver, the First.
46. Sentence pattern: "not X, but Y", "It isn't X. It's Y.", and "less a X than a Y".
47. Theme stated as a question: "What is power for?", "What do we owe the land?", "What does it cost?"
48. Character formula: wants to finish what a late spouse, child or teacher began.
49. Dry-joke template: bureaucracy in a fantasy setting, such as forms in triplicate, a guild fee or a sign that says "Regulations prohibit...".
50. Closing cadence: a short portentous line such as "The land remembers.", "The well remembers you.", or "And somewhere, a well stirs."
