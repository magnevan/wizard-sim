# The elements: what each one *is*

A living page, unlike the dated explorations: edit it when the world or the spells change. It records the essence of each element, so the world (places, creatures, people, devices) and the spells can be designed from the same idea. The spell list may change over time; when a spell and its element's essence disagree, decide which one moves, and note it here.

See [Story canon](../campaign/canon.md#approved) for the approved setting. The element identities below are working design sketches agreed in outline. [World and Progression exploration](../explorations/2026-11-07-world-and-progression.md) · the identities in play were redefined on 2026-11-28: [Element identities](../explorations/2026-11-28-element-identities.md).

## The premise

Magic runs through the world as **fields** (broad, slow, like a climate) and **veins** (narrow, strong, like a river or an ore seam). People settle where fields help them, the way towns grow along rivers: smiths at a swell of fire, farms on water and life, mines on earth. Where a field is strong, the craft built on it advanced further, so some places have steam engines or rudimentary electricity while the rest of the world is medieval.

The seven **field elements** are Water, Fire, Cold, Lightning, Earth, Life and Arcane. Shield and Sigil are **casting styles** (agreed 2026-11-28, not built yet: [casting-styles](../ideas/casting-styles.md)): they belong to the caster, not the land, and are paid from the caster's own Concentration and Bindings instead of runes.

**Casting directly is new.** The vein shift made it possible for wizards to cast from their hands. Shield and Sigil are the caster's own part of that, which is why they aren't fields. [Story canon](../campaign/canon.md#approved)

**What this means in play:**
- Zones bend the *regen share* of field elements. Shield and Sigil work the same everywhere.
- Field elements deepen through *attunement* at places in the world; Concentration and Bindings grow through *training*.
- Where two fields meet, the spell rules for that pair show up in the landscape (a Fire and Water confluence is a land of steam).

## How to read an element in play

Our game differs from Magicka in three ways that shape the elements (the user, 2026-11-28):
- **No Magicks list.** Expressiveness comes through the core casting: more gestures, more elements, and combinations that each do something (Life + Fire must be useful, not nonsense).
- **Power is capped by the wizard,** who grows during the game, and the world holds casters and creatures at other power levels. So **effects are amounts, not switches**: no absolute reflection, immunity or dispel, and statuses build rather than flip.
- **A longer, deeper game:** a slower learning curve and a higher ceiling.

So each element answers the same five questions:
1. **On a body:** its verb.
2. **Shape:** does it lead a spell, and with what shape? The baseline family (Fire, Cold, Water) mostly doesn't: they colour the shape another element gives.
3. **With other magic:** what it does to other elements and to standing effects.
4. **More runes:** what an extra rune buys, always a bigger amount.
5. **Weakness or cost.**

**Three ways a spell is paid for** decide what can disrupt it (agreed 2026-11-28, [casting-styles](../ideas/casting-styles.md)):
- **Released:** once it leaves your hands it's the world's. A fire patch, rain puddles, burning, a wet body. Paid once in runes; it fades by its own nature. Arcane can't dispel it; Fire dries it.
- **Concentration:** held against pressure, continuously (force fields, wards). It drains harder the harder it's pushed.
- **A Binding:** a made thing holding one of your Bindings (a weapon, a turret, a glyph, a rock wall). When it ends, the Binding comes back after a short cooldown. When there's time to prepare, your Bindings cap how much you can set up.

## Fields, veins and wells

- **A field** is broad and ambient, like rainfall or a climate. Its strength is what a zone's band measures (edge, heartland, well).
- **A vein** is a narrow channel of strength inside or between fields, like a river. It exists in the world, not as a gameplay effect: no boost for standing on one (the user, 2026-11-08). Devices and towns sit on them (a forge on a fire vein).
- **A well** is built by people where veins meet, to draw on them. A civilization can raise a great one, and there are smaller ones too. A region's push peaks at its great well. (Changed 2026-11-09: wells are constructed, not natural.)
- **Opposites can meet, depending on how they appear.** The rules cancel Fire and Cold, Water and Lightning, Life and Arcane, and Earth and Lightning, but in the world that's case by case. The user: "fire and frost is a bit weird to overlap. Water and lightning is not so crazy, lightning is not a persistent thing in the world. Rain and lightning is a common thing to happen together." A **hush** (a dead zone where neither holds) is a possible feature where fire meets frost, not a law. *(2026-11-08.)*

## The field elements

Marks: *(built)* is in the game; *(agreed)* the user agreed on 2026-11-28, not built; *(proposed)* Claude's, not yet confirmed.

### Water: what flows and fills
- **In the world:** rivers, lakes, rain, tides. The most common field; nearly every town sits on some.
- **People:** farming (with Life), mills, ports, baths. Water-veined towns are old and populous.
- **Creatures:** amphibious, slow-wearing, hard to burn.
- **In play: the medium.** It changes what a body is, so the next element hits it differently.
  - **On a body:** soaks (a soak pool: more Water soaks harder); a little damage; pushes. *(built)*
  - **Shape:** colours another element's shape; alone a spray, a splash, a swell. *(built)*
  - **With other magic:** douses fire; freezes with Cold (Ice); the wetter a body, the more Lightning and a freeze pay; with Fire it's steam, which never soaks: cover, not a setup. Rain lingers and keeps puddles topped up. *(built; 2026-11-11, the spell audit)*
  - **More runes:** a harder soak and a further push.
  - **Weakness:** little damage of its own; it helps the enemy's Lightning as much as yours.
  - **Its push vs Earth's:** both have mass. Water leans to a **sustained shove** (a current), Earth to a **single heavy impact**, though either can push either way depending on the spell. *(agreed as a direction)* *(The shove may move to Air, if Air comes in: `../ideas/air-element.md`.)*
- **Opposite:** Lightning.

### Fire: what consumes and transforms
- **In the world:** volcanic swells, hot springs, seams under old forges. Unstable: fire fields flare and fade.
- **People:** smiths, glassmakers, kilns. Where Fire meets Water, steam and the first engines; where it meets Lightning, arc foundries, the most industrial place in the world.
- **Creatures:** quick, aggressive, short-lived; drawn to burning.
- **In play: the best damage, spread over time, and it consumes.**
  - **On a body:** high damage, much of it as burning over time; you risk setting yourself alight. *(built)*
  - **Shape:** colours another element's shape (Earth + Fire is a fireball); alone a flamethrower, a wave of flame, a burning patch that denies ground. *(built)*
  - **With other magic:** it consumes: it burns away Wet, Chill, ice and growth. That makes it hard to combine, and players can get in each other's way, so they build their kits with care. *(built for Wet, Chill and ice; growth once Life grows things)*
  - **More runes:** more damage and longer burning.
  - **Weakness:** its damage over time is hard to get full value from. **Stone doesn't burn, it only heats:** Fire does less to Earth and rock. *(agreed to try; the practical effects still to see)*
- **Opposite:** Cold.

### Cold: what stills and preserves
- **In the world:** mountains, the far north, deep caves. Cold fields are slow and patient; they hold what's in them.
- **People:** ice roads in winter, storehouses, monasteries that keep records. Cold keeps things, including the past.
- **Creatures:** large, slow, armoured; things that wait.
- **In play: control.** The biggest crowd control, for less damage (except freeze and shatter).
  - **On a body:** chill slows walking and attacks on a sliding scale; with Water it freezes; ice sheets and shatter. *(built)*
  - **Shape:** colours another element's shape; alone a frost spray, the Frost ring, freezing fog. *(built)*
  - **With other magic:** with Water it makes Ice, whose identity is damage (16 a rune, the fastest missiles, spikes), so Cold is control and Ice the damage. *(built)*
  - **More runes:** a harder chill.
  - **Weakness:** little damage until something freezes.
  - **Parked:** "preserve" as a verb in play: Frost slowing the decay of a standing effect. If it ever comes, it's for matter only (a frozen body, an ice wall), never energy: Frost wouldn't make a lightning storm last longer (the user).
- **Opposite:** Fire.

### Lightning: what leaps and connects
- **In the world:** storm coasts, high peaks, open steppe. It isn't persistent: it arrives in bursts and strikes the same places again, so it can share ground with Water (storms over a marsh). Where it meets Cold, spark peaks; where it meets Fire, arc foundries and wildfire steppe; where it meets Water, the storm marsh.
- **People:** the most "modern" towns: rods, coils, crude electric lamps and signals. Also the most dangerous to live in.
- **Creatures:** fast, twitchy, in swarms that chain.
- **In play: quick, it chooses its own path, and it disrupts actions.**
  - **On a body:** good damage at once; stagger and stun. *(built)*
  - **Shape:** it leads its own: the arc that jumps between bodies and through water, the fork. *(built)*
  - **Its path follows rules you can learn** (nearest, wettest, tallest, and later metal), so it looks chaotic to a beginner and is predictable to an expert. "Unpredictable" would break pillar 3's promise that friendly fire is avoidable on purpose. *(agreed)*
  - **With other magic:** Water amplifies it. **It disrupts actions** (stagger, interrupting a cast or a channel); Arcane disrupts magic. *(agreed)*
  - **More runes:** more damage and more jumps.
  - **Weakness:** hard to aim around allies; Earth grounds it (they cancel in the queue).
- **Opposite:** Water.

### Earth: what holds and endures
- **In the world:** mountains, plains of stone, ore seams. The steadiest field; it barely moves in a lifetime.
- **People:** miners, masons, fortress towns. Wealthy and conservative.
- **Creatures:** heavy, thick-skinned, territorial.
- **In play: it binds magic to the physical world.** Earth turns a spell into matter. *(the user's idea, explored 2026-11-28)*
  - **On a body:** mass, impact and knockback; physical damage. *(built)*
  - **Shape:** it leads the rock (which carries the others: Earth + Fire is a fireball), the long shockwave and walls. *(built)*
  - **With other magic:** what it makes is matter, so **Arcane can't disrupt it**: there's no magic left in it to strip *(proposed: a rock wall is a Binding, but the Binding holds matter)*. Earth carrying an element delivers it as matter, so a magical shield refuses the element but takes the rock's mass. *(proposed)* It grounds Lightning. With Life it may grow living structures (vines, thorn hedges, roots that hold a body), which heal themselves and burn. *(the user, to explore)*
  - **More runes:** a bigger rock, more HP, a longer wall. *(built)*
  - **Weakness: its nature, not an element.** Stone resists nothing in particular, it's just a lot of it: it takes full damage from everything, and sheer force or a shatter breaks it. A wall can't move and blocks your allies too, and it's paid in Earth and a Binding. *(proposed)*
  - **Walls are Bindings** (the user): the Earth is spent, dismissing a wall frees its Binding only after the cooldown, and no Concentration is drawn. This replaces the odd model where a rock wall took a Shield rune but cost nothing to hold. *(agreed)*
- **Opposite:** none yet (Air would be, on T).

### Life: what grows
- **In the world:** the field living things make. Thick in old forests and good farmland, thin in deserts and blights. The only field that grows and shrinks with what lives on it, so it can be harmed and restored.
- **People:** farmers, healers, druid orders. Life land is the breadbasket, and fought over for it.
- **Creatures:** many, varied, regenerating; the wildest regions.
- **In play: healing, still thinly defined.** *(open thread)*
  - **On a body:** heals; the other elements in a heal lend their resistances; the Healing link and Mend. *(built)*
  - **Shape:** it leads; the element lending the shape sets the size, Life sets the strength. *(built)*
  - **Directions still open:** growth over time as Fire's mirror (Fire consumes, Life grows); living structures as Earth + Life rather than a kind of wall of their own. **Rejected:** Life cleansing statuses. It takes over Arcane's job rather than complementing it, and clashes with Fire drying you off (the user).
- **Opposite:** Arcane.

### Arcane: the deep field
- **In the world:** under every other field, near the surface only where the crust of the others is torn: rifts, ruins, very old places. Rare, coveted, dangerous. People exposed to it develop more magical ability.
- **People:** scholars and wizards. Arcane draws out a caster's innate abilities, which is why **the academy sits on an Arcane vein**.
- **Creatures:** few, strange, out of place.
- **In play: raw magic, concentrated; it overloads shaped magic.** The first element not rooted in a physical effect. *(agreed 2026-11-28)*
  - **It concentrates:** Arcane trades immediacy for intensity, and each cast shows it differently. Force is the beam *(built)*; Area is a charge and a release *(agreed, new)*; Place is a delayed explosion (concentrate, then release) rather than a slow lingering effect *(built)*. It never makes a lasting thing; that's what a Binding is for.
  - **It disrupts magic, like overvoltage:** raw magic poured into shaped magic overloads it. It acts on what a caster is still paying for, by amounts (non-binary). Against **Concentration** it drains the holder's pool faster, so a shield takes more from Arcane than flesh or stone does. Against a **Binding** it strips charges or HP. A timed magical effect loses strength or duration. Released effects (burning, wet) aren't magic any more and it can't touch them. *(agreed)*
  - **Power against power:** how much it disrupts is the caster's power against the holder's, so a weak wizard's Arcane dents a strong ward but doesn't break it.
  - **On yourself: opening yourself** (replaces the glass cannon framing; *agreed*): it strips the magic held on you, buffs and hostile effects alike (wards, auras, Haste, imbued resistances), floods your regen, and while it lasts magic acts more strongly on you in both directions, heals included. A partner can open themselves before you heal them. What it doesn't do: dry you or put out flames (that's Fire's).
  - **More runes:** more intensity (+70 % a rune), more disruption.
  - **Weakness:** matter. Stone and other released effects are out of its reach, and an Arcane coat on a field resists it.
- **Opposite:** Life.

## Shield, Earth and Arcane against each other

Every effect has a **magic** part and a **matter** part *(proposed 2026-11-28, the user: "this system could work")*:

| | Defends by | Strong against | Weak against | Costs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Shield** (a force field) | *Quality:* high resistance to a defined change (an element, a status) | Elemental magic: sprays, statuses, elemental beams | **Arcane:** raw magic has no element to refuse, so it pours into the refusal itself. Mass gets partly through | Concentration |
| **Earth** (a rock wall) | *Quantity:* mass and HP, no resistance | Arcane (nothing to disrupt); Lightning (grounded) | Sheer force, a shatter; full damage from everything | Earth runes and a Binding |

- **Shield weak against Arcane** is the reverse of Magicka, where shields beat beams. The user accepts it here: it's a different game. **A field coated with Arcane resists the disruption**, by the existing rule that each rune in a coated field resists its element, at the cost of the slot another coat would take.
- **Proposed: timing beats the beam.** An active parry beats it (the Prism guard already returns beams), while a standing barrier gets eroded. The caster's skill wins, not the wall.

## The casting styles: Shield and Sigil

Agreed 2026-11-28, not built: [casting-styles](../ideas/casting-styles.md). Each is still a key you press and still takes a queue slot, so the spell list isn't changed by this; but neither has runes. They spend the caster's own **Concentration** and **Bindings**, which grow through training, and no zone weakens them. Both styles can't be in one cast (they cancel, as today).

### Shield: the will to refuse
- **What it is:** a caster's capacity to hold and to not be moved.
- **Paid in Concentration:** a real-number pool with its own regen. Refusing takes **continuous concentration**: barriers and wards drain it while they stand, and drain harder as they're hit, since refusing a change costs more the harder the world pushes. When it runs out, the oldest gives way. *(The user, 2026-11-08, carried over.)* Instant uses (Blink) spend a lump at once. What a Shield cast makes of matter (a rock wall, an ice wall) is a Binding instead.
- **In play:** barriers, wards on allies, Blink. Guards (Shield + Weapon) are conjured things, so they're **Bindings**, like weapons (the user: "it was always kind of weird that they weren't"); their parry costs nothing extra.

### Sigil: the will to shape
- **What it is:** a caster's capacity to bind magic into a lasting form: a blade, a turret, a glyph. Only casters can do it, which is why weapons and devices need Sigil.
- **Paid in Bindings:** a few discrete slots. Each made thing holds one until it goes off, breaks or is dismissed, and the Binding comes back after a short cooldown. Shield is attention held continuously; a Binding is attention held in pieces.
- **In play:** weapons, turrets, glyphs, orbitals, the Lens, auras, Haste, the sculpting wand (per the [spell pass](../explorations/2026-11-27-spell-pass.md)).

**The resource follows what's made, not the key:** Sigil casts are mostly Bindings and Shield casts mostly Concentration, but a Shield + Earth wall and a guard are Bindings. So Shield's Concentration is what's left: force fields, wards and Blink.
