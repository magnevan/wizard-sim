The simulation is ours in every option

Online-ready co-op needs a deterministic sim. No engine's physics, navigation, timers or networking is deterministic enough for lockstep or rollback: not Godot's, not Unity's, not Phaser's, and not Bevy's float maths without extra work. So whatever we pick, we write the game rules ourselves, and the engine renders them. That turns one big choice into two smaller ones.

Sim · ours

Spells, elements, statuses, enemies, walls, surfaces, collision, flow fields. Fixed 60 Hz step, seeded RNG, our own trig, plain data we can hash and snapshot. Its language is the hard choice to undo. Only TypeScript reuses rules-c.js, the skeleton's sim, its tests and the Atlas audits as they are.

Engine · pick one

Drawing, VFX, audio, asset loading, maybe an editor. It reads the sim each frame and never writes to it. In TS this layer can be swapped (PixiJS, Phaser or raw WebGL behind one interface).

Platform

A browser link for playtests; Steam on desktop later. Networking sits behind a thin transport: WebRTC in the browser, Steam Networking on desktop, loopback for couch co-op and tests.

Your question: "TS + WebGL" doesn't have to mean a custom engine. In practice, reuse means a renderer library (PixiJS v8, or Phaser 4 used only to draw) plus its atlases, particles, filters, text and audio. Full JS engines (Phaser's scenes, Excalibur, KAPLAY, LittleJS) want to own the loop and the game objects, which fights a deterministic sim. Our skeleton already has a loop, a World, spells, statuses, a wave director, flow fields and gamepads. The part it's missing is a fast renderer.

My recommendation
PixiJS v8 renderer + our TypeScript sim + Electron for Steam later

It is the only option that keeps everything we've built and keeps your workflow of opening a link to try a build. It is also the one where I can build, test and screenshot every change myself. The real cost is porting the per-frame pixel VFX (beams, fire sheets) to GPU meshes and small shaders. The risks are Electron's size, and performance if enemy counts grow into the hundreds; to guard against that, profile early and keep hot state in typed arrays. Godot 4.7 is the strong second if a level editor and engine-grade VFX tools matter more to you than reuse.
