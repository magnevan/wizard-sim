# The art trial, round 1 (2026-11-06)

Stage 3 of `explorations/2026-11-06-campaign-and-content.md`, run early because the user installed PixelLab's MCP server and added a paid Gemini key. The question: which art source should make which assets for the campaign? The results are on the Academy Art Trial page (https://claude.ai/artifact/S2wttPmXSg7YXZhJQbL4gN), for the user to rate.

## The setup

Five academy props: a lectern, a bookshelf, an archery target, a rune circle on the floor, and a robed teacher. They are generic, with no lore in them.

Every source got the same brief where it could take it:
- **A style reference** cut from the game: real sprites and floor from a 1× screenshot (`tools/art-trial/prep.py`).
- **A palette:** 34 colours, mostly the ones our art code uses most (`tools/art-trial/palette-lock.json`).
- **The scale:** a wizard is about 30 art pixels tall.

Each set was then placed on the game's real floor beside a real wizard (`compose.py`), because that's the comparison that matters.

## Results

| Source | Spent | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Code-drawn (`code-props.html`, the game's pixel library) | About 180 lines | Closest to the game's chunky look. Plainer than the others. Variants are nearly free. |
| PixelLab object sheet (`create_1_direction_object`, 48 px, reference and palette) | 10 generations, 16 objects, 2 minutes | Real pixels, on palette, no cleanup. The richest detail. Leans isometric, adds stray bits, and is busier than our wizards. It invented 11 extra props in the same style. |
| PixelLab Pro (`create_object_pro_flash`, low top-down) | 5 generations for one prop | Tested on the bookshelf only. Straight-on, on palette, and a good match for our world. |
| PixelLab Pixflux (no reference) | 1 generation per prop | The look wanders (an anime teacher, a saturated circle). Not a fair style test, because it had no reference. |
| Gemini 3 Pro Image | 1 call for all five, 16 s | Best grasp of the angle and proportions. Not true pixel art: cleanup turns small detail to mud. |
| Gemini 3.1 Flash Image | 1 call for all five, 11 s | Stronger isometric lean. It kept floor tiles under the circle, and the books dissolved in cleanup. |

**Findings:**
- Palette locking works natively in PixelLab. For Gemini it needs our cleanup step (`cleanup.py`), which costs detail.
- The biggest open risk is **detail level**, more than quality: PixelLab's props are more detailed than our wizards, so a room of them could outshine the characters.
- The free PixelLab plan runs one job at a time, and 20 of its 40 trial generations are spent.
- Gemini's API returns token counts, not prices (1,120 image tokens per call), so the real cost is on the user's billing page. Two of the day's 20 allowed calls were used.

## Claude's recommendation

- **PixelLab Pro,** straight-on and palette-locked, for unique props and set pieces.
- **Code-drawn** for tiles, anything with variants, and everything driven by game state (spells, characters).
- **Gemini** for concept art and mood references, not sprites.
- **Next:** one mock academy room with the chosen sources together, judged in the running game.

Generated assets stay trial material for now. Before any ship, their terms need reading and their provenance needs recording in `meta.json`, as the content plan says.
