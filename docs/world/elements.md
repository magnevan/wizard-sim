# The elements: what each one *is*

A living page, unlike the dated explorations: edit it when the world or the spells change. It records the essence of each element, so the world (places, creatures, people, devices) and the spells can be designed from the same idea. The spell list may change over time; when a spell and its element's essence disagree, decide which one moves, and note it here.

Started 2026-11-07 from `../explorations/2026-11-07-world-and-progression.md`. The premise is the user's; the essences are Claude's proposals, agreed in outline and open to change. Lore details (names, history) stay the user's.

## The premise

Magic runs through the world as **fields** (broad, slow, like a climate) and **veins** (narrow, strong, like a river or an ore seam). People settle where fields help them, the way towns grow along rivers: smiths at a swell of fire, farms on water and life, mines on earth. Where a field is strong, the craft built on it advanced further, so some places have steam engines or rudimentary electricity while the rest of the world is medieval.

Seven elements are **fields**: Water, Fire, Cold, Lightning, Earth, Life, Arcane. Two are **innate**: Shield and Sigil belong to the caster, not the land.

**Casting is new (the user, 2026-11-09).** Until the veins began shifting, wizards worked magic only through structures that tap fields. The shift is why they can now draw on it themselves, and the academy's years over an Arcane vein are why these people can. "Innate" below still means "of the caster, not the land", but a better word is wanted now that all casting is new. See `../campaign/canon.md`.

**What this means in play:**
- Zones bend the *regen share* of field elements; they never touch Shield or Sigil, which work the same everywhere. Your innate magic is the baseline you carry into every region.
- Field elements deepen through *attunement* at places in the world; Shield and Sigil grow through *training*.
- Where two fields meet, the spell rules for that pair show up in the landscape (a Fire and Water confluence is a land of steam).

## Fields, veins and wells

- **A field** is broad and ambient, like rainfall or a climate. Its strength is what a zone's band measures (edge, heartland, well).
- **A vein** is a narrow channel of strength inside or between fields, like a river. It exists in the world, not as a gameplay effect: no boost for standing on one (the user, 2026-11-08). Devices and towns sit on them (a forge on a fire vein).
- **A well** is built by people where veins meet, to draw on them. A civilization can raise a great one, and there are smaller ones too. A region's push peaks at its great well. (Changed 2026-11-09: wells are constructed, not natural.)
- **Opposites can meet, depending on how they appear.** The rules cancel Fire and Cold, Water and Lightning, Life and Arcane, and Earth and Lightning, but in the world that's case by case. The user: "fire and frost is a bit weird to overlap. Water and lightning is not so crazy, lightning is not a persistent thing in the world. Rain and lightning is a common thing to happen together." A **hush** (a dead zone where neither holds) is a possible feature where fire meets frost, not a law. *(2026-11-08.)*

## The field elements

### Water: what flows and fills
- **In the world:** rivers, lakes, rain, tides. The most common field; nearly every town sits on some.
- **People:** farming (with Life), mills, ports, baths. Water-veined towns are old and populous.
- **Creatures:** amphibious, slow-wearing, hard to burn.
- **In play:** primarily a synergy element, with some knockback for crowd control. Soaks and douses; sets up freezes and conduction; Rain lingers and keeps puddles topped up. *(Its shove may move to Air, if Air comes in: `../ideas/air-element.md`.)*
- **Opposite:** Lightning.

### Fire: what consumes and transforms
- **In the world:** volcanic swells, hot springs, seams under old forges. Unstable: fire fields flare and fade.
- **People:** smiths, glassmakers, kilns. Where Fire meets Water, steam and the first engines; where it meets Lightning, arc foundries, the most industrial place in the world.
- **Creatures:** quick, aggressive, short-lived; drawn to burning.
- **In play:** the best overall damage, and that's its whole job. It's harder to synergize (it undoes Wet and Chill on purpose, so players can get in each other's way and must build their kit with care), its damage over time is harder to get full value from, and you risk setting yourself alight. Fire patches deny ground.
- **Opposite:** Cold.

### Cold: what stills and preserves
- **In the world:** mountains, the far north, deep caves. Cold fields are slow and patient; they hold what's in them.
- **People:** ice roads in winter, storehouses, monasteries that keep records. Cold keeps things, including the past.
- **Creatures:** large, slow, armoured; things that wait.
- **In play:** the biggest crowd control, for less damage (except freeze and shatter). Chill slows walking and attacks on a sliding scale; with Water it freezes; ice sheets and shatter.
- **Opposite:** Fire.

### Lightning: what leaps and connects
- **In the world:** storm coasts, high peaks, open steppe. It isn't persistent: it arrives in bursts and strikes the same places again, so it can share ground with Water (storms over a marsh). Where it meets Cold, spark peaks; where it meets Fire, arc foundries and wildfire steppe; where it meets Water, the storm marsh.
- **People:** the most "modern" towns: rods, coils, crude electric lamps and signals. Also the most dangerous to live in.
- **Creatures:** fast, twitchy, in swarms that chain.
- **In play:** good damage, a little chaotic in where it lands, with some stagger. Jumps between bodies and through water; stuns; fast and hard to aim around allies.
- **Opposite:** Water.

### Earth: what holds and endures
- **In the world:** mountains, plains of stone, ore seams. The steadiest field; it barely moves in a lifetime.
- **People:** miners, masons, fortress towns. Wealthy and conservative.
- **Creatures:** heavy, thick-skinned, territorial.
- **In play:** *stuff*, not energy: rocks and walls paid for once; mass and knockback; it carries other elements (Earth + Fire is a fireball).
- **Opposite:** none yet (Air would be, on T).

### Life: what grows
- **In the world:** the field living things make. Thick in old forests and good farmland, thin in deserts and blights. The only field that grows and shrinks with what lives on it, so it can be harmed and restored.
- **People:** farmers, healers, druid orders. Life land is the breadbasket, and fought over for it.
- **Creatures:** many, varied, regenerating; the wildest regions.
- **In play:** only heals; the other elements in a heal lend their resistances; Healing link and Mend.
- **Opposite:** Arcane.

### Arcane: the deep field
- **In the world:** under every other field, near the surface only where the crust of the others is torn: rifts, ruins, very old places. Rare, coveted, dangerous.
- **People:** scholars and wizards. Arcane draws out a caster's innate abilities, which is why **the academy sits on an Arcane vein**.
- **Creatures:** few, strange, out of place.
- **In play:** the raw, piercing beam; Attune (Arcane on yourself) floods every pool's regen, because the deep field feeds the others.
- **Opposite:** Life.

## The innate elements

Both cost the caster's own attention, which is why they behave unlike the field elements in the rules.

### Shield: the will to refuse
- **What it is:** a caster's capacity to hold and to not be moved. Not drawn from the land, so no zone weakens it.
- **Why it drains:** refusing takes **continuous concentration**. Barriers and wards drain the Shield pool while they stand (Model A), and they drain harder as they're hit: refusing a change costs more the harder the world pushes. When the concentration runs out, the oldest gives way. *(The user, 2026-11-08.)*
- **In play:** barriers, wards on allies, guards (Shield + Weapon), the parry.
- **Opposite:** Sigil.

### Sigil: the will to shape
- **What it is:** a caster's capacity to bind magic into a lasting form: a blade, a turret, a glyph. Only casters can do it, which is why weapons and devices need Sigil.
- **Why it binds a rune:** holding a shape takes **discrete attention**. Each device holds one Sigil rune until it goes off, breaks or is dismissed. Shield is concentration held continuously; Sigil is attention held in pieces.
- **In play:** weapons, turrets, glyphs, orbitals, the Lens, Blink, auras, the Double, the sculpting wand.
- **Opposite:** Shield.

## Undecided

- **Air** (`../ideas/air-element.md`): a tenth element would make five clean pairs (Earth's opposite) and is a candidate for the element unlocked late, through the story. As a field: high places, steady winds, maybe what lifts airships. Cost: a tenth key, about 81 % more spells, and changes to Water and the Shield wave.
- **Confluences** so far: Earth + Fire, the forge city; Fire + Water, steam towns; Fire + Lightning, arc foundries; Lightning + Cold, spark peaks; Cold + Water, ice roads; Life + Water, the breadbasket. More to come.
- **Veins have no direct gameplay effect** (settled 2026-11-08).
- **Do fields have a cost when tapped?** Can a vein be overdrawn and run dry? It's a story question (spine C in the exploration) as much as a world one.
