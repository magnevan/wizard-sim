# Verdict: the creator

| **Q** The Flitting Year | 9 | 9 | 9 | 6 | 8 | 41 |

| **P** Before All the People | 7 | 5 | 10 | 9 | 6 | 37 |

**Ranking: Q, S, P, R.**

## Q, The Flitting Year

**Would love:** it alone extends the creator's lore instead of plotting over it. Magic runs like climate, so the climate moves, on a published date, and children learn the order from a shoving game. Every land becomes two lands. The circuit needs no conspiracy: 211 years of cohorts trained so that hands would exist when the year came, and you drew the year. Four thousand people in folding chairs applauding the volcano going out is exactly the warm, dry note requested. A towed school, feeding sounders, and past and future Flits could fill years.

**Would wince:** attunement drifts toward logistics. "Mastering one aspect in each new land" competes with the job calendar, and the arrive, choose, front shape repeats. The Strait's fixed 212 dead strains "light touch". Above all, the ending is a tally: the player has been managing lamps, not sitting with people.

## S, Your Go

**Would love:** the closest match to the tone, taking the slight Pokémon fun and making it human without copying it. Bulls licking a fortress, twenty minutes of noon, glass bubbles caught in nets: each region is an open slot for another joke and slate, so it is easy to own. "Every time you lost, you kept her" is the best line in the four. Being thrown off the bridge by the game's last input is an ending only a spellcasting game could have.

**Would wince:** the wells barely matter. Attuning is a job on the way to the next spar, and this summer could be a tour of anywhere. The world's magic goes unexamined, so there is less to spend years on than the towns promise. It rests on four people loved through one line per supper.

## P, Before All the People

**Would love:** the circuit cannot be removed, because your word is the attunement's price and the road back is the plot. The ford's rule ("if nobody saw it burning, you never did it") and the herders' wedding are terrific. The Singing is the most powerful scene in any candidate, and the bowl on the last post earns its silence.

**Would wince:** it is a solemn game about debt. The fun of each new land sits under what you owe it, and the wedding shows how little else is light. The climax is a migration and a border, nearer political tragedy than classic fantasy. The creator inherits a machine: seven required oaths, tight scheduling and costly branches, with little slack for their own ideas.

## R, What He Had on Him

**Would love:** spells as forensics is a real discovery, worth stealing regardless. "Ten. That's right." is devastating.

**Would wince:** it is someone else's finished novel. Wells "only give depth" while locals teach the real tricks, so the circuit is an itinerary for seven pockets. Almost nothing can move without breaking a clue, the opposite of what a lore owner wants. The world exists to hold evidence, and the tone is noir, not warm.

## Improving the winner

Yes. Take **S's supper table** to fix Q's lowest score. End each leg with one long meal on the plotting floor, one line each from Bettany, Wenna, Hesper, Teague, Keziah and Pim, with places left laid for whoever the year has taken. After the Strait, Keziah's chair stays empty. The final count is then read at a table the player has sat at for forty hours, and the lamps become the last image of something already felt. It costs almost nothing to build.
